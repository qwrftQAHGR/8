# KANNAZUKI
### *The Month Without Gods* · setting pitch v0.1

> Working title. Alternatives: *Musubi*, *A Hundred Bells*, *The Hare's Errand*.

---

## The pitch in one breath

Every autumn, the gods of Japan leave their shrines and gather at Izumo to tie the coming year's bonds: who meets whom, who loves whom, what gets born. This year, someone cut the knot.

The gods are sealed inside Izumo. A year's worth of *musubi*, the power that both **ties** and **births**, burst across two worlds. The biggest piece of it ended up in you. Late one night you rang the bell of a shrine the size of a vending machine and asked for something, and the half-dead rabbit hiding behind the offering box made a desperate call.

Now dozens of people across two worlds are fated to fall for you, and every one you meet and fail to love will die of it. You've been remade: futa, insatiable, and so fertile that anything you conceive, or that's conceived in you, is born in a week. Everything that hunts in the dark can smell you. The god who's quietly taking over heaven's empty seat wants what's inside you.

And honestly? You're so pent up you can barely think.

---

## Tone

Romantic comedy up front, horror-action at the edges. The comedy comes from people: the harem, a household that doubles every few weeks, a rabbit with opinions. The dread comes from the world: things that ride on people's shoulders, gods who smile too much, the dead getting restless. Both happen in the same evening. A date gets interrupted by a mushi swarm, and a fight ends in a love hotel because that's how you heal people now.

Rule of thumb: **the stakes are never a joke, even when everything else is.** People can die, and some will.

## Two Japans

**Utsushiyo** (現世, "the present world") is our Japan: konbini, commuter trains, overtime, typhoon season. Normal people can't see what moves around them. The exceptions are shrine families, the dying, the very drunk, and children under seven ("until seven, they belong to the gods").

**Tokoyo** (常世, "the everlasting world") is a parallel modern Japan full of yokai, kemono, monster-folk and gods. It has the same islands, the same coastline and mostly the same map, but its history went another way.

The two worlds used to overlap at twilight. Then came the Meiji government's Shrine Consolidation Policy (1906–1914, real), which merged or demolished around seventy thousand local shrines in a decade. Every small shrine was a door. Gaslight and then electric light chased the dark out of every street, and the overlap tore apart. Tokoyo calls this **the Severance**. Since then it has modernised on its own track:

- **It kept the old names.** The megacity where Tokyo stands is still **Edo**. Kyoto is **Heian-kyō** and Osaka is **Naniwa**. Picture neon on tile roofs and expressways threading over shrine forests.
- **It kept its canals.** Tokyo paved over its rivers after the war, but Edo didn't, and kappa run the water-buses.
- **Its tech is a hybrid.** Some came over with crossers. Some came via *tsukumogami*: objects that wake up after a hundred years, or lately after being loved and thrown away. The rest is home-grown. Edo's power grid runs on **raijū** (thunder-beasts) in the substations, and the raijū union strikes about as often as you'd expect.
- **Phones:** an Utsushiyo phone gets no signal in Tokoyo except near crossing points ("one bar, and the bar's a funny shape"). Tokoyo has its own carriers.
- **Society.** Gods were the old aristocracy and the local governors. Below them:
  - fox houses: old money, Inari's messengers, currently leaderless;
  - tanuki: entertainment, retail, cons;
  - tengu: the police (the **Kebiishi**) and the mountain garrisons;
  - oni: construction, labour and the gangs;
  - kappa: water and transport;
  - jorōgumo: textiles;
  - tsukumogami: a huge and growing underclass.

  **Dejima**, Edo's foreign quarter, is home to lamia, harpies, slimes, minotaurs, succubi, and other monster-folk who came over from other countries' hidden worlds. It's the most vibrant district in Edo and the most heavily policed.
- **Bodies and gender.** Half of Tokoyo can change shape, so nobody fusses about presentation. A fox who's a boy on Tuesday and a girl on Thursday is unremarkable, and femboy foxes and hares are practically a fashion.
- **Humans** are rare in Tokoyo: the spirited-away, the married-in, exiles. They're called **utsushi**, and they're vulnerable, because some things still believe a human liver is medicine.

**Crossing over** happens at the seams:
- walking through a torii at twilight (*ōmagatoki*, the hour of meeting demons);
- riding the last train past the last stop until it pulls into a station that isn't on any map (Kisaragi Station is real, and it's busy);
- and sometimes, without meaning to, *kamikakushi*: being spirited away.

**One rule:** a normal human who eats Tokoyo food starts to forget home. Rescue them before dinner.

## What went wrong: the Kamiari Incident

In the tenth month every kami in Japan travels to Izumo. Everywhere else it's *Kannazuki*, "the month without gods"; in Izumo it's *Kamiarizuki*, "the month with gods". There they tie the next year's *en* (縁), the bonds of fate, meeting and marriage, into a single **Great Knot**. Once it's tied, the gods go home.

This year a reed boat came in on the tide alongside the gods' sea-snake escort, and something aboard it cut the Knot mid-binding.

- **The gods are trapped.** The burst Knot snarled into **the Tangle**. Izumo's great sacred rope grew like a strangling vine over the whole shrine precinct, and the gods are inside it. Nobody can get in, and nobody has come out.
- **Kannazuki didn't end.** In Utsushiyo nobody notices, except that shrines feel hollow, every omikuji comes up bad luck, and the evening news is full of accidents, disappearances and sudden cruelty. In Tokoyo everyone notices, because every region's governing god is gone.
- **The year's musubi scattered.** Most of it fell as loose ends, and some of it tied itself wrong. Its heart, the densest and rawest part, went into you.
- **One god was home.** Ebisu, god of fortune and commerce, is traditionally the *rusugami*, the caretaker who stays behind during Kannazuki (a real tradition). He has stepped in to keep things running, and Edo loves him for it.

## The shrine, the hare, and you

Your town has a shrine wedged between a coin laundry and a parking lot. **Shirousagi-sha** is a branch shrine of the White Hare of Inaba, with rabbit statues where the guardian dogs should be. It survived the Meiji mergers because the hare refused to leave.

On the night of the Incident the hare came home skinned, exactly as in the myth. He had grabbed the heart of the Knot as it burst and run for the nearest door, and hunters took his hide on the way. He was bleeding out behind the offering box when you rang the bell and prayed.

*(What you prayed for, why you were there, and who you were before are yours to write.)*

Musubi hears prayers. Inaba made a choice, and it went into you. The first thing it did was regrow the hare's skin under your hands, the way Ōkuninushi healed him two thousand years ago. Then the hare looked at you, the way he once looked at a young god on a beach, and told you who you'd marry.

He didn't mention there'd be this many.

## What the musubi did to you

*Musubi* (産霊 / 結び) is a pun the gods take seriously: the same word means *tying* and *the generative force that makes things be born*. You are carrying a year's supply of both.

**Your body.** You were remade to hold it, with both halves of creation in one vessel. Whatever you were before, you're now futa: fully equipped on both sides, immensely virile, immensely fertile, and driven. Contraception just… doesn't work.

**The week.** Any child conceived *of* you or *in* you is born seven days later.
- **Days 1–3:** hunger, heat and sleepiness, with nothing showing.
- **Days 4–5:** showing.
- **Day 6:** heavy.
- **Day 7:** the birth, which is fast and comparatively easy but still a birth.

For a partner with a day job, this is a serious logistics problem.

**Your chest.** Your breasts grow a little with every birth, because the vessel is filling.

**The Ubuya.** In the myth, Izanagi answered death by swearing to build fifteen hundred *ubuya*, birthing huts, every day. You have one: a pocket world you reach by opening any sliding door while thinking of home.
- It starts as a single hut thatched with cormorant feathers, as in the tale of Toyotama-hime, in a misty valley.
- It grows with every child, from homestead to hamlet to village.
- Inside, **growing things grow fast.** Children age about a year every two days, so a newborn is grown in roughly six weeks, and crops ripen in days. Adults don't age in there at all.
- Partners whose knots are closed can come and go with you. Nobody else can get in. Yet.

The Ubuya has a keeper. **Yae** is an *ubume*, the ghost of a woman who died in childbirth on a bridge in the 1700s, and she still carries a bundle that turned to stone three centuries ago. She turned up at the first birth and never left. She is midwife and nanny, and terrifying when crossed.

**Your children**, the *musubiko*, take after both parents (a tanuki partner's kids are tanuki with your eyes) plus a pinch of musubi. They're fiercely loyal to each other and to you. Once grown, they're adults with their own lives:
- some stay and run the Ubuya;
- some go out and fight;
- some head into Tokoyo, where a brand-new house with god-touched blood is political dynamite.

The bell never rings for blood kin.

## The Bell, and what it costs

There are no threads to see. You *hear* them.

When you meet a destined partner, both of you hear a shrine bell, the *suzu* you shake to wake a god, and nobody else does. Hearts jump and their scent goes vivid. You both know.

That's the knot half-tied, and **a half-tied knot is a noose.**

If it isn't closed, **koiwazurai** sets in: lovesickness, the classical poets' disease.
- **At first:** longing and insomnia.
- **Within a week:** fever and bad luck.
- **Then:** mushi come for them, drawn to a loose end like flies.
- **Within a month or so,** if nothing changes, they die: an accident, a fall, something in an alley.

**Love saves them; sex blesses them.** A knot closes when the love is real and mutual: said, meant and accepted. That stops the koiwazurai. The blessing comes from lying together.

The only other way out of a half-tied knot is to have it **cut**, and the people who can do that are not your friends. Someone who has been cut survives, but never hears the bell again, for anyone.

**The partner pool** spans both worlds and every species: women, femboys, and femme-leaning nonbinary folks. All of them are adults, because Izumo never ties marriage-en for the young. As for how many, Inaba's guess is "dozens, maybe more, and nobody should be counting."

**Finding them.** Fate does most of the work. En is the art of coincidence, and coincidences now bend around you. For the rest there's Inaba, whose ears pick up a partner's bell before it rings: a twitch, a turn of the head, *"East. Faint. Someone's about to have a very strange week."*

## Riyaku: the blessing

*Go-riyaku* is what every shrine promises, the practical benefit a god grants. Yours is real.

- **The first time** a partner lies with you is the big one, the **First Knot**.
  - They gain the Sight (they can see mushi and the unseen) if they didn't have it.
  - They jump a level in whatever they already are. A kitsune grows a tail. A human with a drop of something old in them wakes up. A plain human gets a small spark of their own.
  - They get a gut-sense when you're in danger.
- **After that, returns diminish.** Repeat intimacy still heals wounds, restores stamina and leaves an **afterglow**, a few hours of heightened ability. But the permanent gains shrink to a trickle. *The first cup is sake; the rest is water.*
- **Except when it means something.** A real emotional milestone (a confession that costs something, a sacrifice, a promise kept) tightens the knot and brings back a genuine surge. The system rewards depth, not repetition.
- **Births** are a second major blessing for whoever carries the child.

This is the action backbone. Your partners are your strength, and every new knot is a power spike. Retreating to a love hotel to heal mid-arc is a legitimate tactic.

## What you can do yourself

You're not a fighter at first. You're a vessel that everything wants. Still, musubi gives you a small kit that grows with every closed knot.

- **Tying.** With a physical cord in your hands, you can tie knots that *hold*. Any cord will do: string, rope, a hair-tie, the strip of sacred straw rope Inaba gave you. Bind a mushi, seal a door, tie a wound shut so it heals, tie an oath. Tying a real knot takes real seconds, and that's the tension in a fight.
- **Untying.** You can undo stray knots, the year's misfired bonds. That's a steady source of trouble and side jobs.
- **Mending and growing.** Your touch speeds healing, and things around you want to grow.
- **The door.** You can enter the Ubuya through any sliding door. You can come out again *near any partner whose knot is closed*, in either world. The corridor is long and it takes something out of you, but it's how you show up in time.
- **Endgame.** You're carrying the heart of the Great Knot. You may be the only thing that can untie the Tangle and re-tie the year, and every knot you close is practice.

## Why you can't just stay home

**First, you want to.** Musubi runs hot. You're randy all the time, and you're not built to sit on it.

**Second, the Swell.** Unspent musubi festers. Go too long without spending it on love, sex or births and it rots, in stages:
- fever and restlessness;
- then the world around you overgrows wrong: the houseplant splits its pot and blackens at the leaf-edges, milk sours overnight, mould blooms on the walls;
- then mushi gather at your window.

Hold out long enough and the vessel of creation becomes a vessel of calamity. The first gods of calamity were born from the filth a god washed off himself, and you could be next.

**Third, everyone you've already met.** Half-tied knots don't wait.

**Fourth, hiding doesn't work.** You smell like a festival to everything that hunts. The only safety is strength, and strength comes from knots.

## The threats

### Mushi, Magatsu, Tatari: the things on people's shoulders
When Izanagi washed off the filth of the underworld, gods of calamity were born from it: the Magatsuhi. Their spawn is everywhere. The gods' purifications kept it in check, and with the gods gone it's breeding.

- **Mushi** (虫). Japanese already has the idioms: "the bug in my belly won't settle", "a bug's warning". Here they're literal. Mushi are small, eyeless, many-legged things that ride on shoulders, crawl into ears and whisper. They feed on kegare (resentment, grief, rot, cruelty) and breed more of it. Normal people can't see them. They just feel cold, irritable, hopeless or mean.
- **Magatsu.** When enough mushi gather, they congeal into something with mass: too many mouths, a stink, an appetite. Typical nests are a black-company office, an accident-blackspot bridge, a love hotel where something went very wrong. A Magatsu can drag a person off a platform.
- **Tatari.** A Magatsu that has eaten enough of one person's grudge to take on their shape and name. A curse with a personality. These are the boss fights.

Salt, sake, fire, sacred rope, a blessed partner's claws, and your knots all work. Killing one isn't the end, though. The kegare it leaves behind has to be cleaned up, or it seeds the next one. In Tokoyo most people *can* see mushi, so they're a public-health problem. With the gods gone, cleaning crews work on private contract: rich districts stay clean and poor ones rot.

### The Ashiya Group (Utsushiyo)
On paper: real estate, private security, and very discreet "spiritual consulting" for politicians and executives. In fact: descendants of Ashiya Dōman, the folk sorcerer who was Abe no Seimei's rival.
- Their exorcists carry ofuda and salt-loaded pistols.
- They do kill mushi, for paying clients.
- They also bind yokai as *shikigami* under contracts that aren't much better than ownership.

Musubi on tap is the most valuable thing they've ever heard of, and they know roughly where to find it.

### The Cutters: Hashihime of Uji
The Bridge Princess became an oni out of jealousy a thousand years ago, and she's still why wedding parties avoid Uji Bridge. Her *enkiri* cult cuts bonds. To be fair, plenty of people go to her to escape a marriage, a master, or a stray knot that's strangling them. She is the only way out of a half-tied knot.

To her, one person bound to dozens is the worst chain she has ever seen. Cutting a closed knot doesn't kill anyone. It leaves someone who remembers loving you and can no longer feel it.

### The Caretaker: Ebisu
Fat, laughing, a fish under one arm, his face on every banknote in Edo. Ebisu stayed home this Kannazuki as he always does. Now that every other god is gone, *Ebisu & Co.* runs the wards, the cleaning crews, the Kebiishi payroll and the trains. His festival, the Ebisu-kō, has always fallen in Kannazuki, and this year it looks a lot like a coronation.

The oldest stories say Ebisu is someone else wearing a lucky face. **Hiruko** was the first child Izanagi and Izanami ever had: born boneless, judged wrong, and set adrift in a reed boat. He washed ashore, put on a smile, and has kept it on for two thousand years. He's the one god who wasn't at Izumo, and it was a reed boat that came in on the tide.

He wants the musubi, and specifically what you have: a parent who keeps every child. So he's going to be kind to you. He'll open doors, solve problems and send gifts. What he wants is to be born again, *properly*, through you. Nobody knows what a primordial god born from a whole year's musubi would do to the world that threw him away, possibly not even him.

### Yomi: the slow one
At the slope of Yomotsu Hirasaka, a boulder seals off the land of the dead. Izanami stands on the far side, keeping her vow to kill a thousand people a day, while the living answer with fifteen hundred births. The gods used to watch that boulder, and nobody's watching it now.

Late in the story the *yomigaeri* start coming home. (The word for resurrection is literally "returning from Yomi".) They wear the faces of people's dead. And there's no bigger beacon in either world than a body that gives birth in a week.

## Knock-on effects

- **Stray knots.** The musubi that didn't hit you is tying wrong:
  - strangers who can't stop thinking about each other;
  - a yakuza and the detective tailing him;
  - a salaryman devoted to a vending machine, which is unhelpfully waking up.

  The results are stalkers, feuds and comedy. You can untie these knots and Hashihime can cut them, so you're both busy.
- **The year-end purification won't happen.** The Ōharae, the Great Purification, is held twice a year to wash away six months of kegare, and the next one is on New Year's Eve. Without gods it can't be done properly. The mushi problem worsens every week until 31 December, and that night the kegare of a godless half-year will have nowhere to go.
- **Gaps in the sky.** Over Tokoyo, whole constellations, the stars of the absent gods, have gone dark.
- **Leaderless houses.** Inari is trapped, so the fox houses have no head and are tearing into each other. Other regions are run by whichever local boss or temple guardian stepped up. Buddhist powers never went to Izumo: temple guardians, and the tengu of the old mountains. They are now the only check on Ebisu.
- **More crossings, both ways.** Spirited-away humans are turning up in Tokoyo far more often, and there's a black market for them. Mushi and worse are slipping into Utsushiyo through every crack.
- **Partners' lives blow up.** A woman suddenly sees things on her coworkers' shoulders. A grandmother goes quiet when someone asks about the family's snow-pale skin. A seven-day pregnancy has to be explained to a boss. Humans with a **drop** of something old (fox, yuki-onna, dragon) are more common than anyone thinks, and the First Knot wakes it up.
- **Your household becomes a clan.** Children grow up in weeks, and within a few months you head a house: the first new house in Tokoyo in a century, with god-blood in it. Old houses with dwindling lines will want marriages, alliances, or you dead. None of this was anyone's plan. It's fallout from a theological accident.
- **Every faction wants something different from you.** Ashiya wants a resource. Hashihime wants you cut loose. Ebisu wants a parent. Yomi wants the opposite of everything you are. If the gods ever get out, they'll want their Knot back.

These are sketches to show the range, not a checklist. The bell decides.

- **Asahina Mizore** *(Utsushiyo; human, with an unknown drop of yuki-onna).* 24, works the konbini night shift. Deadpan, always cold, never sweats, and her grandmother knows exactly why. The First Knot brings the frost out.
- **Mamemaru** *(tanuki living in Utsushiyo; femboy).* A broke, chaotic VTuber whose "model" is just his own face, ears and tail with no filter. A shapeshifter. Once blessed, his transformations gain real mass.
- **Ashiya Tamaki** *(Utsushiyo; human of a known lineage, with a fox ancestor the family doesn't mention).* The Ashiya Group's heiress, sent to watch you, and then the bell rang. - **Shiori** *(Tokoyo; okuri-inu, full anthro wolf-dog).* A night courier on Tokoyo's mountain roads. Okuri-inu follow travellers in the dark: if you trip, they eat you, and if you don't, they see you home. She is very protective, and it takes real effort for her not to make you trip.
- **Tsumugi** *(Tokoyo; jorōgumo).* A spider-bodied tailor with a shop in Ginza's mirror. Elegant, predatory, lonely. Her silk holds a knot like nothing else, so she makes your cords.

## Inaba

In the myth, the White Hare of Inaba tricked a line of *wani* into forming a bridge across the sea and was skinned for it. He took bad advice from eighty petty gods and was healed by the one kind god who came last: Ōkuninushi, lord of en and of the hidden world. Then he foretold that god's marriage, and he has been Ōkuninushi's messenger ever since.

He looks like a slight young man of about twenty, with white hair, red eyes and long ears that betray every feeling he tries to hide. He wears an oversized hoodie over hakama. In Utsushiyo, people without the Sight see a white rabbit. He's sharp-tongued and fast, a coward in a fight and brave where it counts. He knows everybody in Tokoyo and owes half of them money.

He hears bells, and he knows how this is supposed to work. He's also carrying something: about the reed boat, about how the hunters knew where to find him, and about who asked him a favour before the gathering. That's for later.

## Shape of the story

This is a sandbox with a clock: no rails, just pressure that rises month by month.

1. **Kannazuki (now).** Awakening and the first bells. The first crossing, and learning there are two Japans. Mushi. The Ebisu-kō in Edo, where the Caretaker takes the stage.
2. **Shimotsuki (the frost month).** Ashiya moves and the Cutters strike. The Ubuya fills up. The Tangle starts spreading out from Izumo.
3. **Shiwasu (December).** The purification that won't happen, and New Year's Eve.
4. **After.** The boulder, the returning dead, the road to Izumo, and what Hiruko actually wants.

## How it plays at the table

- Bonds are **heard and felt**, never seen: the bell, a scent, a tug in the gut.
- Inaba's **ears** are the compass.
- Mushi are visible only to the Sighted, so a scene can show a coworker with something on her shoulder that only you notice.
- Blessings show in **what people can do**, not in how they look. No marks, no glow, no brands.
- The Swell shows in **the surroundings**: plants, mould, milk, bugs at the window.
- Pregnancies last **one week** and show from day 4. Births happen in the Ubuya when possible.
- Nobody keeps a list. Not you, not Inaba, not the narrator.

## Steering clear of

- **Ledgers and registries.** No book of fates, no tally, no roster of who's next. The only gods who might keep records are locked in a tangle at Izumo.
- **Visible threads and glowing marks.** Sound, scent and instinct do that job.
- **A birth-rate allegory.** The fertility is a side effect of a theological accident. The central problem is a coup and a sealed heaven, not a population chart. Tokoyo cares about your children for reasons of power and bloodline, not demographics.

## Open questions before the deep build

1. **The MC.** I left their life before the shrine, their prayer and their reason for being there blank. Before the deep build I need at least their prior body and gender and roughly who they are, so the transformation and opening land right.
2. **Ebisu / Hiruko** as the smiling centre of the conspiracy: keep, or would you rather a different main antagonist?
3. **Partner count.** Open-ended ("dozens, nobody's counting"), or a hard 100 as homage? I lean open-ended, because a fixed number invites a tally.
4. **Inaba:** guide only, a slow burn, or has his bell already rung and he's hiding the koiwazurai?
5. **Dials:**
   - Is about six weeks from newborn to adult in the Ubuya right?
   - Does your chest grow only from births you carry, or from every child of yours?
   - Lactation on or off? (If on, it's restorative: a handy way to heal the party.)
   - Can the MC ever *choose* not to conceive, or is it always on?
6. **Darkness ceiling.** Can a named partner die on-screen if the MC fails them?
7. **Home base.** Where does the MC live? My default is a neighbourhood on the Chūō Line in western Tokyo: city on one side, Okutama's mountains on the other.
