# GO-EN — Series Pitch (draft 1)

> **ご縁 / 五円.** *Go-en*, "a fated bond," is pronounced exactly like *go-en*, "five yen." People drop five-yen coins into shrine boxes when they pray for love. That's how this starts.

**Tags:** Japan · present day · parallel modern Japan · yokai · monster folk / MGE / furry · femboys & femme enbies · action · romance · unseen threats in both worlds · hidden fantastic bloodlines

---

## The short version

Every autumn, the gods of Japan gather at Izumo to tie the coming year's bonds. Last autumn, something was waiting for them on the beach with shears.

A year on, the world is quietly coming apart at the seams. Accidents come in clusters. Families forget their own members overnight. Gods go feral. In the other Japan, the one most people don't know exists, dens sell a drug made of other people's love. Into all this walks MC, who drops a five-yen coin into the box of a forgotten roadside shrine and makes a small, honest wish. The god inside is dying and has nothing left to give except everything, so it gives MC everything.

Now MC carries the largest surviving piece of the force that ties the world together, and their body has been rewritten to hold it: futa, ferociously virile, ferociously fertile. Their destined partners are scattered across two Japans, and each one dies if MC meets them and doesn't love them back. MC also has a sarcastic white hare for a guide, a phantom house full of children who grow up in weeks, and a growing list of people who want them cut, caged, bottled, or worshipped.

It's a harem rom-com stitched onto a monster-hunting thriller. The romance gets to be silly. The monsters never are.

## 1. The core idea: *musubi*

The whole premise hangs off one real Shinto word.

**Musubi** means *to tie* (結び: a knot, a bond, the red string of fate). It is also the old word for *the power of becoming* (産霊), the generative force the first gods were named after and the reason anything is born or grows. In GO-EN those are a single force, and MC is full of it.

| The premise says… | …because of musubi |
|---|---|
| MC has destined partners | Musubi ties things. MC is holding the loose ends, and the loose ends are people. |
| Meeting one and not loving them is lethal | A half-tied thread is exposed, and something eats exposed threads (§5). |
| Futa, immensely virile and fertile | Musubi *makes* things. Raw, inside a human body, it comes out the most direct way a body has of making anything. |
| One-week pregnancies | Becoming, at full pressure. |
| A pocket dimension where kids grow fast | The god's own house, where the musubi pools (§7). |
| Sex gives blessings | Musubi makes things *more*. A partner who takes it in becomes more of what they already are (§6). |
| MC's breasts grow with every birth | The vessel stretching to hold what it carries. |

**The fertility is fallout, not a mission.** Nobody planned this. No god appointed MC to fix anything. The force in MC was meant to tie the world's bonds; trapped in one body, it does what it does, which is make things, through the only channels a body has. The world's crisis is about bonds being *cut*. The babies are what happens when that much becoming has to go somewhere.

## 2. Two Japans: Omote and Ura

Think of the world as one bolt of cloth with two faces. **Omote** (表, "the front") is our Japan. **Ura** (裏, "the back") is the other one: the same islands, mountains, and coastlines, with a different people and a different history. Bonds are threads that pass through both faces, which is why MC's destined are scattered across both.

### Omote, the front
- Japan as it is now: phones, konbini, last trains, unpaid overtime.
- Most people can't see yokai or phantoms. When something supernatural *can* be seen, ordinary people get **the Glaze**: they explain it away without noticing they're doing it. Fox ears are a headband, a tail is cosplay, and a fight against something invisible looks like two drunks scuffling. A seven-meter phantom flattens a car and everyone, witnesses included, believes the news when it says gas explosion. The Glaze has limits, though. A lamia's tail on a rush-hour train is too much to explain away, so big-bodied folk stay in the Ura or travel disguised.
- A few people know: shrine families (only some with real power), freelance exorcists and temple monks, yokai living in disguise (foxes and tanuki pass most easily), and some much less friendly groups (§10).
- **Hidden blood.** Over the centuries a lot of yokai married into human families; the fox-wife of legend wasn't the only one. Most of their descendants have no idea. Some old families know, and they either hide it or hate it. The ancestry shows up as the Sight, as uncanny luck, or as nothing at all until something wakes it (§6).

### Ura, the back
- A parallel *modern* Japan whose people are yokai, beast-folk, and monster folk. Skyscrapers with tiled roofs, vending machines, idols, packed commuter trains: the same rhythms as Omote, very different bodies.
- **It kept the old names.** The capital is **Edo**, the merchant city is **Naniwa**, and the old court still sits at **Heian-kyō**. Its history forked a long way back. The Ura modernized without ever stopping being old.
- **Old things wake up.** Anything more than a century old can become a *tsukumogami*. The oldest train lines have opinions, heritage buildings sulk, and a hundred-year-old phone booth will tell you who called whom. The tech is modern. The antiques are alive.
- **Everyone lives here.** Kitsune, tanuki, oni, tengu, kappa, nekomata, jorōgumo, yuki-onna, ningyo, dragonfolk, anthro beast-folk of every kind. The Ura is global too: every country has a back side, so there are immigrants and tourists from abroad. Lamias teach English, a minotaur runs a ramen shop, harpies run courier services, and a dragon property investor owns half of Naniwa.
- **It's frank.** The Ura is far looser than Omote about bodies and desire: mixed baths, cross-species romance as ordinary as any other kind, heat seasons as a fact of life. A futa human who smells like a god is a sensation, not a scandal. The Night Parade (*Hyakki Yagyō*) is a monthly street festival.
- **Five-yen coins are luck money.** A coin offered with a sincere prayer carries a sliver of real bond, so Omote's five-yen coins trade like charms in the Ura. MC's pocket change is suddenly worth something.
- **Humans are rare, almost legendary.** Most are people who were spirited away, fell through, and stayed. The Ura's feelings about Omote are tangled: it loves Omote's pop culture and resents its poachers, its exorcists, and how easily it forgets its gods.
- **Who holds power:**
  - the **Bakufu** in Edo, the modern government: pragmatic and overwhelmed;
  - the **Fox Court** at Heian-kyō, the old aristocracy, which used to handle dealings with the gods and is now scrambling;
  - the **tengu** houses, who hold the sky;
  - the **oni** clans, who build everything and run half the underworld;
  - the **tanuki** merchant houses, who sell everything else;
  - and a rising movement that wants the door between the worlds shut for good (§10).

### Getting across
- **Ōmagatoki.** Dusk is "the hour of meeting demons," and some thresholds open then: old torii, shrine paths, wells, tunnels. You have to know how, or be unlucky.
- **Kisaragi Station.** The station from the urban legend. The last train sometimes stops there, and it works as the unofficial border crossing. The tanuki stationmaster has seen everything and charges accordingly.
- **The House's back door** (§7). MC's private way across.
- **Seam tears.** Accidental, dangerous, and more common every month (§10).

MC's first crossing should be a set piece. The back door is the gentle way in; Kisaragi is the dramatic one.

## 3. The inciting night

**The Izumo Night.** In the tenth month, the gods of Japan come ashore at Inasa Beach and gather at Izumo to drink, argue, and tie the coming year's bonds into one great cord. That cord, the **Great Knot**, also holds Omote and Ura together. Last year there were blades waiting in the surf. The Great Knot was cut to pieces, gods were cut in half, and the Great Lord of Izumo hasn't been seen since. In Omote, the news reported a freak storm, some downed trees, and a brush fire. In the Ura, Izumo-of-the-Back is a cordoned-off ruin.

**The Nameless God.** One minor god of musubi escaped with a piece of the cord. It crawled home to its roadside shrine, a weathered stone box at the edge of somewhere (where, exactly, is your call). It had been cut so badly that nobody remembers its name anymore, the hare included. Then it waited a year for someone to pray.

**Five yen.** MC dropped a five-yen coin in the box and made a small, honest wish. What they prayed for is yours to decide, and I want to use it: the god answered *that wish* with all of itself, and the story should keep echoing it. The god poured its musubi and its piece of the Great Knot into MC, then went out like a candle. MC got its house too.

*(Not all of the Nameless God died. See "Aragami" in §10.)*

## 4. What MC is now

*MC's history, personality, and starting circumstances are yours. This section covers what the setting does to them.*

- **The body.** Futa. Ferociously virile and fertile in both directions: a pregnancy lasts one week whether MC is the one carrying or not (§7). Their breasts grow with every birth, a visible measure of how far they've come. The musubi takes the weight, so there's no wrecked back, just a wardrobe in permanent crisis.
- **The Swell.** Musubi is the force of becoming, and inside a body it turns into appetite. MC's libido isn't a personality quirk. It's pressure. Left unspent, it builds into restlessness, then heat, then fever, and MC starts giving off a scent like warm rain and incense. Partners find it intoxicating; predators find it delicious. Using the musubi in a fight stokes it too, so MC comes out of every battle starving.
- **The Sight.** MC sees frays, phantoms, and disguised yokai. They don't see threads. Nobody does.
- **Tying.** MC's one real power, and it grows. At first MC can tie off a small fray with a touch; it shrieks and goes still. Later MC can bind a wound shut, re-tie someone who's been Cut back into their family's memory, stitch a seam tear closed, and eventually wrestle a wild god back to its people. Each knot MC ties makes the musubi stronger and steadier. That's MC's progression, and it's measured in what MC can mend, never in numbers.
- **What MC's life is holding up.** If MC dies, every knot snaps at once, and every partner becomes a loose strand on the same night. The partners know this. They are unbearably protective.

## 5. The destined: the Chime and the knot

### The Chime
When MC meets one of their destined, both of them hear a shrine bell that isn't there. It's a single clear note, and only the two of them hear it. There's no lightning, no mark, no thread in the air.

- **There's no list.** Nobody knows how many there are, who they are, or where. Fate does the introductions, and fate is pushy: the destined turn up in MC's path at airports, crime scenes, and in the middle of fights. Inaba's estimate of the total changes weekly. (*"Somewhere between 'a lot' and 'oh no.'"*)
- They come from both sides: human women, femboys, femme enbies, yokai, beast-folk, monster girls, and the occasional thing nobody has a word for. Every one of them is an adult, and reads as one.

### The half-knot, or why it's lethal
The Chime opens a partner's eyes. From that moment they have the Sight: they can see the frays, and the frays can see *them*. Every destined partner is a loose strand of the Great Knot, and loose strands are food.

- **Half-tied.** After the Chime, the partner is drawn toward MC but not yet tied off. While MC is open to them, MC's attention shields them.
- **Fraying.** If MC turns them away (rejects them, ghosts them, refuses them), the half-knot frays. Leaving it hanging too long frays it too, just more slowly. The world starts going wrong around the partner: a run of bad luck first, then near misses, and MC, who has the Sight, watches frays gather on them, a few more each day. Within days an "accident" finishes it. Everyone else calls it a tragedy. MC knows it was a meal.
- **Love ties the knot, not sex.** The knot closes when both of them mean it: a confession accepted, a kiss that counts, "I'm yours" said out loud and true. The bell rings a second time, warmly. Sex is a separate thing with its own effects (§6). Keeping the two apart leaves room for slow burns and stops the knot from becoming a sex-or-die loop.
- **Tied partners can't be frayed.** Phantoms slide off them like rain off oiled paper, and ordinary cutters can't get a grip on the knot. They can still be shot, clawed, and broken. The knot protects them from predators, not from violence. Only one thing can cut a tied knot: the shears from the Izumo Night (§10).
- **The knot holds as long as the love does.** It survives arguments, distance, jealousy, and bad weeks. It doesn't survive real abandonment. The musubi doesn't make MC love anyone. It just makes room.

### Not meeting them isn't safe either
Since the Izumo Night, the cord's loose ends have been hunted. The Kamikiri track them, and the frays find them eventually anyway. Some of MC's destined were dead before MC ever reached the shrine. Staying home doesn't protect anyone. It just means somebody else gets to them first.

## 6. The blessing

Sleeping with MC blesses a partner. The first time is big; after that, the returns diminish.

### The Bloom (first night with a tied partner)
*A destined partner who sleeps with MC before the knot is tied only gets a Spark (below). The Bloom waits for the knot.*

The partner becomes *more of what they are.*
- A kitsune grows a new tail overnight, normally a century's work.
- A human with buried ancestry wakes up with it: ears, scales, cold breath, a voice the water obeys. Sometimes they had no idea.
- An ordinary human gets a **knack**, a small supernatural gift that grows out of who they already are. A nurse's hands start knitting wounds closed, a sprinter can outrun a car, a liar's lies briefly come true.
- An oni's strength stops being a figure of speech, a yuki-onna's chill becomes weather, and a tengu can outfly a bullet train.

**"More themselves" includes the flaws.** The new tail comes with new appetites. The oni's temper grows with the strength. The yuki-onna can't hug her human friends anymore. Every Bloom is a character beat as well as a power-up.

### Deepening
Later nights still give something, but less each time: stamina, faster healing, sharper senses, luck in a crisis, a finer edge on the Bloom's gift. Eventually it levels off into upkeep, like the tug in a partner's chest when MC is in real danger. There's nothing to track; the story notices.

### Second Blooms
Some story moments open the tap again:
- **A child together.** *Ko wa kasugai*, the proverb goes: a child is the clamp that holds two people together. Here that's literal. The parent of MC's child is anchored (even the Izumo shears struggle with that knot) and often blooms again, in a direction shaped by the child.
- **Sacred nights.** A festival night, a holy site, Kannazuki itself.
- **Surviving something together.** The first night after both of them nearly died. The musubi answers need.

### The Spark (everyone else)
Lovers who aren't destined get a **Spark**: a short run of luck and vitality, a good week. No Bloom, no Sight. That's still enough to make MC wanted everywhere, which means Ura bar crawls, honey traps, "musubi tourists," and some very awkward exes.

## 7. The House and the children

### The Mayoiga
The Nameless God left MC its home, a **Mayoiga**: the lost house of Tōno folklore, a farmhouse in the mountains that appears only to travelers who've lost their way. Red and white flowers by the gate, rice already steaming, bowls set out for guests, and nobody in sight.

- **Get lost on purpose.** MC walks without watching where they're going and turns down streets they don't know, and after a few minutes the gate is just there. The House won't appear to hostile eyes, so this doesn't work with an enemy on MC's heels. Anyone holding MC's hand can come in. Tied partners can find it alone after their first visit.
- **It's the hinge between the worlds.** The front door opens onto the Omote street you came in from, and the back door onto the same spot in the Ura. It's a crossing, not a teleporter.
- **It grows.** Every tied partner gets a new room, shaped like them. The nursery keeps expanding. The bath is turning into an onsen. Courtyards appear, then wings, then eventually something more like a village.
- **It tells you, in its own way.** There are no meters and no numbers. When a partner's knot is strained, their room goes cold, a door sticks, the flowers under their window wilt. The House gives you a mood, not a dashboard.
- **The staff are tsukumogami**: a lantern majordomo, an umbrella that minds the door, a tanuki teakettle who runs the kitchen, a pair of geta that run errands.
- **The Ubume.** In folklore the ubume is the ghost of a woman who died giving birth; she stands by a river at night and asks strangers to hold her baby. This one is the House's midwife: stern, tender, very old. *"No one dies giving birth in my house. Not again."* The folklore also says that whoever holds her baby until dawn is granted great strength. She doesn't offer that lightly.

### Pregnancy
- **A week, either direction.** Anyone MC gets pregnant carries for a week, and so does MC when they're the one pregnant. The first day feels like nothing, by the fourth it's obvious, and on the seventh it's time. Births happen at the House, because the Ubume insists.
- **The cost is hunger.** A one-week pregnancy eats like a whole season. The House's kitchen never runs out, which is just as well.
- **Contraception doesn't work on musubi.** Only one thing does: a bitter tea Inaba brews from the hare's herbs. Skipping it is a choice. Forgetting it is a plot.
- Human partners need a week off and a cover story. Ura partners mostly get congratulations.

### The children
- **A week to be born, about three weeks to grow up.** Inside the House, children grow roughly a year per day: walking by the end of the first day, school-age within a week, adult in three. Their minds keep pace. The House teaches them, and they learn as if they were remembering.
- **Outside the House, they grow at a normal rate.** A parent can take a baby out into the world to keep them little longer, but outside, a musubi-born child is a beacon to every hungry thing in two worlds. That choice is real, and it should hurt.
- **Heritage shows.** Kits, horns, scales, wings, or a perfectly human-looking kid with their grandmother's fox eyes. Sometimes a child reveals a partner's hidden ancestry before any Bloom does.
- **Each child carries a spark of musubi.** They see frays from birth and can tie small things: close a cut, mend a cup, pin a fray. They're natural hunters.
- **They grow into a clan.** Within months MC's household is a power in its own right, with grown children going out into both worlds, holding the House, joining the fight, and becoming targets. The bible will cover how they find their feet. The RP can follow a few of them closely and keep the rest in the background.
- **Kin are kin.** The Chime never rings for blood, because a knot can't be tied with its own end. MC's children are family, full stop.

## 8. Inaba, the guide

**The White Hare of Inaba**, from the oldest myths. The hare tricked the sea-*wani* into lining up as a bridge, got skinned for bragging about it, and was healed by the young god who would become Lord of Izumo. Then it prophesied whom he would marry, which makes it the first matchmaker in the old books. Inaba has been the gods' go-between ever since.

- **On the Izumo Night, Inaba ran.** Hares run. They've spent the year since living with it and following the Nameless God's trail, and they reached the shrine thirty seconds too late. *"You prayed to a dying god. With five yen."*
- **Two shapes.** One is a white hare small enough to ride in MC's hood. The other is a person: femme, nonbinary, white hair, red eyes, long ears stuffed under a beanie in Omote. Vain about the ears. Hates being touched on the back. Won't go near the sea.
- **On honesty:** *"I lied once and got skinned for it. I don't lie about important things anymore. Everything else is fair game."*
- **What Inaba brings.**
  - They know the rules better than anyone alive, and they freely admit nobody has ever seen raw musubi in a human body before.
  - They know the Ura well and are a minor celebrity there; the hare's face is on charm merch.
  - Their ears sometimes twitch near an unmet strand. That's a hunch, never a map.
  - They coach MC's romances, sometimes even well.
  - They carry fresh water and cattail pollen, the cure from the myth, which heals flesh and briefly steadies a fraying bond.
  - They fight by running away and kicking very hard.
- **Inaba is not one of the destined.** They say so often. *(Optional hook: they're lying. It would be the one important lie.)*

## 9. The real problem: the world is coming unstitched

Since the Izumo Night, bonds have been cut faster than they can grow back. Every cut thread leaves a frayed end, and frayed ends are hungry.

- **In Omote:** accidents in clusters. People forgotten overnight, leaving an empty room or a gap in a family photo that nobody notices. A self-help craze about "cutting toxic ties" that works a little too well. Shrines going dark, or going dangerous. Spiriting-away cases at a rate the police have quietly stopped publicizing.
- **In the Ura:** gods gone feral. Seam tears in the middle of cities. Dens selling a drug made of other people's love. A separatist movement winning seats by promising to shut the door on humanity forever. Most yokai can't see frays either; the Ura is as blind to what's eating it as Omote is.

Each side blames the other. Under both, someone is finishing what the Izumo Night started and cutting the two faces of the world apart for good. And the largest piece of the Great Knot left in either world is inside MC.

**The clock.** Kannazuki comes every year. If the gods can't gather, or they gather and the blades are waiting again, the cut becomes permanent. Nobody knows what a world with only one face looks like. Inaba's guess: *"Cloth with one side isn't cloth. It's lint."*

## 10. Threats

Almost all of these are invisible to ordinary people on both sides.

### The phantom ecology
- **Frays** (*hotsure*). The common phantom: the loose ends of cut bonds, clots of hair and thread with too many mouths. They ride on shoulders, cling to ceilings, and crowd into train cars. They feed on bonds, and the people they feed on turn forgetful and spiteful and keep having near misses. One fray is a nuisance. A nest means a death toll.
- **The Cut** (*kirare*). People whose bonds have all been cut. First everyone forgets them, then they forget themselves, then they come apart into frays. If a single thread is left, they can be re-tied: think of a mother who still sets an extra place at dinner without knowing why. Saving one of the Cut is among the best and worst scenes this setting can do.
- **Patchworks** (*tsugihagi*). Big phantoms stitched together from hundreds of stolen bonds. They wear stolen faces, speak in the voices of people you love, nest in empty apartment blocks and closed hospitals, and set traps.
- **Aragami.** The Izumo shears cut gods in half, separating the gentle soul (*nigimitama*) from the rough soul (*aramitama*). The gentle halves fade. The rough halves go wild. A river god without its gentleness is a flood with a grudge, and a mountain god without it turns a hiking trail into a hunting ground. **The Nameless God's rough half is out there**, and it can smell the rest of itself in MC.
- **Seam tears** (*hokorobi*). Rips between the worlds. People fall in, things fall out, and sometimes a whole office floor spends an hour on the wrong side. A konbini in Saitama spent forty minutes in the Ura last month; the staff came back with no memory of it and a tanuki's business card. Worse things live in the seams.

### People
- **The Kamikiri.** In folklore they were petty yokai with scissor beaks who snipped people's hair (*kami*, 髪) in the night. Someone gave them new shears and a new meaning for the word: *kami*, 神, gods. They were the blades in the surf on the Izumo Night. They're elite and fanatical, and they carry the only edges that can cut a tied knot. They don't need MC dead. They need MC *cut*.
- **Hakushi, "Clean Slate"** (Omote). A polished wellness movement with seminars, an app, and plenty of influencers, all selling the same line: *cut the ties that weigh you down.* In the back rooms they do it literally, with consecrated shears (lesser blades than the Izumo ones, but plenty for ordinary bonds). Members feel light, then hollow, then useful. The threads they cut don't go to waste.
- **Thread.** They get sold. In the Ura, stolen bonds are smoked in dens and chewed in alleys for a few hours of feeling exactly what someone else felt for someone they loved. Threadheads lose the ability to form bonds of their own and eventually join the Cut. Humans pay to have their love cut out, yokai pay to smoke it, and the cartel in the middle gets rich off both worlds. A strand of MC's knot would be the highest high in existence.
- **Tokoyo Pharmaceuticals** (Omote). A respectable company with excellent longevity supplements, made from ningyo flesh. It also trades in kitsune tails and tengu feathers. Its board would give a great deal to get MC into a tank.
- **Sakoku** (Ura). The movement to cut the worlds apart and close the door on humanity for good. Its grievances are real (poachers, exorcists, forgotten gods), and so are many of its members. Some of its leaders take orders from someone they can't remember meeting. A human who holds the two worlds together through their love life is everything Sakoku stands against.
- **Exorcists** (Omote). Neither villains nor friends. Plenty of them can't tell a kitsune nurse from a Patchwork and don't try. A human vessel full of god-power, surrounded by yokai lovers, looks a lot like a target.
- **Old monsters** (Ura). The Ura's wild places still have the classics: giant centipedes, earth spiders, sea serpents. Those are your big set pieces.

### Who's on MC's side (sort of)
- **The surviving gods**: scattered, diminished, mostly hiding. Some will help for a price. Some will try to take the musubi "for safekeeping."
- **The Fox Court**, which wants MC alive and married into the family.
- **A few exorcists and one beat cop** who've noticed the accidents clustering, and noticed that nobody else seems to notice.
- **Kisaragi's stationmaster**: neutral, for a fee.
- **The House, the Ubume, the kids, and the partners**: the only side that's entirely MC's.

## 11. The Unremembered

Behind the Kamikiri, behind Hakushi, and behind the voice some of Sakoku's leaders take orders from, there is something no one can remember. Its followers call it *Sensei*. You can sit with it for an hour and agree with every word, and five minutes later you won't remember its face, its voice, or that anyone else was in the room. It has cut every bond anyone ever had to it.

**By default** it's the old **god of severance**: the god people pray to when they need a bad marriage ended or a grief let go. For a thousand years its work was necessary. Somewhere along the way it decided that every bond is a leash and every fate is a cage, and that the kindest cut is the last one.

**Recommended twist (yours to veto):** it's one of MC's destined, the one thread it never managed to cut. That's why MC, and only MC, can remember it after meeting it. It's why it works through proxies and never comes in person: if they meet and MC refuses to love it, it dies like any other loose strand. And it puts the story's last question in MC's hands: cut it, or tie it.

## 12. Why MC goes out

1. **Libido.** The Swell isn't optional. A vessel that stays home alone becomes a lighthouse: MC's apartment fills up with frays, and worse things follow them in.
2. **Consequence.** MC's destined are being hunted right now, and fate will throw them into MC's path regardless. Meet one and turn away, and they're dead in days. Stay home, and the frays and the Kamikiri get to them first. Either way, people die because MC stayed in bed alone.
3. **The long game.** Every knot re-weaves a little of the world. The streets around MC go quiet as the frays thin out. The Cut can be mended and wild gods can be calmed. And Kannazuki is coming.

## 13. Knock-on effects

- **MC's neighborhood becomes a sanctuary.** The more knots MC ties, the quieter the area around them gets. Stray yokai, the half-Cut, and refugees from seam tears start drifting toward it, and MC ends up with a community by accident.
- **Everyone wants a piece, for different reasons.** The cutters want the knot cut. Tokoyo wants it bottled. Sakoku wants it gone. The Fox Court wants to marry into it. A growing number of people want to worship it. Not every enemy wants MC dead, and that's worse.
- **A fertility cult nobody asked for.** Rumors cross over, and an Ura shrine festival springs up in MC's honor, complete with floats. Nobody consults MC.
- **Honey traps.** The Spark makes MC a seduction target for anyone who wants luck, information, or a few strands of MC's hair. Not every Chime-less beauty at the bar is just flirting.
- **Enemies time things.** MC is regularly very pregnant, and the other side owns calendars.
- **Bloomed partners have explaining to do.** The nurse who woke up with fox ears has a shift at seven. The Glaze covers her at work. It doesn't cover her mother.
- **Love becomes tactics.** The enemy learns how the knot works and plays it: arranging meetings in the worst possible circumstances, manipulating MC into turning someone away, sending a destined partner in as the assassin.
- **Childhood goes too fast.** Each parent gets about three weeks of childhood per child: first steps on a Monday, a fight about curfew two weeks later. The only way to slow it down is to take the child outside, where things can see them.
- **The wardrobe.** A jorōgumo couturier in Edo treats MC's changing measurements as a personal challenge.
- **Onsen.** A problem in Omote. Not a problem at all in the Ura.
- **Pocket change.** Prayed-over five-yen coins buy a surprising amount in the Ura. Inaba calls this embezzlement.
- **The House has politics.** Many partners, many kids, one house, one bath. No magic makes them get along. The House makes it easier, and the war makes it necessary.

## 14. Sample strands (partner seeds)

Most partners should be met in play. These show the range; use them, change them, or toss them.

## 15. Tone

**Two engines run side by side.** The rom-com engine is Rentarou-style sincerity, absurd escalation, a chaotic house, Inaba's mouth, and MC's body complicating everything at the worst possible moment. The horror-action engine is invisible things eating people, feral gods, cartels, cults, and shears in the dark. When a threat is on screen, it's never the joke.

**A week, for flavor:**
- **Mon:** A nest of frays in a Shinjuku office tower. A whole department has started forgetting each other's names.
- **Tue:** Inaba books MC a date with a yuki-onna. MC is four days pregnant.
- **Wed:** Sunday's baby is walking, talking, and has opinions about bedtime.
- **Thu:** A raid on a Thread den in Naniwa goes wrong in an educational way.
- **Fri:** The Chime rings in the middle of a knife fight, for the one holding the knife.
- **Sat:** There's a festival in the Ura. It's for MC. There are floats.
- **Sun:** Someone's room in the House has gone cold, and MC has to figure out why.

**Touchstones:** *The 100 Girlfriends Who Really, Really, Really, Really, Really Love You* (heart and sincerity), *Noragami* (phantoms, gods, grit), *Natsume's Book of Friends* and *Spirited Away* (the texture of a yokai world), *Jujutsu Kaisen* (what a fight costs), *Monster Girl Encyclopedia* (who's in the dating pool and how they feel about it).

**Hard lines for everything we build:**
- **No visible threads, no glowing marks.** Bonds are felt and heard. They're never drawn on skin or hanging in the air.
- **No ledgers.** No registry of partners, no book of fates, no divine paperwork, no counts. Nobody knows the number.
- **Not a demographics metaphor.** The fertility is fallout from carrying a god's power, not a cure for anything.
- **Adults only.** Every partner is an adult. MC's children are family and never anything else.
- **Consequences stick.** NPCs die. Partners get hurt. The Cut stay cut unless someone does the work.
