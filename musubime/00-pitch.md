# MUSUBIME — The Knot of the Godless Month
*Setting pitch v0.1. Broad strokes only. The deep build comes after you've marked this up.*

---

## The one-liner

Every October the gods of Japan leave home and gather at Izumo to tie the coming year's red threads of fate. While they're away, small local kami are left to mind the shop. This year one of them fumbled it. A century of neglected threads, all of them threads that cross between **our Japan and the Japan behind it**, came loose at once and snapped onto the first person to ring her bell.

That's the MC. They're now **the Knot**: the single point where dozens of destined loves from two worlds meet. Meeting those people and not loving them gets those people killed. Doing nothing gets everyone killed, the MC included.

---

## 1. The Two Japans

### Omote (表, "the Front"): our Japan, 2026
Convenience stores, commuter trains, smartphones, rent. Almost nobody here knows the other side exists. The few who do are old shrine families, a handful of folklorists, and people who were "spirited away" as kids, came back, and learned to stop talking about it.

### Ura (裏, "the Back"): Ura-Akitsushima
Ura isn't the past, and it isn't a random parallel. **It's a refuge.** Japan always had a "back side": the far side of the mountains, the far side of dusk. Gods and yokai used both sides. Then Omote stopped believing. The killing blow was the **Meiji shrine mergers (1906–1914)**, which really did tear down tens of thousands of local shrines. After that, the displaced kami, the yokai, and even the animals Omote had driven extinct moved fully into the Back and built a country of their own.

**Look and feel:** Edo through Taishō in silhouette. Wooden castle towns, canal barges, paper lanterns burning with foxfire, rickshaws pulled by oni, tengu courier routes along the ridgelines, waterworks run by kappa. **Omote machines don't last there.** Ura's air smothers electricity within a day or two, so a phone is a brief luxury and a dead one is a collector's item. Ura isn't modern because of what it is, and also because it doesn't want to be.

**How Ura knows about us:**
- **Kamikakushi.** People have been slipping through for a thousand years. Some stayed, and their descendants are Ura's humans.
- **Nagaremono (流れ物), "drift."** Lost things wash up on Ura's lost-and-found shores: umbrellas left on trains, single gloves, keychains, phone charms, manga volumes, now and then a whole vending machine. Drift that sits long enough wakes up as tsukumogami.
- **The result is that Ura has an Omote craze.** Rich kitsune wear thrifted hoodies over silk kimono. Tanuki stalls sell "genuine Omote" coffee cans, empty, as display pieces. Scholars argue over what a *konbini* was *for*. Opinions of Omote run from starry-eyed romance to old resentment: *they tore down our shrines.*

**Crossing:** Through a torii at *ōmagatoki*, dusk, "the hour of meeting demons." Ordinary people cross rarely and by accident. **The Knot crosses on purpose** and can bring someone along by holding their hand. Time runs at the same speed in both worlds.

## 2. Peoples

**Ura**
- **Kami.** Minor gods ranging from household spirits to mountain lords. Personal, petty, generous, prideful.
- **Yokai.** Kitsune (each tail means more age and power), tanuki (merchant clans and the kitsune's eternal rivals), oni, tengu, kappa, nekomata, jorōgumo, yuki-onna, nure-onna, ningyo, rokurokubi, hone-onna, futakuchi-onna, tsukumogami, and more.
- **Kemonobito (獣人), beastfolk.** The animals of Japanese legend who became peoples. The headline is the **Japanese wolf**: extinct in Omote since 1905, alive in Ura as a proud mountain nation that remembers exactly why it left. Also Nara's deer, cranes (who still repay kindness), boar, monkeys, serow, and hares.
- **Ura humans.** Descendants of the spirited-away. Some have a little something else mixed in.
- **On gender:** in a country full of shapeshifters, sex and presentation are fluid and nobody finds them very interesting. Femboys and femme nonbinary folk are common and unremarked. A kitsune might switch for a party.

**Omote**
- Mostly ordinary humans.
- **Faded holdouts.** A few yokai never left. They're thin and half-real now, living on scraps of belief: the nekomata who runs a cat café, the yuki-onna on the night shift at a ski lodge, the kappa who swims laps at the ward pool at 5 a.m.
- **Hidden blood.** Old Japan married its monsters. Abe no Seimei's mother was a fox. Some fishing towns have a grandmother who never seems to age (ningyo). Some yamabushi families have a tengu somewhere back in the tree. **Some descendants know. Most don't.** This matters a lot (see the Blessing).

## 3. The Knot

### What happened
In October, Kannazuki, "the month without gods," **Tsunagi-hime (繋ぎ姫, "the Joining Princess")** was the minor kami of a nearly abandoned matchmaking shrine and had been left behind to keep an eye on the threads Izumo doesn't handle well: **the ones that cross between Omote and Ura.** Those have been neglected since the worlds pulled apart after Meiji. When the MC climbed her steps, rang her bell, and prayed (*the MC's prayer is yours to write; it should matter*), the snarl let go all at once and landed on them.

### What the MC is now
**The Musubime (結び目), the Knot.** An unknown number of invisible red threads now end at the MC's left little finger. "Dozens," says the deity. "About a hundred," says Inaba, who lies. Each thread leads to a destined partner somewhere in Omote or in Ura.

**No list, no tally, no map.** The MC finds partners by heat (§4) and recognizes them on contact.

### The meeting
When the MC first meets a destined partner, **both of them hear a single shrine bell that nobody else hears, and both feel a sharp tug at the little finger.** That's the whole sign: no light, no mark. From that moment they're bound.

### The rule (lethal)
Once they've met, a destined partner **has to be loved**, really loved and shown it, not managed or strung along.
- **Rejection or abandonment** brings on **itoyami (糸病み), thread-sickness:** sleeplessness, fever, appetite gone, a greyness other people start to notice. Then the world turns against them. A foot slips on a station platform, a sign falls. This takes days to weeks depending on how hard the thread snapped.
- **Ordinary absence** of a few days just aches, and reuniting cures it.
- As in Rentarou, partners feel the pull, but **the courtship still has to happen.** The bell starts things. It doesn't finish them.
- **Every destined partner is an adult.** No exceptions. That includes small-statured races, which are adult.

### The change
A Knot has to bind with every kind of person, so Tsunagi-hime remade the MC to give *and* receive: **futa, immensely virile, immensely fertile.** It's a solution, and she's sheepish about it.

## 4. The Heat: libido and compass in one

The tangle *pulls*. The MC lives with **en-netsu (縁熱, bond-fever)**, a constant simmer of arousal that **spikes near an unmet destined partner.**

- **It's the only compass.** It gets hotter as you get closer. It's hot-and-cold, not GPS, so a packed train car or a festival crowd turns into a nightmare game of *which one of you is it.*
- **Time with met partners** eases it. **Sex with destined partners** satisfies it. Sex with anyone else only takes the edge off.
- **A birth**, of any of the MC's children, cools it down for a while.

It's uncontrollable on purpose. The MC's body isn't fully theirs to say no to.

## 5. The Cost of Doing Nothing

If the MC refuses (hides, stops meeting people, tries to wait it out), the threads stay put. **The heat climbs, and the MC starts to turn.**

**Signs of turning, all physical and all easy to show in a scene:**
- Fine **red silk** starts growing from the fingertips and catches on fabric.
- The MC catches themself **tying knots** in cords, hair, straps, without thinking.
- Sleep gets thin. Hunger stops being about food.
- Later: spinnerets, extra joints, a reflection that's slow to follow.

Tying threads (meeting, loving, bedding) pushes the turning back.

**The endpoint:** the Knot becomes a great yokai, a living tangle, and **every met partner's thread snaps at once.** Every one of them dies of itoyami.

### The precedent: Ayatori-gozen
This has happened before. Around 1700, in the Genroku era, there was another Knot. Ura calls her **Ayatori-gozen, the Lady of the String Figures.** She ran, or ran out of time, and turned. She's still there: a vast web of red silk across a ravine deep in Ura, alive, patient, and something mothers warn children about. Everyone she had met died.

She is what failure looks like for the MC, and she's an open hook. Can a turned Knot be untangled? Does one of the MC's threads lead into that ravine?

## 6. Children and the Mayoiga

### Pregnancy
**One week, whether the MC carries or sires.**
- **Days 1–3:** nothing shows.
- **Days 4–6:** the belly rounds fast. In Omote this needs a cover story, which is a nice complication.
- **Day 7:** labor. It **pulls the parent to the nearest door, and the door opens into the Mayoiga.** Births happen there and never on a train platform. The body recovers in about a day.

### The Mayoiga (迷い家, the Lost House)
From Tōno folklore: a grand house in a hidden valley, empty-seeming, the hearth always warm, the rice always fresh. **It's Tsunagi-hime's apology gift.** It's half-alive and **grows rooms as the family grows.** The MC can open any door into it by intending to. Other people get in with the MC, or with the children once they're grown.

**Time runs at "a year a week."** While the MC is outside, each week out there is a year inside. **While the MC is in the house, time runs normally.** So:
- Children reach adulthood in about four months of the MC's time.
- Every visit they're older, which is bittersweet and easy to play in a scene.
- The MC doesn't age away their life by visiting.

### The Ubume
In folklore, the ubume is the ghost of a woman who died in childbirth and begs passersby to hold her baby. Here they've found peace. **The ubume are the Mayoiga's midwives and nannies.** They're gentle, a little eerie, and fiercely protective.

### The children (musubi-ko, 結び子)
- They mostly take after the other parent's kind, and they share a family knack for **bringing people together.**
- As adults they can come and go, so they become the MC's household: helpers, go-betweens between the worlds, a small clan with a hidden house.
- **Hard rule: they carry no threads to the MC or to each other. They're family, never partners.**

### The body keeps count
**Every child born of the MC, carried or sired, makes the MC's chest grow a step.** No ledger is needed. In Ura, people read it at a glance, the way you'd read a kitsune's tails.

## 7. The Blessing: *Okage* (お陰)

*Okage-sama de* means "thanks to you, by your grace." The first time the MC lies with a destined partner, the partner receives the **Okage**, and **it draws out what they already are:**

| Partner | Okage |
|---|---|
| Ordinary human | Good fortune in the thing they want most, strong health, a new knack |
| Human with hidden blood | **Awakening.** Ears, tail, horns, or scales come in over days, along with the powers that go with them. A coworker grows fox ears overnight. |
| Ura yokai | Growth. **A kitsune gains a tail.** An oni's strength doubles. A tsukumogami gains a full human form. |
| Faded Omote yokai | **Rooting.** They become solid and real again and stop fading. |
| Kami | A share of lost divinity returns. Their shrine gets visitors again. |
| Shapeshifter | Forms that hold steady, or a new form |

Later intimacy and shared children deepen it. **Non-destined partners** get a pleasant spell of luck and nothing more.

**Knock-on effects:** blessing-seekers. Kitsune houses would scheme for a tail. Tanuki guilds would try to sell access. Awakened Omote descendants suddenly need Ura's help to understand what they are. Faded holdouts quietly start looking for the MC.

## 8. The Guide: Inaba

**The White Hare of Inaba**, out of the Kojiki. She lied to the wani sharks to cross the sea, was flayed for it, and was healed by Ōkuninushi, the great god of matchmaking. Then she foretold his marriage. **She was the first matchmaker in Japanese myth,** and Izumo has sent her to clean up Tsunagi-hime's mess.

- **Form:** white hair, long hare ears, flawless skin she's quietly vain about (it grew back). She can **shrink into an actual white hare** and ride in the MC's bag through Omote.
- **Talent:** **she hears threads hum.** She can tell when one is close and taut. She can't tell whose it is or exactly where. She's a second compass, not a list.
- **Personality:** glib, teasing, an old trickster who's reformed but not *that* reformed. She only tells "small" lies now and always pays her debts. She makes hare jokes about the MC's libido and doesn't think they're jokes.
- **The hook:** she flatly insists she is *not* one of the MC's threads. She is a known liar.

## 9. The Culprit: Tsunagi-hime

The kami of a hillside matchmaking shrine down to one elderly caretaker. Earnest, overworked, scatterbrained, guilty. She lives in Omote near the MC and drops by with apologies and bad ideas. Every partner the MC ties gives her shrine a little more life. *(Is she on a thread too? Your call.)*

## 10. Who in Ura Cares

- **Izumo, Ōkuninushi's court.** Officially embarrassed, officially supportive. It sent Inaba. It privately has other options.
- **The En-kiri (縁切り), severance shrines.** These are real: shrines where you pray to *cut* a bad tie. In Ura they're an order that believes the tangle is an abomination that should be **cut at the Knot.** They say this kills only the MC. They might be wrong. They might be lying. They are antagonists with a point.
- **Kitsune houses and tanuki guilds.** They want the Okage for their own, and they turn matchmaking into politics.
- **The jorōgumo weavers.** The only people who truly understand thread. They have ties to Ayatori-gozen's ravine. They're dangerous allies.
- **Drift merchants.** Comic relief. They'd sell their mothers for a working phone charger and see the MC as the greatest business opportunity in a thousand years.

## 11. Tone

- **Rentarou-style romantic comedy** with a big cast and real stakes underneath.
- **Folk horror at dusk.** Torii, crossings, the ravine, the turning.
- **Warm domesticity** in the Mayoiga.
- **Explicit, enthusiastic, sex-positive.** Desire is the engine, not something to be ashamed of.

**Ground rules for the bible and the worldbook:**
- No ledgers, registries, or partner tallies. The body, the bell, and the heat carry the information.
- No glowing marks, sigils, or light shows. Signs are sound, touch, heat, silk, and flesh.
- Nothing that's hard to show in a scene. Every mechanic is something a character can see, hear, or feel.
- **This is not a metaphor for Japan's birth rate.** Births are personal and joyful. Nobody in either world talks about demographics.
- All partners are adults. Children are family, never partners.

## 12. Worldbook Plan (for after the bible)

The bible is the long-form source and the worldbook is the compressed version of it.
