# THE HUNDRED AND EIGHT KNOTS
### *Hyakuhachi Musubi* (百八結び) — Broad Pitch

> A matchmaking hare drops a year's worth of fate into a shrine's offering box at the worst possible moment. The person praying there walks away tied to a hundred and eight destined lovers, split across modern Japan and the hidden half of it that never left the age of gods. Meet one and fail to love them, and they die. Ignore them, and fate starts dragging them to you anyway.

---

## 1. The Two Sides: *Omote* and *Ura*

**The origin is real myth, pushed a little further.** In the *Kojiki*, Ōkuninushi, the god of Izumo and of matchmaking, hands rule of the land to Heaven's line (the *Kuni-yuzuri*). In return he keeps rule over "hidden matters." In this setting that wasn't a metaphor. The land was split like a kimono: the outer face and the lining.

- **Omote (表, "the Face")**: our Japan. Modern, electric, gods mostly quiet. That's the name the other side uses for it.
- **Ura (裏, "the Lining")**: the hidden half Ōkuninushi kept. Same islands, same mountains and rivers, different history. Gods walk openly and yokai never went into hiding. Edo-era Japan had a fashion called *ura-masari*: plain outside, lavish silk lining. The Ura is that: the side nobody sees, and the richer one.

### What the Ura is like
- **Not modern, and doesn't feel the lack.** The Ura never industrialized because it never needed to. Its work runs on *agreements with things that are alive*: foxfire lanterns, river kappa who run ferries for cucumbers and respect, tools that turned into tsukumogami after a hundred years of use. Picture Edo-to-Taishō streets: wood, paper, lacquer, lantern-light.
- **The rule of Turning.** In the Ura, anything that lives long enough, or is loved or feared enough, eventually *turns* and becomes a person. Cats become nekomata, foxes become kitsune, an old umbrella becomes kasa-obake. That's where the variety of peoples comes from, and why they range from "human with ears" to "fully a fox who walks upright and does taxes." Long-lived, heavily turned folk tend to look more beast or monster. Younger or shapeshifting folk look more human. Furry, MGE and kemonomimi all fit under one rule.
- **Foreign folk.** Other countries have their own Linings. The Ura has one sanctioned port where they meet, a Dejima analogue. That's where elves, centaurs, lamia, harpies, minotaur sailors and stranger things come from. They're traders, exiles and émigrés, and a little exotic even to the locals.
- **The capital is Ura-Izumo.** Ōkuninushi's seat. In Omote, October is *Kannazuki*, "the month without gods," because every god leaves for Izumo. In the Ura, Izumo is where they actually go: a festival city of shrines and bridges where the coming year's fates (*en*, 縁) are tied.

### How the Ura knows about us
- **Drift (*nagaremono*).** Things lost or forgotten in Omote wash up in the Ura: umbrellas left on trains, single socks, dead phones, abandoned bikes, manga left on seats. Japan loses a staggering number of umbrellas, so the Ura has a kasa-obake population boom. Drift is raw material, curiosity, status symbol and occasionally religious relic. Some backwater village has a dead vending machine for a shrine.
- **The spirited-away.** People who vanish in Omote (*kamikakushi*) sometimes just crossed over. A few stayed. They're the Face-born, and they're where most of the Ura's real knowledge of us comes from.
- **What the Ura thinks of Omote:** a loud, bright, iron country where the gods went quiet and everyone shows only their public face (*tatemae*). Ura scholars studying it from drifted manga hold some gloriously wrong theories. Ura folk see themselves as the side where people say what they mean (*honne*), and that includes what they want in bed.

### Crossing: *go the back way at dusk*
*Ura* means "back," so you enter the Lining through the **back of things**: a shrine's rear gate, a back alley, a shop's back door, the back of a mirror. It only works at *ōmagatoki*, dusk, the old "hour of meeting demons." It's easy to render and evocative, and it has hard limits. Inaba can take MC across at other hours. Later the Tamatebako (§4) works as a door to both sides.

### Omote isn't purely mundane
The old crossings left blood behind. Abe no Seimei's mother was a fox, and that's the famous case, not the only one. Some Omote humans have **fantastic ancestry**:
- **Known**: old shrine families, the leftovers of the onmyō tradition, a fishing village that remembers its mermaid great-grandmother.
- **Unknown**: the convenience-store clerk whose grandmother "came from nowhere and left the same way" was a crane wife. She has no idea.

Most of it sleeps. Partnering with MC can wake it (§4).

## 2. The Inciting Incident: Kannazuki

It's October and the gods are away at Izumo. Every year their messengers carry the fates of each region to the council. One of those messengers is **Inaba**, the White Hare of Inaba from the *Kojiki*: the trickster hare the sharks flayed and Ōkuninushi healed, the one who prophesied his marriage. That makes Inaba the first matchmaker in Japanese myth.

Inaba was carrying a bundle of red threads and stopped at their own small, neglected branch shrine. MC was there, threw a **5-yen coin** in the offering box (*go-en*, a pun on *goen*, "fate, connection," which is why people offer it) and prayed for someone. Inaba startled the way hares do and dropped the bundle. It fell into the box with MC's coin and prayer, and every thread in it tied itself to the last wish it touched.

**About a hundred and eight threads.** It's the number of earthly desires (*bonnō*), the number of times temple bells ring on New Year's Eve. It's approximate. Inaba didn't count before dropping them.

The council at Izumo can't undo it. Cutting a destined thread counts as rejection, and rejection is fatal (§3). So they ruled: *if the knot can't be undone, make the one who holds it able to keep it.* They remade MC (§4), lent them the Tamatebako, and assigned Inaba to fix their own mess.

## 3. The Rules of the Thread

Keep these simple and physical. Nothing glows.

- **The Tug.** In Japanese folklore the red thread ties the left little finger. When MC first makes real contact with a destined partner (eye contact, a touch, a voice at close range), both feel it: a sharp pull in the left pinky, a jolt under the sternum, a heartbeat that won't settle. Neither can mistake it, and nobody else notices anything. Both sides feel it, so partners know too.
- **Inaba can see the threads.** Red eyes see red thread. MC can't see them, only feel a faint directional tug in the pinky when a destined partner is near, which gets stronger with distance closed. That gives a hunt without a map.
- **Met and not loved → the curse.** After the Tug, if the bond is refused, neglected or betrayed, the partner falls under **the oldest curse there is**. Izanami was the first wife. Her husband saw her dead and rotting and ran. At the boulder sealing the underworld she swore death on the living. In this setting her curse lives inside every red thread: *those who meet their destined and turn away shall die.* It almost never triggers, because most people have one thread and never meet its other end, or do and marry. MC has a hundred and eight. The curse shows up as escalating misfortune and wasting over about a month: near-misses, illness, the smell of rot, a woman in a filthy kimono at the edge of sight. Then death.
- **Tying the knot.** A bond is safe once it's **tied**: mutual love acknowledged by both, then consummated. *Musubi* means a knot, and in Shinto also the generative force of creation. The curse lifts, and the blessing (§4) is the proof.
- **Unmet partners pine.** Since the knot pulled all the threads taut at once, every untied destined partner suffers *koi-wazurai*, classical lovesickness: restlessness, fever dreams of someone they've never met, slowly fading. It's slow (months, not weeks) but real, and some of them started out fragile.
- **Fate doesn't wait.** If MC hides, the threads start **reeling partners in**: wrong trains, sudden downpours, a shared umbrella, a transfer student. Romcom coincidences become a literal force, so the narrator can throw meet-cutes at MC whenever it likes. Forced meetings are unprepared meetings, and unprepared meetings go wrong.

## 4. The Gift: What MC Became

The council needed MC able to tie a thread to *anyone*: woman, man, femboy, nonbinary, oni, fox, goddess. So they gave MC both halves of creation, in the spirit of the paired creator gods Takamimusubi and Kamimusubi.

- **MC is made futanari.** Immensely virile and immensely fertile, able to sire and to carry.
- **One-week gestation.** Any child conceived *of* MC or *in* MC comes to term in about seven days. There's mythic precedent: the blossom goddess Konohanasakuya-hime conceived in a single night. The belly grows visibly day by day, which is easy to render. A one-week pregnancy can't go anywhere near an Omote hospital, so births happen in the Tamatebako.
- **MC's breasts grow with each birth** MC carries. That's cumulative and permanent, and it causes practical problems (clothing, posture, being recognized) that the bible will give solutions for (see the tsukumogami tailor below).

### The Fever (reason #1)
The generative force builds up in MC and has to go somewhere. In three stages:
1. **Simmer.** Constant low arousal, distractibility, a warm scent people lean toward.
2. **Boil.** It spills into the surroundings: flowers open out of season, people nearby flush and lose their train of thought, animals get frisky. These are cues a narrator can easily describe.
3. **Scald.** MC can barely think, and fate pulls threads in harder (see "Fate doesn't wait").

**Relief:** any sex takes the edge off for a while. Only **tying a destined bond** truly resets it. Flings buy time and destined bonds buy peace.

### The Blessing (*musubi no kago*)
Tying a bond **completes what the partner is**. The effects are physical and lasting, never a tattoo.
- **Human, no ancestry**: robust health and vitality, one talent sharpened to a fine point, and the ability to see the Ura and walk the back way.
- **Human, dormant ancestry**: it **wakes** over the following day: ears, a tail, feathers along the arms, gills that only open in salt water. Comedy, panic and identity crisis follow. Known ancestry gets stronger instead.
- **Yokai and races**: a step up in power (a kitsune gains a tail) or something missing restored. A nopperabō gains a face. A tsukumogami can finally leave its object for good. A cursed being gets relief.
- **Gods**: their faded strength or worship comes back, or something they lost is returned.
- **For every tied partner**: they can always find their way back to MC, and they can cross between the sides alongside MC.

### The Tamatebako (pocket dimension)
In the Urashima Tarō story, the Dragon Palace princess gives a lacquered jewel box that **holds years**. The council requisitioned it from Ryūgū-jō for MC. The princess is annoyed (see the partner seeds).

- Open the lid anywhere and it's a door into a **hidden valley**, a sky that cycles through the seasons every few days, and a **living house** (a tsukumogami ryokan) that adds rooms as the family grows.
- **The box pays out years to those who haven't had theirs.** Children inside grow about **one year per day** until they're grown, then age normally. Adults inside don't age at all. It's a safe home base for both realms, and it's always pretty.
- **The midwife is an *ubume***, the ghost of a woman who died in childbirth. Folklore has her haunting crossroads, begging strangers to hold her baby. She got purpose back, and she runs the nursery like a field marshal.
- **The children (*musubi-ko*)** take after both parents: a fox child, an oni child, a human child with MC's eyes. They're born of both sides and are the only beings who **cross freely, without dusk or back ways**. Grown, they become MC's household: helpers, scouts, guardians of partners in hiding, and a faction in their own right later on.

## 5. Inaba, the Guide

**Inaba of the White Hare**, a minor kami of matchmaking. The default proposal is *femme-presenting, nonbinary*: small, white-haired, long ears (one notched), red eyes, and they smell of cattail pollen (the remedy Ōkuninushi used to heal them, which they still carry in a pouch).

- **A compulsive liar**, which is how the sharks got them, **with one exception**: since the flaying they *cannot* lie about threads. They lie about everything else, constantly. So they're trustworthy exactly where it matters and infuriating everywhere else.
- **Guilty and invested.** This is their mess. If it goes badly they lose their shrine, their status and Ōkuninushi's favor.
- **Can** see threads, cross the sides at will, and talk to gods as a messenger. **Can't** fight much, make anyone fall in love, or cut a thread.
- **Hates sharks** (crocodiles too, to be safe) and hates the Eighty Brothers (§6) with their whole small body.
- *Optional twist:* Inaba swears loudly that their own thread isn't in that bundle.

## 6. Why MC Goes Out, and Who Stands in the Way

### MC's reasons
1. **The Fever.** MC can't stay home and stay sane.
2. **The cost of inaction.** Partners are pining somewhere, some of them fragile. Hiding doesn't stop meetings, it only makes them worse ones. Every destined partner MC finds before fate forces a meeting is one whose first encounter MC chose.

### Opposition
- **The Yasogami, the Eighty Brothers.** In the myth these eighty brother gods tricked Inaba into being flayed, lost Princess Yakami to Ōkuninushi, and killed him twice out of jealousy (a red-hot boulder dressed as a boar, a tree split to crush him). Now a mortal has a hundred and eight destined loves and they still have none. They're eighty jealous suitor-gods, each with his own pet scheme: sabotaging first meetings, seducing partners away, faking a Tug, or killing MC outright. They're mostly petty and sometimes lethal, which makes them good recurring villains.
- **Yomi's collectors, the *shikome*.** The hags of the underworld. They aren't schemers. They come when a thread is spurned, because the curse is a debt and they collect. They show up as rot-smell, a dirty kimono, too many fingers. **Peaches ward them off**, because Izanagi threw peaches at them during his escape. MC's household is never without peaches.
- **The Onmyō remnant (Omote).** The government bureau of onmyōdō was formally abolished in 1870. A small knot of shrine families quietly kept watching the back ways. They aren't a bureaucracy, just tired custodians who are suddenly very alarmed. They can be allies, rivals or partners.
- **Ura courts.** A blessing that strengthens bloodlines is politically priceless. The kitsune houses of Inari, the tanuki merchant guilds and the tengu mountain abbeys will all court MC, push hopeful heirs, and try to pass off non-destined candidates. The Tug can't be faked, at least not without help from a Yasogami.

## 7. Knock-On Effects

- **The back ways are thinning around MC.** Each tied cross-realm thread pulls the Face and the Lining a little closer. Expect more drift, yokai sightings in MC's town, and Ura folk wandering into Omote convenience stores. That's *action* having consequences too.
- **Awakened humans need somewhere to go.** A woman who grew a tail overnight can't go to work Monday. MC's household becomes a refuge, with the Tamatebako as a safe house.
- **MC is a celebrity in the Ura.** An Omote native at all is a curiosity. One whom the gods remade is a legend in the making. MC's pocket change counts as wealth: 5-yen coins are luck charms over there.
- **En-kiri shrines are deadly to MC.** Shrines for cutting bad ties exist, but cutting a destined thread counts as rejection.
- **The partners know about each other.** Tied partners feel a faint kinship, *braided* threads. It doesn't erase jealousy, which is still allowed and still fun, but it leans the harem toward household rather than battlefield.
- **MC's body needs practical support.** A tsukumogami kimono-tailor (a sewing box that turned) works out how to dress a chest that grows after every birth. Omote clothing stores are a recurring humiliation.
- **Romcom tropes are physics now.** The narrator can lean on coincidence freely, because coincidence is fate working.

## 8. Partner Seeds (a taste, not a roster)

**Omote**
- **The miko across town.** She has unknown fox blood from the Kuzunoha line, distant kin to Seimei. Her shrine's fox statues have started turning their heads toward her.
- **The night-shift konbini clerk.** Unknown crane-wife descent. She weaves compulsively at 3 a.m. and doesn't know why.
- **A femboy onnagata of the kabuki stage.** His family has known about its tanuki line for generations and keeps it buried.
- **Yao Bikuni.** The nun who ate mermaid flesh 800 years ago and never died. She runs a seaside soba shop and wants either an ending or a reason.
- **Kuchisake-onna.** The urban legend, now a dental hygienist. Everyone wears a mask, so nobody notices. She still asks *"Am I pretty?"*, and with a destined thread involved, the answer is life or death.
- **Hachishaku-sama.** The eight-foot woman of the rice-field roads, lonely in the rural emptiness. *Po, po, po.*
- **The Onmyō detective.** Known fox ancestry, which she resents completely. Assigned to investigate MC.

**Ura**
- **Otohime of Ryūgū-jō.** She wants her box back, and she'll take MC with it.
- **Iwanaga-hime.** The rock princess. The god Ninigi sent her home for being plain and kept her lovely sister, and humanity lost its long lives for it. She's the original *unloved bride*, huge and stony and patient. Her blessing is longevity.
- **Kiyohime.** A monk fled from her once, so she became a serpent and burned him alive inside a temple bell. She's the met-and-not-loved story walking around: devoted, and extremely dangerous to disappoint.
- **An oni bandit chief of Onigashima**, femme and nonbinary. Drinks, brawls, keeps their word.
- **A karasu-tengu warrior-monk** sworn to celibacy. That's going well.
- **A tanuki merchant femboy** and his running feud with the kitsune trading house.
- **A jorōgumo madam** of Ura-Izumo's pleasure quarter, who has seen everything and is not used to her heart racing.
- **A lamia cartographer at the foreign port**, who has mapped every Lining except her own feelings.
- **The umbrella MC lost at nine.** It drifted, turned, and became a person who has been waiting a very long time to be picked up again.

## 9. The Deep Thread (optional long arc)

Inaba admits, eventually and without being able to lie about it, that one thread in the bundle **doesn't go sideways. It goes down**, under the floor of the world, past the boulder at Yomotsu Hirasaka. The first bride who was met and not loved, whose curse built this whole lethal system, is on the end of MC's hundred-and-eighth thread.

Whether to follow it, and what tying *that* knot would do to the curse, is the endgame.

## 10. Design Guardrails

- **All partners are adults.** Ancient, ageless or young-looking types are explicitly adult in age and mind.
- **Offspring are family, never partners.** Musubi-ko are kin, household and allies only.
- **Nothing glows.** No marks, sigils or HUDs. Bonds show up as feelings, behavior and bodily change.
- **No bookkeeping.** The count of 108 is approximate and the story never tracks it. Nobody keeps registries or ledgers. Inaba's eyes and MC's pinky do the work.
- **It isn't a metaphor about Japan's birthrate.** The births are a mythic consequence of musubi, not commentary.
- **Everything has to work in roleplay.** Every rule is something a narrator can describe in a sentence and a player can act on.

## 11. Worldbook Note

You want every entry always on, so the whole worldbook gets sent with every message. The plan is to keep the **bible** deep and the **worldbook** lean: distilled rules, tone and the core cast at roughly 4–6k tokens total, with partner entries as short hooks you can expand.
