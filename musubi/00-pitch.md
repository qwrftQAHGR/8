# MUSUBI 結び — Broad Pitch (v0.1)

> *Musubi* means "a knot", as in the red thread of fate. It also means "generation": in Shinto, *musubi* (産霊) is the creative, life-making force behind the kami Takami-musubi and Kami-musubi. The whole setting is built on that double meaning: **the knot that ties people, and the power that makes life.**

**Tags:** Japan · modern day · parallel world · yokai · monster girls / MGE / kemono · femboys & femme NB · hidden fantastic ancestry · harem romance with real stakes

---

## 1. The One-Paragraph Pitch

There are two Japans. **Utsushiyo** (現世) is the one you know. **Kakuriyo** (隠世) is its hidden twin, a parallel archipelago where yokai, beastfolk and monster races built their own modern world after the Veil came down between the realms a thousand years ago. When the Veil fell it cut every red thread that ran from one side to the other: centuries of destined love that could never happen. Those severed threads didn't vanish. They drifted, tangled and rotted into a single snarl, and that snarl has finally caught on someone. When the MC wanders into a forgotten branch shrine of the **White Hare of Inaba**, the knot settles on them. Now they're tied to roughly a hundred destined partners across both worlds. They've been remade into a living vessel of *musubi*: futa, absurdly virile and fertile, with a body that answers to the gods' oldest power. A sharp-tongued hare goddess is their guide. And they have two very good reasons not to sit still: a hunger they can't switch off, and a calamity building up inside them that gets out if they don't follow the threads.

## 2. The Two Realms

### Utsushiyo: Our Japan
Modern Japan, unchanged on the surface: konbini, commuter trains, cram schools, rainy-season gloom. Underneath, it has quietly bled into Kakuriyo for a thousand years:

- **Hidden-ancestry humans.** The old folktales are true. Kuzunoha the fox-wife, the yuki-onna who married a woodcutter, oni brides in mountain villages, mermaid-flesh eaters. Their descendants are all over the place, and most have no idea. Some families half-know ("we don't go out on snowy nights", "grandma had fox teeth"). Others are oblivious until something wakes the blood.
- **A handful of people who know.** A few old shrine families and onmyōdō lineages remember that the other side exists. They aren't a government agency, just a scattered, ageing network of priests, a few rich families, and some people who were spirited away and came back.
- **Thin places.** Torii at the edge of town, abandoned stations, tunnels, the last car of the last train. At *ōmagatoki* (逢魔時), the twilight "hour of meeting demons", these can open.

### Kakuriyo: The Hidden Japan
**Same island shapes, different country.** Kakuriyo is not medieval and not a fairy-tale forest. It had its own modernisation and runs on different principles:

- **Living technology.** In Kakuriyo, any object used for long enough wakes up as a *tsukumogami*. So their tech is *alive*. A phone might sulk, trams have moods, a rice cooker might refuse to work for someone rude. Engineers are part mechanic, part diplomat. Power comes from foxfire lanterns, river spirits under contract, and lightning bargained down from the raijū.
- **Imported modernity.** For centuries, people spirited away from our side (*kamikakushi*) have arrived carrying knowledge, and lately carrying phones. Kakuriyo has trains, neon, pop idols and convenience stores, all a few degrees off: the konbini is run by a tanuki chain, the idol is a rokurokubi, the bullet train is a lacquered serpent-carriage.
- **Humans live there too.** *Kamikakushi* families, descended from the spirited away, are a real minority. Some are proud of it, some are treated as exotic, some keep their heads down.
- **Fluid bodies, fluid norms.** In a society full of shapeshifters, gender presentation is casual and personal. Femboys, femme NB folk and people who change presentation from day to day are ordinary in Kakuriyo. A kitsune who looks different every week is barely worth gossiping about.

**Sketch geography (to expand later):**
- **Miyako (都)**: the capital, a Heian-kyō that never fell. Vermilion gates beside neon towers, ruled by old yokai aristocratic houses, with kitsune clans heading most of them. Court politics, marriage alliances, bloodline obsession.
- **Ōe**: the opposite number to Edo/Tokyo, built on oni money. A sprawl of industry, nightlife and blunt people who don't trust fox politics.
- **Kurama Heights / the Tengu Peaks**: mountain city-states of the tengu, conservative and martial. They believe the Veil exists for good reason.
- **The Riverlands**: the kappa run the water and know it, and every bridge has a toll and a guardian.
- **Yuki-no-Kuni**: the far snowy north. Yuki-onna matriarchies, ice-fishing towns, a long winter.
- **The Southern Seas**: ningyo, wani (sea-dragons), and island courts that barely count as part of the country.

**Crossing over.** Ordinary people cross only at twilight, through thin places, and not reliably. The best-known route is a phantom train line that pulls into **Kisaragi Station** (きさらぎ駅, the internet ghost story is true) and only runs at dusk. Getting back is the hard part.

## 3. What Happened: The Knot

Every October, Kannazuki, "the month without gods", Japan's kami gather at Izumo to decide the year's matches and tie the red threads. The White Hare of Inaba was the first matchmaker in myth: she foretold the marriage of Ōkuninushi, the god of en-musubi. Since then her job has been to carry threads where the gods can't easily go.

About a thousand years ago the Veil came down between the realms. Nobody agrees on who did it (theories in §9). Every thread that crossed the divide was cut. Cut threads are supposed to dissolve, but these were *destined* threads, and destiny doesn't dissolve tidily. For centuries the loose ends drifted along the seam between worlds, tangling into a **snarl** of unrealised love, longing and unborn generations. Inaba was meant to carry those threads, and she has spent ten centuries trying to keep the snarl from spoiling.

She failed. Left alone too long, *musubi* (life-force) sours into *magatsuhi* (禍津日), calamity-force. The snarl was about to go bad. It needed an anchor, a living person it could resolve through. The MC turned up at the right shrine at the right moment, or the wrong one, and the snarl caught.

**So the MC's ~100 destined partners are not random.** Each of them was the other half of a love the Veil cut off. Those severed destinies have been re-routed onto one person. That explains why they're spread across both realms, why there are so many, and why some are humans who don't even know they have yokai blood.

> Inaba doesn't have a list and won't make one. She can feel when the MC is *near* a thread. She doesn't know the exact number. "About a hundred, give or take, stop asking."

## 4. The Rules of the Thread

Kept deliberately simple, so they're easy to play in RP.

**Recognition.** When the MC first meets a destined partner, *both* feel it: a sharp tug in the left little finger, where the red thread is tied in folklore, and a hard lurch of the heart. It feels like missing a step on the stairs. No glow, no marks, no UI. Nobody else notices anything. Only the two of them know something just happened.

**The pull.** After that, the partner is *drawn* to the MC. Not mind-controlled: they notice the MC, think about them, find excuses to be nearby, and are oddly compatible with them. Whether they *like* that is another matter. Proud ones resent it, shy ones panic, cynical ones fight it.

**Love closes the thread.** Mutual, real affection *ties* the knot. It has to be genuine. Seduction, coercion, faked feelings and sex without feeling don't satisfy the thread. The MC has to actually love them, and they have to love the MC back.

**Thread-rot (the Rentarou rule).** Once they've met, a partner whose thread goes unanswered begins to rot. The MC can neglect them, reject them or simply fail to love them, and it happens either way. It's slow, visible and tragic:
1. **Pining.** Insomnia, loss of appetite, fever-dreams of the MC, a feeling of wrongness.
2. **Ill luck.** Things break around them. Small accidents, lost jobs, burnt meals, stumbles at the edges of platforms.
3. **Calamity.** The misfortune turns lethal. A truck, a fall, a fire, a sickness no doctor can explain.

The timeline depends on the person (a human might last weeks, a hardy oni months), but it always ends the same way. **Meeting a partner starts the clock.** That's why the MC can't simply avoid people, and why the romance is never optional for long.

**Intimacy brings the blessing.** Love *ties* the thread and stops the rot. Being with the MC physically *after* the thread is tied passes on *okage* (お陰), the blessing (§6).

## 5. What the MC Becomes

When the knot catches, the MC's body is remade into a vessel for *musubi*. The threads carry both halves of every severed match, wives and husbands, mothers and fathers. So the vessel has to be able to do *everything*:

- **Futa.** Fully functional on both sides.
- **Immensely virile and fertile.** Their stamina is extraordinary, their recovery is unnatural, and their fertility is close to absolute.
- **One-week gestation.** Any child conceived *of* the MC, or *in* the MC, comes to term in about **seven days**. The pregnancy is fast, visible and intense, and the birth is quick and blessedly safe (musubi looks after its own). This goes for the MC's partners and for the MC.
- **A body that keeps the score.** The MC's breasts grow fuller with each birth. It's a physical record of how much life has passed through them. Expect the MC's wardrobe to need regular replacing.
- **The hunger.** The libido is not a quirk. It's the pressure of a thousand years of unrealised love pushing through one body. It builds through the day, flares near destined partners, and cannot be ignored for long (§7).

**What the MC does *not* get:** no combat powers, no magic beam, no stat sheet. Their "power" is generative: they make life, and they make the people they love stronger. Anything else, they have to borrow, earn or charm out of someone.

## 6. Okage: The Blessing

Being with the MC passes a share of *musubi* into the partner. It's a blessing shaped to *who they are*, and it works with their nature instead of overwriting it:

- **Yokai grow into themselves.** A one-tailed kitsune wakes up with a second tail. A weak tanuki finds their illusions holding firm. A tsukumogami gains a body that no longer depends on its object.
- **Hidden ancestry wakes up.** The office worker whose great-grandmother was a yuki-onna starts frosting the windows. The quiet classmate with mermaid blood can suddenly breathe underwater. Some partners find out *what they are* this way, and that can be the start of a whole arc.
- **Humans get the small miracles.** Luck turns. Old illnesses fade. Talent sharpens. Aging slows a little.
- **The bond itself.** Partners can sense each other's distress at a distance, and every partner has a way into the MC's home (§8).

Blessings deepen with the *depth* of the bond, not the number of encounters. A partner who is truly loved gains more than one who is merely satisfied.

The obvious knock-on: **people will want this.** Kakuriyo's power-hungry noble houses would kill to get musubi into their bloodlines. Partners who gain power gain enemies. Partners who didn't know they were yokai have to work out what to tell their families.

## 7. Why the MC Can't Sit Still

**Reason one: the hunger.** It's unmanageable. Self-relief barely takes the edge off. Sex with people who aren't destined partners helps briefly but brings no blessing, and the MC can't count on it. The pressure only truly eases with destined partners, and a *new* thread tied gives the deepest relief. On a bad day the MC's thinking gets foggy, they turn reckless, and their skin runs hot.

**Reason two: the calamity.** The snarl was going bad when it caught the MC, and it still is. While threads stay untied, the *musubi* inside the MC slowly sours into *magatsuhi*. If the MC stalls, ignores the threads or tries to hide:
1. **Fever and ache.** The hunger turns painful.
2. **Bad luck leaks out.** Milk sours around them. Mirrors crack. Strangers start arguments. Plants wilt on the windowsill.
3. **Calamity draws calamity.** Misfortune spirits start to circle: binbōgami (poverty gods), yakubyōgami (plague gods), hungry ghosts. Some of them follow the MC home.
4. **The end state.** If it goes on long enough, the MC becomes a *magatsukami*, a god of calamity. It wouldn't end with the MC dying, either.

**Plus the partners' clock.** Every partner the MC has met and not yet loved is rotting. So the MC is caught both ways: tie threads to stay sane, and tie them fast enough that nobody dies, without ever faking the love that makes it work.

The easiest way to relieve the pressure is to *find more partners*. Finding more partners starts more clocks. That's the engine of the story.

## 8. The Mayoiga: Home Between Worlds

In Tōno folklore, the **Mayoiga** (迷い家, "the house you get lost into") is a grand house deep in the mountains. It appears only to lost travellers and is always empty, warm and waiting. Anyone who takes something from it is blessed.

The MC is given one.

- **Getting in.** The MC can slide open *any* sliding door (fusuma, shoji, closet, train door) while thinking of home, and step into the Mayoiga's entranceway. Partners whose threads are tied can do the same. Nobody else can find it unless they're brought.
- **What it is.** An old, rambling farmhouse-estate that grows new rooms as the household grows. Around it are endless mountain valleys, rice terraces and a hot spring, plus seasons that don't quite match the outside world. The house is a living thing, kindly and a little possessive. It has opinions about guests.
- **Children grow fast here.** Children born of musubi grow at a dizzying rate inside the Mayoiga. A newborn is crawling in days, walking and talking within a couple of weeks, and fully grown within a season or two. Their growth then levels off and they age normally from that point. Ordinary visitors age at the normal rate. The house speeds up *musubi*, not time itself.
- **The children (*musubiko*).** They take after the parent who isn't the MC: a kitsune partner has kitsune children, an oni partner has oni. They also carry something of the MC, and they can usually *sense the red threads* far better than their parent can. As they grow they become the household, keeping the house, helping the MC and defending the family. Eventually they go out into both worlds, scattering new lines of musubi blood. **They are family, and never romantic or sexual partners.** That's a hard line in the setting.
- **Why it matters.** A hundred partners, children arriving weekly, two worlds and nowhere to put anyone. The Mayoiga solves the logistics, and it also turns into the emotional centre of the story: a growing, chaotic, multi-species household.

The Mayoiga is also the one thing the MC's enemies most want to find.

## 9. The Guide: Inaba

**The White Hare of Inaba** (因幡の白兎) is a real figure of myth. She tricked the sea-crocodiles into forming a bridge, was skinned for it, and was healed by Ōkuninushi. Out of gratitude she foretold his marriage and became the patron of matchmaking. **Hakuto Shrine**, with stone hares in place of guardian lion-dogs, is a real en-musubi shrine.

**This Inaba:**
- **Form.** Usually a small, sharp-eyed woman with long white hare ears, dressed somewhere between a shrine maiden and someone who just rolled out of bed. She can turn into an actual white rabbit, which she does to get out of conversations.
- **Personality.** A reformed trickster. Fast-talking, smug and a terrible liar who still lies anyway. Deeply, privately guilty: the snarl was her job, she spent a thousand years failing at it, and now a mortal is paying the price. She covers that with teasing.
- **Role.** She senses when the MC is near a thread, explains the rules (mostly correctly), handles the gods, knows Kakuriyo's back alleys, and nags. She's also comic relief and the closest thing the MC has to a confidante.
- **Limits.** She's a minor goddess, strong in luck and prophecy and weak in a fight. She doesn't know everything, and she's sometimes flat-out wrong. Gods she outranks are rare.
- **The open question.** Is Inaba one of the threads? (She'll swear she isn't. The pinky tug at the shrine was "static electricity".)

## 10. Pressure From Outside

The setting needs friction beyond the harem. Seed factions:

- **Hashihime of Uji and the Severers.** Hashihime is the real folkloric "bridge princess", a woman so consumed by jealousy that she became a demon goddess of *en-kiri*, the cutting of bonds. People come to her shrines to end relationships. She sees the knot as fate *enslaving* a hundred people to one stranger. She wants to cut it. Cutting it would also kill every partner at once, and she either doesn't believe that or has decided it's worth it. She's a philosophical antagonist: free will against destiny. (Possibly one of the threads herself. That would be exquisite.)
- **The Great Houses of Miyako.** Kitsune and other noble clans who want musubi bloodlines. Some will court the MC honestly. Others will try to capture, bargain with or blackmail them. They're political, patient, and very good at smiling.
- **The Tengu Peaks.** They believe the Veil stopped a war. The knot sits right on the seam between worlds, and every tied thread thins the Veil a little. They'd prefer the MC stopped, by persuasion if possible.
- **Calamity spirits.** Binbōgami, plague gods and hungry ghosts, drawn to the magatsuhi in the MC like flies. They aren't schemers, just a steady trickle of supernatural trouble.
- **Human-side watchers.** An old onmyōdō family, an ageing priest who knows exactly what Hakuto shrines are for, a rich collector of the strange. These are allies, obstacles or both. Small-scale and personal, not an agency.
- **The Veil itself.** Who brought it down, and why? (Early theories: the gods, to stop a war; a single heartbroken goddess; the tengu; nobody, because it was a natural cataclysm.) Each tied thread mends a little of what the Veil broke. Tie enough of them and the two worlds might start touching again. Is that a good thing? Nobody agrees.

## 11. Sample Destined Partners (Tone Seeds)

These aren't final characters, just a range of what the thread pulls in:

- **The overworked oni.** An Ōe logistics manager, two metres tall, horned, perpetually exhausted, who thinks romance is for people with free time. Her thread-rot would come as a forklift accident.
- **The kitsune heiress.** A three-tailed daughter of a Miyako great house whose family *wants* her to land the MC, which is exactly why she refuses.
- **The tanuki femboy clerk.** Works the night shift at a Kakuriyo konbini and runs small scams on the side. He's adorable and knows it, and he's far more serious than he lets on.
- **The classmate who doesn't know.** A quiet human girl in the MC's own town whose family "doesn't go out on snowy nights". After okage, her breath starts to fog in summer.
- **The jorōgumo tailor.** A spider-woman dressmaker who notices the MC's growing chest and ends up as their permanent outfitter, along with whatever else she becomes.
- **The nekomata, femme NB.** A two-tailed cat spirit, a mercenary courier between the realms. Grumpy, sleeps all day, and knows both worlds' underground better than Inaba does.
- **The kappa lifeguard.** Sunny, athletic, from the Riverlands. Very literal about "saving" the MC from things.
- **The spirited-away human.** Born in Kakuriyo to kamikakushi parents, and has never seen the human world they were "supposed" to belong to.

## 12. Tone & Design Principles

- **Harem comedy with a real heart.** As warm and chaotic as *100 Girlfriends*, but the death clock is real and the love has to be real too. People can laugh *and* lose someone.
- **Easy to play.** Every rule is something you can show in a scene: a tug in the pinky, a sour carton of milk, a sliding door that opens onto a mountain house. No meters, no glowing sigils, no status windows.
- **No paperwork.** No registries, no partner ledger, no bureaucratic tallies. Inaba *refuses* to keep count, as a running joke. The world's institutions are people, not forms.
- **Folklore first.** Every big piece is anchored in real Japanese myth (musubi, Inaba, Izumo, Kannazuki, the Mayoiga, Hashihime, ōmagatoki, Kisaragi Station, kamikakushi) and then pushed further.
- **Not a metaphor.** This is not about Japan's birth rate, and nobody makes speeches about demographics. It's about one person, a hundred people they're meant to love, and two worlds catching up with each other.
- **Adults only.** Every destined partner is an adult. The children who grow up in the Mayoiga are family, full stop.

## 13. Questions For You (Before the Deep Build)

1. **The MC.** You're writing their background. Do you want the setting to stay neutral on their gender and origin before the shrine, or should I build hooks around a default? Also, did they know about their own ancestry, or do they have any?
2. **Breast growth.** Does it happen with every child of theirs (sired *or* carried), or only births the MC carries? With ~100 partners, "every child" escalates fast. A blend would work too: big growth for births they carry, smaller for children they father.
3. **Non-destined partners.** Can the MC have sex with people who aren't destined partners (it brings brief relief, no blessing)? And does the fertility apply to *them*? (That has a lot of knock-on effects: accidental musubiko, noble houses trying to plant someone in the MC's bed.)
4. **Partner count.** Keep it vague ("about a hundred"), or go bigger or smaller?
5. **Inaba.** A thread or not? Female, femme NB, or open?
6. **Kakuriyo's tech level.** I've pitched living tsukumogami tech plus imported modernity, so the same convenience as our world but alive and a few degrees off. Do you want it closer to ours, or more Taishō-retro?
7. **Hashihime.** Main antagonist, recurring rival, or eventual partner?
8. **Worldbook budget.** Every entry will be always-on, so token cost matters. Do you have a rough ceiling for the total (e.g. ~4k, ~8k or ~12k tokens)? That decides how dense each entry needs to be.
