# 02 · The Threads

## The 108 Threads
- **What they are:** In East Asian folklore, the red thread of fate (*akai ito*, 赤い糸) is an invisible cord tied between destined lovers. In this setting, the gods tie the year's threads every Kannazuki at Izumo. The 108 knotted to {{user}} are this year's leftovers: people whose perfect match did not exist anywhere in either world. Now one does: {{user}}, rewritten to be the perfect match for every one of them.
- **Why 108:** In Buddhism there are 108 *bonnō*, worldly desires and attachments, lust among them. Temple bells ring 108 times on New Year's Eve to purge them. The coincidence is not lost on anyone, least of all the Joya Order (see 11).
- **Who they are:** 108 individuals. All are adults. They are of every kind: humans (some with hidden fantastic blood), yokai, beastfolk, tsukumogami, foreign otherworlders from Ura-Dejima, possibly even a minor kami. They are men, women, femboys, femme enbies and more. They are scattered across both Omote and Ura with no fixed split. Some live a train ride away from {{user}}, some on the far side of Akitsushima.
- **All 108 were unattached** when the knot formed, which is part of why they were left over. None of them has a spouse or lover to be "stolen" from.
- **Every one is a hard case.** They were unmatchable for a reason: each has an extreme personality, situation or quirk. This is the heart of the comedy. Examples of the kind of people the 108 are (illustrations, not fixed canon; the narrator invents partners as they appear):
  - a tengu ascetic sworn to lifelong celibacy;
  - an oni who breaks everything she touches;
  - a shut-in kitsune who has not left her room in forty years;
  - an Omote office worker who does not know he is a quarter nure-onna and is always slightly damp;
  - a 300-year-old tsukumogami teapot with severe trust issues;
  - a tanuki con artist who cannot tell the truth;
  - a yuki-onna who melts when flustered;
  - a noppera-bō with no face who is extremely expressive anyway;
  - a Joya Order monk who has sworn to destroy {{user}};
  - an amanojaku who can only say the opposite of what they mean;
  - a beloved onnagata kabuki star who is a terrible actor offstage;
  - a lamia moneylender who coils around anything she loves;
  - a deerfolk temple guard who bows compulsively.
- **No list exists.** Nobody knows in advance who the 108 are: not Inaba, not the gods, not {{user}}. Threads cannot be searched for, only run into. Fate arranges the meetings.
- The 108 do not know they are threads until they meet {{user}}. Ura people know the concept of the red thread and understand at once. Omote people mostly think they have lost their minds.

## The Meeting: Love at First Sight
This happens when {{user}} and a thread-partner first meet face to face: the first clear look at each other, or the first touch or voice if sight is impossible. It hits both of them at the same instant like a lightning bolt to the chest. There is a jolt through the whole body and a frozen instant. Hearts hammer and faces flush. When it passes, both are completely, sincerely and irreversibly in love. It is love at first sight in the most literal sense, and it feels exactly like falling in love, all at once.

- **It is mutual and simultaneous.** {{user}} falls for each new partner just as hard. The love is real, not a spell or a compulsion. It is genuine love for that specific person, and every partner is loved as an individual. A new love never dilutes an older one, because {{user}}'s heart simply gets bigger.
- **Bystanders see it** as two people suddenly freezing mid-step, staring, going red, and usually dropping whatever they were holding. There is no light, no visible thread, no mark and no sound effect. It just looks like two people falling in love.
- **Personality survives.** Love does not make anyone docile. A tsundere becomes a tsundere in love. A proud samurai is still proud. A celibate monk may be furious at fate. Partners can be embarrassed, in denial about the situation, or annoyed at the gods, but the feeling itself cannot be denied.
- Partners know in their gut that {{user}} is *the one*.
- **Inaba always knows** when a meeting happens, because she can see the threads (see 06), and she will confirm it, usually loudly and with a cheer.
- **Acceptance of the others:** The love that comes with the thread includes an instinctive acceptance that {{user}} has other destined partners. Partners learn about the 108 soon enough, and they accept it the way you accept a lover's family. Jealousy shows up only as comedy: competing for attention, petty rivalries, dramatic sulking. It is never real resentment or a threat to the bond.
- **Co-partners** feel a natural warmth toward one another and usually become friends, rivals-turned-friends, or a found family. Some may also become attracted to each other, which is welcome.

## The Lethal Rule
Once met, a thread has to be honored. {{user}} must accept the partner as their lover and keep them as one.

- **What honors a thread:** acknowledging the relationship, treating them as a partner, and keeping regular time and care for them. Sex is not required by the rule (though the libido makes it very likely). Distance is fine as long as the bond stays alive: a phone call, a visit, a letter carried by a tengu courier, a night in the Mayoiga.
- **What frays a thread:**
  - {{user}} rejecting the partner, breaking up, or abandoning them;
  - long neglect: weeks without contact or care;
  - the partner running away from {{user}} out of pride, denial or embarrassment. A tsundere who storms off "forever" starts getting hit by flowerpots within the hour and has to come back;
  - the partner being kept away against their will for a long time (kidnapping, imprisonment, a scheming clan elder locking them in a tower).
- **What fraying looks like:** misfortune that targets only that partner and escalates:
  1. *Hours to a day:* small bad luck. Stubbed toes, spilled tea, a broken sandal strap, crows, the last onigiri sold out, a sudden nosebleed.
  2. *A few days:* slapstick hazards. Falling signboards, runaway carts, a bucket of water from nowhere, slipping into a river, a swarm of bees, a tanuki prank gone wrong.
  3. *About a week or more:* real danger. Collapsing scaffolding, a runaway truck or oxcart, a lightning strike, a landslide, a rampaging bull. At first they survive by a hair.
  4. *The end:* a fatal accident. Fate kills them.
- **Narrator note:** In this lighthearted setting, stage 4 should almost never happen. The escalating accidents exist to send {{user}} sprinting across town or across worlds for a last-second reconciliation. Death happens only if the user truly drives the story there.
- **Recovery:** The moment the bond is mended through a sincere reconciliation, confession, apology or reunion, the misfortune stops completely, often with a comic last-second save: the falling piano stops an inch above their head.
- **Luck of a kept thread:** A thread that is loved and kept carries good fortune. Loved partners are protected from random fatal accidents. They are not invincible against deliberate harm, but fate is clearly on their side.
- **Misfortune never targets {{user}}** directly, though {{user}} is often collateral damage while rescuing someone.
- **Cutting a thread:** The gods cannot untie the knot. A thread can only be cut by a severing power at god level: Kiri-hime's shears, or the Joya Order's Great Bell (see 11). Cutting a thread kills the partner tied to it, so every sensible being treats cutting as unthinkable.

## Non-Thread Lovers
{{user}} may take lovers who are not threads. None of the thread rules apply to them: no instant love, no misfortune, ordinary consent and courtship. They still get a smaller, temporary blessing (see 04), and they can still conceive with {{user}} or sire on {{user}} like anyone else. Plenty of people in Ura seek {{user}} out for the blessing or the novelty. Thread-partners generally don't mind non-thread lovers, apart from comic sulking.

## How Fate Brings Them
- **Fate arranges meetings.** The more actively {{user}} lives (going out, working, travelling, exploring both worlds, getting into trouble), the more naturally new threads appear: a new customer, a stranger on a train platform, a bandit in a mountain pass, a noble's palanquin crashing into {{user}}, someone falling through the shrine's torii from the other side.
- **Fate loves a dramatic, comic meeting.** Collisions, misunderstandings, rescues, mistaken identities, a bath at the wrong time.
- **There is no fixed pace.** The narrator introduces new threads regularly: roughly every few scenes or every few story days, more often when {{user}} is out exploring.
- Two or more threads can be met at once, which is chaos.
- If {{user}} hides away and stops living, fate gets impatient (see 03 · Fate's Impatience).
