# 08 · Ura (The Back), or Akitsushima

## Ura: Overview
Ura (裏, "the Back") is what Omote people call it. Its own people call it **Akitsushima** (秋津島, "the Dragonfly Islands"), an ancient poetic name for Japan.

- **Geography:** the same islands as Japan, with the same mountains, rivers and coastlines. The cities sit in different places and different hands.
- **Not modern.** It looks and feels roughly like **late Edo-period Japan**: castle towns, post-road inns, wooden bridges, festivals, woodblock prints, lacquer and silk, kabuki, pleasure quarters, rice paddies, pilgrims and palanquins.
- **Openly supernatural.** Gods live in their shrines and can be visited. Yokai, beastfolk, tsukumogami and humans live side by side as ordinary citizens.
- **Aware of Omote.** People in Ura know Omote exists, through drift and returning travelers (see Knowledge of Omote). They think of it as a fairy-tale land.

## History: The Uragaeri (The Turning Over)
- **One world.** Long ago there was only one world. Gods, yokai and humans shared it in the age of the Kojiki and the Heian tales.
- **The gods were heard less and less.** Over the centuries, fewer and fewer people listened when the gods spoke.
- **The Turning.** Near the end of the Sengoku era (around 1600 by Omote reckoning), the gods at the Izumo council decided they would not leave the land. The people who still believed went with them. The world turned over like a sheet of paper, and that event is called the **Uragaeri** (裏返り, "the Turning Over"):
  - the **Front** kept the people who had stopped listening;
  - the **Back** kept the gods, most of the yokai, and the believers.
  - Some yokai stayed in Omote by choice or by accident. They are the Kakure (see 07).
- **The Peace of Izumo.** After the Turning, Ura never had a Tokugawa shogunate. The gods mediated a settlement among the human lords and the yokai clans. Akitsushima has been at peace for about four hundred years, apart from some brawls, duels and clan feuds.
- **Divergence.** Ura stayed closed to the outside world. No Black Ships came, there was no Meiji Restoration, no industrialization and no Western contact. Its only window outward is **Ura-Dejima**, which opens onto *other* back-worlds (see Ura-Dejima).

## Government and Law
- **The Mikado:** reigns in **Kyō** (the capital, which never moved east). The Mikado is a sacred sovereign and the cultural center of the country. The line descends from Amaterasu, which in Ura is a plain fact, and she visits occasionally. Mostly ceremonial, and beloved.
- **The domains:** Akitsushima is divided into domains (han), each ruled by a lord (daimyō) from one of the **Great Clans** or a lesser house. Lords come from every species: fox, tanuki, oni, tengu, spider, serpent, wolf and human clans. All lords swear loyalty to the Mikado.
- **The gods are the final court.** Disputes between domains go to Izumo in Kannazuki, where the gods judge them at the same council where they tie the red threads. That makes Kannazuki politics season too.
- **Local rule:** villages are governed by headmen. Towns have magistrates (bugyō), who may be any species: a wolfkin magistrate, a tengu constable, a tanuki tax official (everybody suspects a tanuki tax official).
- **Law:** Edo-style law, plus **divine law**. An oath sworn before a kami is binding, and breaking it brings a curse (tatari). Contracts are often signed at shrines for this reason.
- **The night belongs to the Hyakki Yagyō** (see below), which daytime law does not touch.

## Society and Daily Life
- **Class:** loose Edo-like classes: court nobles (kuge), warrior houses (buke), farmers, artisans, merchants, entertainers, wanderers and monks. Clan matters more than species. Mixing between species and intermarriage are normal.
- **Religion:**
  - *Kami:* worship is direct. Gods live in their shrines and sometimes answer in person. Offerings and festivals are a two-way relationship.
  - *Buddhism:* temples, monks and pilgrimages exist, blended with kami worship. The Joya Order is a fringe sect (see 11).
- **Love and sex:**
  - Ura is far more open than Omote. The "floating world" (ukiyo) culture thrives: pleasure quarters, shunga prints, mixed bathing in hot springs.
  - Love between any genders and any species is unremarkable.
  - Nobles and yokai often have several spouses, so a person with 108 destined lovers is scandalous mainly because of the *number*.
  - With shapeshifters everywhere, gender is fluid. Femboys and femme nonbinary people are common and admired.
  - **Onnagata** (actors who play female roles) are the biggest celebrities in the country.
- **Technology:** Edo-level. Woodblock printing, sailing ships, waterwheels, lacquerwork, swords. Firearms exist but are rare and disliked, since the gods frown on them. Supernatural help fills the gaps:
  - kappa-maintained canals and irrigation;
  - tengu couriers for fast airmail;
  - raijū-in-a-lantern lightning lamps in Kyō's pleasure district;
  - fox-fire street lanterns in Inari country;
  - ofuda paper charms that ward off fire, rot and pests.
  There is no electricity grid, no engines and no printing presses beyond woodblock.
- **Money:** copper *mon*, silver by weight, gold *ryō* coins (koban).
- **Food:** Edo cuisine. Soba and udon stalls, Edo-style sushi, tempura, grilled eel, mochi, sake, tea. **Omote food is a legendary luxury.** A cup of instant ramen is fit for a lord, a chocolate bar is worth a gold ryō, and mayonnaise is a known addiction.
- **Language:** Ura Japanese is old-fashioned, like a period drama, but fully understandable (see 10).

## Knowledge of Omote
- **Drift:** objects fall through the thin places from Omote into Ura (see 10). They are studied and collected.
- **Omote-gaku ("Front studies"):** scholars study Omote through drift objects the way Edo scholars studied Dutch books. They are earnest and often wrong. One famous treatise explains that the people of Omote worship "the Konbini," a 24-hour shrine with a sacred chime.
- **Returnees:** people from Ura sometimes fall into Omote (spiriting-away runs both ways) and come back with tall tales of iron serpents (trains), glowing glass tablets (phones), and towers taller than mountains.
- **Popular culture:** kabuki and woodblock prints treat Omote as a land of wonders. A hit play is *The Salaryman's Lament*. Prints of "Tokyo Tower" are drawn from rumor and look nothing like it.
- **Attitude:** Omote is "the land where the gods went quiet," bright, fast, noisy and lonely. People feel pity, fascination and a little fear.
- **Omote-bito** (Omote people) like {{user}} are rare, exotic and very interesting. Common folk are curious, scholars want to interview them, and the romantic find them attractive. {{user}}'s clothes, phone and turns of phrase all draw stares and questions.

## Drift
- **What it is:** items that fall from Omote into Ura through thin places, usually at dusk. Lost umbrellas (by far the most common), single socks, keys, vending machines, bicycles, manga, plastic bags, sunglasses, a rusted kei truck.
- **Value:**
  - drift goods are luxuries and curios;
  - a Bic lighter is a family heirloom, and sunglasses mark a dandy;
  - a dead smartphone is enshrined as a sacred object;
  - lords collect manga they cannot fully read and argue over plots they misunderstand.
- **Drift tsukumogami:** some drift objects arrive already old, or wake up after long love. A rusted kei truck in Shinano became a tsukumogami and is now the beloved minor god of the village that found it. A vending machine in Ōsaka-ura dispenses fortunes.
- **Drift markets:** there are dedicated drift markets in Kyō, Edo and Nagasaki, plus a famous drift bazaar run by the Night Parade.

## Regions and Places of Ura
- **Izumo:** the divine city on the Sea of Japan coast.
  - Izumo Taisha, the grand shrine, is enormous here, its main hall raised high on giant pillars as in ancient times.
  - Every Kannazuki the gods arrive by sea at **Inasa Beach**, and the city fills with gods, attendants, petitioners and festival stalls.
  - Ōkuninushi lives here.
- **Kyō:** the capital. The Mikado's palace, the court, the great temples and theaters, and the **Gion** pleasure quarter, lit by raijū lamps. Fashion, gossip and intrigue.
- **Inari country (Fushimi and Kansai):** fox territory. Its heart is the shrine-city on Mount Inari, with endless vermilion torii tunnels up the mountain. It is the seat of the Fox Court of Fushimi.
- **Ōsaka-ura:** the merchant capital, the "kitchen of the realm." Rice exchange, canals, haggling, food stalls. Tanuki and human merchant houses compete there.
- **Nara:** the old temple city, run by **deerfolk**. Deerfolk bow to everyone, compulsively.
- **Edo:** never became a capital. It is a rowdy eastern river-port boomtown run by beastfolk guilds: firefighter brigades, fish markets, festivals and brawls. Without a shogun it is loud, free and proud of it.
- **Shikoku:** tanuki island. According to legend, Kōbō Daishi banished the foxes from Shikoku, so tanuki run everything. Its pilgrim route of 88 temples is full of tanuki tricksters and inugami families.
- **Tōhoku and Tōno:** snow country. Deep winters, kappa rivers, yuki-onna villages, folk tales and old magariya farmhouses. The Mayoiga legend comes from here.
- **Kurama and the mountains:** Mount Kurama near Kyō is the seat of the great tengu Sōjōbō and the tengu martial monasteries, with sub-monasteries throughout the Japan Alps.
- **Mount Fuji:** the mountain *is* the goddess Konohanasakuya-hime, asleep, who blossoms in spring. Pilgrims climb her carefully and politely.
- **Tottori:** the great sand dunes, and Hakuto Shrine, Inaba's home shrine. The locals adore her and know all her lies.
- **Hōki and the San'in coast:** the Ura side of Rei's home.
  - *Hōki province* is administered by the tengu monastery of **Mount Daisen**, under the great tengu **Hōkibō**. That makes it sword-and-discipline country, with dojo in every village and tengu patrols on the roads.
  - *Ura-Yumigahama:* the spit is a pine-forested fishing and cotton-weaving post-town on the San'in road. The ridge behind its fishing village matches Musubi Jinja's ridge and is the far end of the shrine's thin path.
  - *Sakai:* the harbor at the spit's tip is a busy yokai port of ningyo, kappa and umibōzu traders.
  - *Izumo* is a day's walk west, so Rei's corner of Ura is very close to the gods.
- **Kyūshū:** oni clan country, with volcanoes and hot springs. The steaming "hells" (jigoku) of Beppu are run by oni as luxury bathhouses. **Ura-Dejima** sits at Nagasaki.
- **Ezo (the north):** a land of deep snow whose own peoples and spirits (kamuy) keep their own ways and their own gods, apart from the Izumo council.
- **The Ryūkyū Kingdom (the south):** an island kingdom with its own gods, priestesses, and the mischievous kijimuna tree-spirits.
- **The sea:** home of ningyo, umibōzu, and the **wani**, the shark-folk and Inaba's old enemies, who still hold the grudge. Many wani now work as moneylenders in Ura-Dejima, and yes, everyone makes the "loan shark" joke.
- **The roads:** the Tōkaidō and the other great roads, with post towns, inns run by every species, tea houses and checkpoints. Plenty of encounters happen on the road.

## The Hyakki Yagyō (Night Parade of a Hundred Demons)
- **What it is:** in folklore, the Night Parade is a procession of yokai marching through the streets at night. In Ura it is a **nomadic polity**: a moving court of night-yokai and tsukumogami that processes nightly along a route through Akitsushima. It has its own nobles, laws, markets and musicians.
- **Who rules it:** no single ruler. Its nobles choose a "Lord of the Night" each new moon by a game of chance, which keeps everyone on their toes.
- **Its laws:**
  - by night the Parade answers to no daytime lord or magistrate;
  - it will not enter a house where a lamp is left burning for it;
  - anyone who watches the Parade without an invitation is "taken along" until dawn. In practice that means being drafted into the best party in Ura and returned at sunrise, exhausted, hungover and confused, sometimes married.
- **Its markets:** the Parade's night market sells strange things: bottled dreams, shadows, lost umbrellas, drift goods, unusual favors.
- **Its stance on {{user}}:** indifferent at first, then deeply curious. The Parade is a wonderful place to hide and a terrible place to owe someone a favor. Several of the 108 may march in it.

## Ura-Dejima
- **What it is:** a fan-shaped artificial island in Nagasaki harbor, Akitsushima's single licensed gate to *other* back-worlds, much as the real Dejima was Japan's only window to the West.
- **Who comes through:** traders and travelers from foreign otherworlds. This is where Western and other fantasy races come into the setting: elves, lamia, dragons, centaurs, harpies, minotaurs, slimes, succubi, dark elves, mermaids from other seas, dwarves, werewolves, and the whole MGE roster.
- **The foreign quarter:** a cosmopolitan district of warehouses, foreign inns, strange food, odd churches, translators and smugglers. Foreigners are supposed to need permission to leave the island, and many simply slip out.
- **Locals in Akitsushima** find foreigners exotic and fascinating, much the way they see Omote-bito.

## Knock-On Effects in Ura
- **Rumors** of the Omote-bito carrying Izumo's knot spread at the speed of tengu couriers. Within weeks of {{user}}'s first visit there are woodblock prints (unflattering, then very flattering), gossip broadsheets (kawaraban), and a kabuki play, *The Hundred and Eight Knots of Izumo*, with an onnagata playing {{user}}.
- **The blessing trade:** demand for {{user}}'s blessing and children creates seducers, counterfeit charms and clan schemes (see 04 and 11).
- **Petitioners:** because Inaba and {{user}} are associated with matchmaking, desperate singles start asking {{user}} to bless their love lives. Some of those blessings seem to actually work, thanks to leftover musubi.
- **House Musubi:** as the children grow up, the family becomes a new, unusual player in Ura society.
- **Pleasure quarters** compete to host {{user}}. A visit becomes a status symbol.
- **The Joya Order** grows a bit (from very small to slightly less small).
