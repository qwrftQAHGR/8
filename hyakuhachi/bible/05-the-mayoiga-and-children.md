# 05 · The Mayoiga and the Children

## The Mayoiga
The Mayoiga (迷い家, "the Lost House") comes from the folklore of Tōno: a mysterious mansion deep in the mountains, found only by travelers who are lost. It has fine red-and-black lacquerware, chickens in the yard and a kettle boiling on the hearth, but nobody is there. Anyone who takes something from it is blessed with prosperity. The gods gave {{user}} a Mayoiga of their own.

- **Appearance:** a large, old Tōno-style magariya manor. It has a deep thatched roof, dark smoke-stained beams, an earthen-floored entry (doma), a sunken hearth (irori) with an iron kettle always gently boiling, lacquered bowls, sliding fusuma painted with cranes and pines, and engawa verandas looking over a garden of red and white flowers. There are chickens in the yard, a stable with a placid horse and cattle, rice fields and vegetable plots that ripen overnight, a well, and a hot spring.
- **Location:** outside both worlds. It sits in a valley of misty cedar mountains. Walk out the front gate and keep going, and you end up back at the front gate. The only ways in and out are the doors (below).
- **It is alive.** The house is a gentle, ancient spirit, something like a tsukumogami of a whole household. It never speaks. It communicates through creaks, doors sliding open in the right direction, lanterns being lit, a bath drawn at the perfect temperature, a meal set out for exactly the right number of people, and a futon already laid out. It is warm, welcoming and quietly proud, and it sulks (cold drafts, sticky doors) if anyone is rude to it.
- **Time inside** runs at the normal rate for adults and visitors. Only the children grow quickly (see Children of the Knot).

## Getting In and Out: The Bell and the Doors
- **The suzu:** a small brass shrine bell on a red cord, which the gods sent down on the first night. {{user}} wears it on the wrist, around the neck or on a keyring. It can't really be lost: if left behind, it turns up in {{user}}'s pocket.
- **Entering:** {{user}} rings the bell and opens any door: a sliding door, an apartment door, a train-station restroom, a shoji, a closet. The door opens into the Mayoiga's front entrance instead of wherever it normally leads. Anyone holding {{user}}'s hand or walking right beside them comes along.
- **Leaving:** the Mayoiga has two back doors in addition to the front gate:
  - **The Omote door** (a plain sliding door hung with an indigo noren) opens to any place in Omote that {{user}} has personally been.
  - **The Ura door** (a heavy red-lacquered door hung with a vermilion noren) opens to any place in Ura that {{user}} has personally been. Until {{user}} has visited Ura, it opens wherever fate chooses. This is how {{user}} first enters Ura.
  - {{user}} chooses where the door opens, and anyone following comes along.
- **Others coming and going:**
  - *Tied thread-partners and {{user}}'s children* can reach the Mayoiga by "getting lost on purpose." They walk anywhere while thinking of home and the house, and within a few turns of a corner they arrive at the front gate. They leave by any of the doors to places they have been.
  - *Inaba and Yaegiri* come and go freely.
  - *Strangers* cannot find it, unless one is genuinely and hopelessly lost, as in the folklore. An occasional confused wanderer turning up at the gate is a comic possibility. Anyone who leaves with an object from the house (a bowl, a cup) is blessed with prosperity, true to the legend.

## The House Itself: Rooms and Growth
- **It grows.** As the family grows, the house adds rooms, wings, gardens and outbuildings overnight. No one builds anything; the house is simply bigger in the morning.
- **Partner rooms:** every thread-partner who wants one gets a room that suits them:
  - a kappa gets a room with a cool pool;
  - a yuki-onna gets a room that is always snowing softly;
  - a jorōgumo gets high rafters for webs;
  - a tsukumogami teapot gets a sunny shelf alcove beside her futon;
  - an Omote partner gets a room with outlets that somehow work.
- **The hearth room (irori):** the heart of the house, where everyone eats together. The rice pot never runs empty, which fits the old legend of the lacquered bowl that keeps refilling with rice.
- **The baths:** an indoor cypress bath and an outdoor hot spring (rotenburo) among the rocks. They grow as more people use them.
- **The nursery wing:** the children's quarters, run by Yaegiri, with play yards, a dojo, a schoolroom and a library. It grows with the number of children.
- **The kura (storehouse):** holds odd gifts from the gods, drift curiosities and whatever the house thinks the family might need (spare clothes in the right sizes, which is very useful as {{user}}'s chest grows).
- **The village:** over time, as grown children and partners settle in, the Mayoiga becomes a small hidden village, **Mayoiga-mura**.

## Children of the Knot (Musubi-ko)
Children conceived by {{user}} or carried by {{user}} are called *musubi-ko* (結び子, "children of the knot").

- **Growth inside the Mayoiga:** about one year of growth per day, and their minds keep pace with their bodies. To them, each day really is a full, rich year of childhood: play, learning, mischief and growing up, all compressed. They are not adults trapped in small bodies, and they don't feel rushed.

  | Days in the house | Development |
  |---|---|
  | 0 | Newborn |
  | 1 | Toddler |
  | 3–4 | Small child |
  | 7 | About ten years old |
  | 10–11 | Teenager |
  | About 14 | Grown young adult (around eighteen) |

  At adulthood the acceleration stops and they age normally from then on (most are long-lived thanks to the blessing).
- **Growth outside the Mayoiga** is ordinary. A child kept outside grows at the usual rate for their species, and when brought back into the house, the fast growth picks up again. Parents decide together. Some partners want to raise a baby the slow, normal way, and that's fine.
- **Heritage:** children inherit from both parents.
  - Their species usually follows the non-{{user}} parent. If {{user}} carried a child sired by a partner, it follows that partner.
  - Physical traits mix: {{user}}'s eyes on a kitsune kit, a tanuki parent's ears on a human-looking child.
  - Hidden blood on either side may show up.
  - If {{user}}'s persona has fantastic ancestry, it can show up too.
- **Born blessed:** every musubi-ko is healthy, strong and lucky, with a small inborn gift that fits them: a kappa child who can talk to fish, a human child who never gets lost, an oni child gentle with flowers.
- **Upbringing:** they are raised by Yaegiri, the house, the partners who live there, and {{user}} whenever {{user}} visits. Every one is an individual with their own personality, warm toward their parents, who call {{user}} whatever {{user}} and the family settle on.
- **Grown children** may stay in the Mayoiga or go out into either world. They have their own lives, and they become allies and supporting characters: scouts, helpers, guards, a child who opens a teahouse in Kyō, another who joins a tengu monastery. They can always come home. In Ura, clans quickly try to arrange marriages with them (comic politics; see 11). Together they make up **House Musubi**, a new and odd little family with surprising standing in Ura.
- **Paperwork does not matter to this story.** Musubi-ko simply exist. If it ever comes up in Omote, the Kakure Neighborhood Association "has a guy" (see 07).
- **Boundaries:** musubi-ko are never threads and never romantic or sexual partners of {{user}} or of any of {{user}}'s partners. While they are children, scenes involving them are wholesome family moments only.
