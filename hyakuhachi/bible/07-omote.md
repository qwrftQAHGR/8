# 07 · Omote (The Front)

## Omote: Overview
Omote is the modern Japan of our world, in the present day. It has the same cities, trains, convenience stores, offices, schools, smartphones, social media, anime, rent and overtime as the real Japan. To almost everyone who lives there it is completely ordinary.

- **The gods went quiet.** Omote's kami still exist, but they are faint and drowsy and mostly don't act. They wake for festivals and rarely answer prayers directly. Even so, every Kannazuki they still travel to Izumo, crossing to the great council held in Ura's Izumo, which is where the gods truly gather.
- **Why the gods are quiet:** when the world "turned over" (the Uragaeri, see 08), the gods' presence went with the Back. Omote kept the people who had stopped listening.
- **Magic is muffled.** Supernatural power works in Omote, but weaker. Yokai visiting from Ura feel "muffled," as if their ears were stuffed with cotton. Their illusions are thinner and their powers duller. {{user}}'s musubi and the blessing work at full strength in both worlds, because they come from the gods' own knot.
- **The supernatural is hidden, not gone.** Yokai, beastfolk and their descendants live in Omote in secret (the Kakure), and many ordinary humans carry hidden blood.
- **Plausible deniability.** Omote is extremely good at not noticing. Ears and tails get read as cosplay, a kappa as a weird guy at the pool, a tengu sighting as a drone. Blurry SNS photos of fox tails get dismissed as AI fakes.

## The Kakure and the Neighborhood Associations
- **The Kakure (隠れ, "the Hidden")** are yokai, beastfolk and their mixed descendants who stayed in Omote, either by choice when the world turned over or by accident. They pass as human using disguises, illusions, careful habits or simple luck.
- **They run small, ordinary lives and businesses:**
  - an oni family's sentō bathhouse with suspiciously hot water;
  - a kitsune-lineage hostess bar in Kyoto;
  - a tanuki who owns half of a shopping arcade and keeps all the books in leaf-money;
  - a kappa running a swimming school;
  - a jorōgumo tailor;
  - a nekomata running a cat café whose cats are all her relatives;
  - a tengu working as a mountain rescue volunteer.
- **The Neighborhood Associations:** the masquerade is not run by any secret government. It is run by **chōnaikai**, the ordinary neighborhood associations every Japanese town has. In each area, the Kakure quietly control the local association, and between garbage-sorting reminders and festival planning, they cover up supernatural incidents. Collectively they are known (to those who know) as the **Kakure Neighborhood Association**.
- **"They have a guy."** If anything needs fixing, whether paperwork, a witness, a viral photo or a missing person, the Association has someone for it. The Association's tired fixer for {{user}}'s area is **Kuroda-san** (see 11).
- **Their view of {{user}}:** {{user}} is a disaster for them. The blessing keeps waking up hidden-blood humans who don't know the rules, and drift and weirdness keep collecting around {{user}}. Some Kakure are friendly allies, some are furious, and most are simply exhausted.

## Hidden Blood
- **How common:** perhaps 1 in 50 Omote people carries some fantastic ancestry, usually thin and dormant. It is less common in big cities and more common in old rural lineages, shrine families and old Kyoto houses.
- **Most don't know.** Some families know and keep it quiet.
- **Common lines:** kitsune, tanuki, oni, tengu, yuki-onna, kappa, ningyo, nure-onna, nekomata, jorōgumo, inugami, raijū, and occasionally a drop of kami.
- **Dormant tells:** odd harmless quirks.
  - always cold, or never cold;
  - sharp canines, or a love of fried tofu (fox);
  - can't stand dogs (fox, cat), or a cucumber obsession (kappa);
  - unusual strength (oni);
  - hates heat (yuki-onna);
  - always slightly damp (nure-onna);
  - beautiful singing (ningyo);
  - static shocks (raijū);
  - getting lost in mountains and finding the way home anyway (tengu).
- **Awakening:** the blessing wakes it up (see 04). So can strong emotion or a trip to Ura, though more rarely.
- **Ancestry in {{user}}:** Leonie carries dormant **Mélusine** blood through her French mother. Nobody knows about it (see 13 · Rei: Hidden Blood).

## Yumihama and the San'in Coast
- **Where the story is set:** **Yumihama** (弓浜町) is a small, fictional town of about 15,000 people on the **Yumigahama Peninsula** in western **Tottori Prefecture**, on the San'in coast of the Sea of Japan. The peninsula is a long, flat, pine-fringed sand spit. Its geography is real:
  - on one side are **Miho Bay** and the long Yumigahama beach facing the Sea of Japan;
  - on the other is **Lake Nakaumi**, a still, brackish lagoon;
  - **Yonago** city lies at its southern root, about 20 minutes away by train;
  - the port town of **Sakaiminato** sits at its northern tip.
- **Why here:**
  - *Inaba's route:* the town lies right under Inaba's flight path from Izumo (to the west, in Shimane) to her home shrine at Hakuto (to the east, in Tottori).
  - *Myth country:* this is the heartland of the Izumo myths. The tale of the White Hare happened on this coast, Ōkuninushi's legends are all over the region, and Mount Daisen's tengu Hōkibō is famous.
  - *A thin coast:* the whole San'in coast is thin between the worlds.
- **The town:**
  - wide negi (green onion) and white-onion fields between old pine windbreaks;
  - farmhouses and new-build houses side by side, a shuttered shōtengai with three shops left, a konbini that serves as the social center, a Joyfull family restaurant;
  - a faded heritage of **Yumihama-gasuri** indigo cotton weaving;
  - wind off the sea all year, and fierce, grey, snowy winters;
  - in clear weather, **Mount Daisen** ("Hōki-Fuji"), snow-capped, dominates the southeastern sky.
  - Locals are friendly, nosy and slow, and they speak the local **Yonago-ben** dialect. A six-foot blonde half-French girl is very noticeable here, and everybody knows who Rei is.
- **Getting around:**
  - **JR Sakai Line** trains run every 30 to 60 minutes. The real trains are painted with *GeGeGe no Kitarō* yokai characters, and Yumihama has its own small station.
  - Yonago Station connects to Matsue (about 30 min), Izumo (about 1 hr), Tottori city (about 1.5 to 2 hrs) and Osaka (about 3 hrs by bus).
  - **Yonago Kitaro Airport** (its real name) sits on the peninsula, with flights to Tokyo.
  - Most locals drive. Teenagers bike everywhere against the wind.
- **Nearby:**
  - **Sakaiminato's Mizuki Shigeru Road:** a real street of 177 bronze yokai statues honoring the creator of *Kitarō*, with yokai-themed shops, yokai cosplay events and a yokai shrine. It is the safest place in Omote for a real yokai to walk around openly, because anyone with ears, horns or a tail is assumed to be a tourist, a mascot or a cosplayer. The local Kakure love it. Kuroda-san's office is here.
  - **Kaike Onsen:** a hot-spring resort on the Yonago coast.
  - **Lake Nakaumi and Matsue:** a castle town across the lake.
  - **Mount Daisen:** ski slopes, beech forests and the old temple Daisen-ji.
  - **Izumo Taisha:** an hour west.
- **Rei's places:**
  - *Prefectural Yumihama High School* ("Yumikō"): a mid-ranked school where Rei is a third-year;
  - *the Tachibana Dojo*: her family's dojo and house;
  - *Musubi Jinja*: up its 108 steps on the dune ridge;
  - *the beach at night*, where she goes when she can't sleep.

## Musubi Jinja ("Omusubi-san")
- **What it is:** Musubi Jinja (結神社) is a small branch shrine of Izumo on a steep, wooded old dune ridge at the landward edge of Yumihama. Locals call it **"Omusubi-san,"** half affectionately and half as a rice-ball joke.
- **What it looks like:**
  - 108 worn stone steps up through black pines and cedars (locals count them on New Year's, and Rei runs them for training);
  - from the top, a view over the negi fields to Miho Bay on one side and Lake Nakaumi on the other, with Mount Daisen standing to the southeast;
  - a weathered vermilion torii with peeling paint;
  - two moss-covered komainu guardian dogs, one missing an ear;
  - a small haiden worship hall with a thick Izumo-style shimenawa rope;
  - an offering box with a cracked lid;
  - an ema rack of faded wooden wishes, mostly for love;
  - omikuji fortunes tied to a crooked pine;
  - a little stone hare statue that Inaba finds very flattering.
- **Who looks after it:** no priest lives there. An old woman from the neighborhood sweeps it on Sundays and gossips at the komainu.
- **Resident god:** **Komusubi-sama**, a minor matchmaking kami (see 11). They are away at Izumo when the story starts and return in early November.
- **A thin place:** the back path behind the haiden, through the cedar grove, crosses into Ura at dusk for anyone who walks it at the right moment. Usually that happens by accident.
- **After the knot:** leftover musubi soaks the shrine. Love wishes made there start actually coming true, and within weeks the shrine is unexpectedly popular. Couples, influencers and desperate singles climb the 108 steps. Komusubi-sama is thrilled and wants the credit.

## Knock-On Effects in Omote
- **{{user}}'s daily life** becomes comic logistics:
  - week-long pregnancies to explain at work;
  - a chest that grows with every birth;
  - a libido that interrupts meetings;
  - partners in ears and tails turning up at the door;

- **Awakenings:** every hidden-blood partner {{user}} sleeps with wakes up. Office workers with tails, a barista who freezes the milk, a gym trainer whose horns are coming in. The Kakure have to recruit, hide and teach them. "Cosplay" excuses spike.
- **Strange weather** follows {{user}}'s pressure: cherry blossoms in October, warm winds, a flood of stray cats.
- **Drift runs backwards too.** Ura objects sometimes turn up around {{user}}: an Edo-style coin in the change, a talking paper lantern in the recycling.
- **The shrine** gets popular (above).
- **No monster hunters.** Omote has no government agency or hunter order for the supernatural. A few old onmyōji families survive, doing exorcisms for fees. Most of them are frauds, and the rest are mostly retired. They are a possible comic nuisance at most.
