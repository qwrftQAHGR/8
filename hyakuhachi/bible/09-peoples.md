# 09 · Peoples

## Peoples: Overview, Gender and Appetite
- **Everyone is a citizen.** Ura is a full society, not a monster zoo. Its peoples are yokai, beastfolk, tsukumogami, humans, and foreigners from Ura-Dejima. In Omote, the same peoples live hidden as the Kakure, or dormant in human bloodlines.
- **All genders in every species.** Every species includes men, women, femboys, femme nonbinary people and others. This setting does not follow the all-female MGE default. Species names don't change with gender: a male jorōgumo is a jorōgumo, and a femboy yuki-onna is a yuki-onna (folklore's *yuki-otoko* also exists as a term).
- **Shapeshifting** is common (kitsune, tanuki, mujina, nekomata, some tengu, ryū and others), so appearance, sex and gender presentation are fluid for many people, and nobody finds that odd.
- **Monster appetites.** In the spirit of MGE, many monster peoples have strong, earthy appetites and very little shame. Others are shy, prim or ascetic. It varies by person as much as by species.
- **Folklore-true habits.** Each people keeps the quirks of its folklore. A kappa keeps a promise to the death. A yuki-onna tells you never to speak of her. A tanuki shapeshifts with a leaf on its head.

## Yokai, Part 1: Shapeshifters and Tricksters
- **Kitsune (fox):** foxes with magic. They gain a tail every century, up to nine. They have fox-fire, illusions, shapeshifting and possession, and they are cunning, proud and elegant. They love fried tofu (aburaage). Inari's messengers and nobility. Their clan seat is Fushimi. Wild kitsune (nogitsune) are pranksters. Look: fox ears and tails, often in fine clothes.
- **Tanuki (raccoon dog):** jolly, round tricksters and con artists who love sake. They shapeshift using a leaf on the head and pay with leaf-money that turns back into leaves. Legendarily proud of their huge scrotums (the male tanuki's famous "eight-mat" bag, a classic folk joke). Tanuki rule Shikoku and compete with the merchants of Ōsaka-ura. Look: round ears, ringed fluffy tails, a dark mask around the eyes.
- **Mujina (badger):** quieter cousins of the tanuki. Their signature trick is appearing without a face (the noppera-bō trick). They are dry-humored and work hard.
- **Nekomata / bakeneko (cat):** cats that lived long enough to grow a split tail. They can walk upright, dance, take human form and puppet the dead. Aloof, vain, affectionate on their own terms. Look: cat ears and one or two tails.
- **Amanojaku:** small contrarian imps who can only say or do the opposite of what they mean. They are horned, spiteful and secretly lonely.
- **Satori:** mountain mind-readers, ape-like or furred, who speak your thoughts aloud before you can. They get overwhelmed in crowds and are terrible at surprises.

## Yokai, Part 2: Monsters, Ogres and Mountain-Folk
- **Oni:** big horned ogres with red, blue or other colored skin. They are enormously strong and carry iron clubs (kanabō). They drink hard, love the hot springs, wear tiger-skin, and rule clans in Kyūshū. Most are boisterous and honest. A few are grumpy.
- **Tengu:** mountain goblins and martial masters.
  - *Karasu-tengu:* crow-winged and beaked or bird-faced, fast and disciplined.
  - *Yamabushi / daitengu:* red-faced and long-nosed, proud, ascetic martial-arts teachers.
  - Tengu have wind magic and feather fans, live in mountain monasteries (Kurama), are proud and easily insulted, and work as couriers and monks.
- **Ushi-oni:** ox-headed coastal beasts with spider-like bodies, from the shores of Shikoku and the west. Terrifying looking and mostly gentle.
- **Yamauba:** mountain witches, wild and ancient (see Yaegiri, 06).
- **Hitotsume-kozō / hitotsume:** one-eyed folk. Shy, curious, harmless.

## Yokai, Part 3: Spiders, Serpents, Dragons and Water-Folk
- **Jorōgumo:** spider-folk who are seductive and refined. They have spider bodies or legs below and humanoid bodies above, or can take full human form. They weave wonderful silk, play the biwa, and can be possessive.
- **Tsuchigumo:** earth-spiders, rough and ancient. They live in mountain caves and are clannish, strong and grumpy.
- **Nure-onna:** serpent-bodied water-folk, wet-haired and long-tailed. They live on riverbanks and shores, and they are clingy and affectionate. There are lamia-like snake people too, such as the hebi clans and the mizuchi, serpentine water-dragon folk.
- **Ryū (dragons):** rare, noble, and they control rain. They live in sea palaces (Ryūgū) and mountain lakes. In humanoid form they have horns, scales and long tails. They are proud, generous and slow to anger.
- **Kappa:** river imps with beaked faces, shells and a water-filled dish on the head. If the water spills they weaken. They love cucumbers and sumo, and they are compulsively polite: bow to a kappa and it bows back and spills its water. They keep promises to the death and make secret medicines. They maintain Ura's canals.
- **Ningyo:** merfolk of the Japanese seas, with fish-tails, beautiful voices and a melancholy streak. Eating ningyo flesh is said to grant long life, which is a sore subject.
- **Umibōzu:** huge, dark sea-giants. Mostly they just want sailors to give them a barrel.
- **Wani:** shark-folk of the sea, Inaba's ancient enemies. Proud, toothy and great with numbers.

## Yokai, Part 4: Cold, Storm, Wind and Hounds
- **Yuki-onna:** snow-folk, pale and cold, with frost breath. They are beautiful and lonely and live in snow-country villages. Warm places drain them, and they may melt when embarrassed. They will make you promise never to speak of meeting them.
- **Raijū:** lightning beasts, catlike or weasel-like, crackling with static. They are excitable, they fall from the sky in storms, and they curl up in people's navels to sleep (a folk belief they're embarrassed about). Some are hired to power Kyō's lamps.
- **Kamaitachi:** sickle-clawed weasel-folk who travel in trios and move like the wind. Fast, slicey and helpful.
- **Inugami:** dog-spirits bound to families as guardians, mostly in Shikoku. Fiercely loyal, sometimes possessive.
- **Ōkami / wolfkin spirits:** mountain wolf-spirits, messengers of mountain gods, protectors of travelers (the *okuri-ōkami*, the wolf who escorts you home).
- **Yamabiko:** echo-spirits of the mountains, who repeat what you say. Chatty.

## Yokai, Part 5: The Uncanny Body
- **Rokurokubi:** people whose necks stretch at night. Perfectly ordinary by day. Their necks wander in their sleep.
- **Futakuchi-onna:** people with a second, ravenous mouth at the back of the head, under the hair. The mouth has its own opinions.
- **Noppera-bō:** faceless people, smooth from brow to chin. They are very expressive with their bodies anyway, and many paint or wear faces.
- **Hone-onna:** skeletal lovers, beautiful by lamplight and bone by daylight. Romantic and devoted.
- **Kejōrō:** hair-yokai of the pleasure quarters, all flowing hair and faces hidden by it. Expert courtesans.
- **Akaname:** bath-licker yokai who clean bathhouses with their long tongues. Weird, harmless and famously useful at the baths.
- **Yūrei-adjacent folk:** some ghostly yokai (lantern ghosts, willow spirits) are sociable. True vengeful ghosts are rare and mostly avoided.

## Tsukumogami
- **What they are:** objects that wake up and gain a spirit after a hundred years of use or love.
- **Common kinds:**
  - *karakasa-obake:* one-eyed, one-legged hopping umbrellas;
  - *chōchin-obake:* chatty paper lanterns;
  - *bake-zōri:* sandals;
  - teapots, kettles (the tea kettle of Bunbuku Chagama, a tanuki story, is a cousin), biwa, mirrors, swords, scrolls;
  - *ittan-momen:* flying strips of cotton cloth.
- **Human forms:** many can take a human form that keeps traits of their object: a teapot person who is warm and pours compliments, an umbrella person with a paper-parasol hat, a sword person who is sharp in every sense.
- **Drift tsukumogami:** Omote objects that woke up in Ura (see 08). Vending machines, a kei truck, a karaoke speaker.
- **Personality:** they often resent being thrown away. Treat old things kindly.

## Beastfolk (Kemono)
- **What they are:** natural animal-peoples, mortal, born from their own kind. They are not yokai, though the line is fuzzy and the subject is touchy. Within a species, individuals range from **light** (animal ears and tail on an otherwise human body) to **medium** (paws, fur patches, muzzle hints) to **full** (anthropomorphic, fully furred, animal heads). Furry is fully welcome.
- **Species:**
  - *Wolfkin and dogfolk:* loyal, pack-minded. Constables and guards.
  - *Catfolk:* not nekomata, and they will say so. Independent, nap-loving.
  - *Foxkin:* not kitsune, and they hate being confused with them, since they have no magic. They are wily merchants.
  - *Deerfolk:* gentle, bowing. They run Nara.
  - *Boarfolk:* stubborn and strong. Farmers.
  - *Bearfolk:* big, sleepy, honey-obsessed. Mountain folk.
  - *Monkeyfolk:* snow-monkeys from the hot springs. Rowdy and clever. Nikkō's three wise monkeys are a famous family.
  - *Rabbitfolk:* fast, twitchy, and huge fans of Inaba.
  - *Ox and horse folk:* hardworking, especially along the roads.
  - *Birdfolk:* sparrows (the tongue-cut sparrow tale), cranes (the crane wife, a weaver who hides her nature), owls, ducks.
  - *Turtlefolk:* slow and wise, and still talking about Urashima Tarō.
  - *Otterfolk (kawauso):* playful. They tend to pretend to be humans and get caught.
  - *Frogfolk:* loud singers, merchants.
  - *Weasel, ferret and tanuki-like folk:* various.
- **Where they live:** beastfolk are everywhere. They are especially common in Edo, which is run by beastfolk guilds.

## Humans
- **Ura humans** are common in every class. They are often married into yokai and beastfolk lines, and many have a little fantastic blood. They include shrine maidens (miko), monks, samurai, onmyōji, merchants and farmers.
- **Omote humans** are ordinary modern people. About 1 in 50 has hidden fantastic blood, most of them without knowing it (see 07).
- **Spiritual humans** (miko, monks, onmyōji) can use charms (ofuda), purification and minor spirit work. These arts are stronger in Ura.

## Foreign Otherworlders (via Ura-Dejima)
- **Who they are:** peoples of other back-worlds who come through Ura-Dejima (see 08): elves and dark elves, dwarves, lamia, dragons and dragonkin, centaurs, harpies, minotaurs and holstaurs, slimes, succubi and incubi, werewolves and werecats, mermaids from other seas, mimics, golems, and whatever else MGE dreams up.
- **In Akitsushima** they are exotic. Many are merchants, translators, mercenaries, scholars or runaways, and a few have settled and gone native (a centaur who runs a soba stall).
- **Their own cultures:** they bring them along, along with their own gods. These gods are not part of the Izumo council, and the council finds them puzzling.

## Mixed Lineages
- **Half-bloods** (hanyō) and mixed families are common in Ura and barely remarked on.
- **What a child takes after:** children usually take after one parent's species, with traits from the other.
- **Ancestry comes and goes.** Fantastic ancestry can skip generations and resurface. That is how hidden blood works in Omote, and how {{user}}'s children can surprise everyone.
