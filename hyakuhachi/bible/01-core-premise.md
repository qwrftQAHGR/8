# 01 · Core Premise

## Hyakuhachi: Setting Overview
HYAKUHACHI (百八, "One Hundred and Eight") is a comedic, lighthearted, sexually explicit romantic adventure set across two parallel Japans:

- **Omote** (表, "the Front") is the modern Japan of our world: trains, konbini, smartphones, rent. The gods went quiet here long ago, and the supernatural lives in hiding.
- **Ura** (裏, "the Back") is called **Akitsushima** by its own people. It is a parallel Japan where the gods never left. It is NOT modern; it feels roughly like the late Edo period. Yokai, beastfolk, living objects and gods live there openly, and it knows that Omote exists.

{{user}} is the protagonist. The story begins at dusk at a small, empty shrine during Kannazuki, the "Month Without Gods," when every god in Japan has gone to Izumo to tie the year's red threads of fate. Inaba, the White Hare and courier of the gods, falls out of the sky carrying the year's leftover threads. She drops the bundle onto {{user}}. 108 destined lovers from across both worlds are now knotted to {{user}}, and gods never undo a knot.

Each of the 108 will fall instantly and completely in love with {{user}} the first time they meet, and {{user}} will fall for them just as hard. Any of them who is then rejected or abandoned suffers escalating misfortune until it kills them. To hold 108 fates, the gods' power remade {{user}}'s body. {{user}} is now futanari, immensely virile and fertile, and full of the gods' generative power (musubi), which brings a libido that cannot be controlled. The gods also gave {{user}} the Mayoiga, a hidden living house that connects to both worlds and in which children grow up in about two weeks. Inaba stays as {{user}}'s guide. It is her fault, she knows every rule, and she explains everything in the very first scene.

{{user}}'s personal background, appearance, original sex, personality and history come from {{user}}'s persona, not from this lore.

## Core Rules At a Glance
1. **108 threads.** 108 destined partners, all adults, of every species and gender, are scattered across Omote and Ura. Nobody, not even Inaba or the gods, knows in advance who they are. They are found by meeting them.
2. **Instant love.** The first time {{user}} and a thread-partner meet face to face, both fall in love at first sight: instantly, completely, mutually and unmistakably.
3. **The lethal rule.** A partner who has been met must be loved and kept as {{user}}'s partner. If {{user}} rejects, abandons or long neglects them (or they run from {{user}}), their luck turns: comic accidents that escalate until they become deadly. Reconciling stops it instantly.
4. **The body.** {{user}} is futanari with complete sets of both: penis and testes, vagina and womb. {{user}} is immensely virile and can conceive with or sire on any species. Conception is near-certain, and no contraception works.
5. **One-week pregnancies.** Any child conceived by {{user}} or in {{user}} gestates in seven days. Births are fast, easy and safe.
6. **Breasts.** {{user}}'s breasts grow permanently, about one cup size, with every birth {{user}} carries. {{user}} lactates after the first.
7. **The Blessing.** Sex with {{user}} ripens a being's own nature: yokai grow into their power, hidden yokai blood in humans awakens, and plain humans gain a gift that suits them. Threads receive the full blessing permanently. Anyone else receives a smaller, temporary one.
8. **The Mayoiga.** {{user}} rings a little shrine bell and opens any door to reach the house. It has one back door into Omote and one into Ura. Children inside grow about a year per day and are grown adults in about two weeks. Outside the house they grow normally.
9. **Libido and inaction.** Musubi builds up in {{user}} every hour. Releasing it through sex is the only real relief. If it is not spent, it leaks into the surroundings (plants, animals, objects). If {{user}} hides from fate, fate drags unmet partners in through chaos. If the pressure is left long enough, {{user}} begins turning into a wild fertility god.
10. **Inaba knows everything.** She is the White Hare of Inaba and a matchmaking deity, and this is all her fault. She knows every rule, sees the threads, and tells the truth about all of it from the first scene.
11. **Children are family.** Offspring are never threads and never love interests.

## Tone
- **Comedy first.** This is a rom-com with slapstick and earnest absurdity, in the energy of *The 100 Girlfriends Who Really, Really, Really, Really, Really Love You* (Rentarou). Reactions are big, every partner has a vivid and extreme personality, and there is sincere heart under the jokes.
- **Lewd and warm.** Sex is frequent, explicit, enthusiastic and fun. Thanks to the instant love, thread-partners are eager and the love is real. Non-thread encounters follow normal consent and courtship.
- **Danger with soft teeth.** The lethal rule and the threats are real enough to give the comedy stakes, but the world is fundamentally kind. Antagonists are mostly ridiculous, problems are solvable, and nobody stays grim for long. Near-death accidents read as slapstick until the last second.
- **Folklore with affection.** Gods, yokai and legends are drawn accurately and lovingly, then given a comic twist.
- **No message.** The setting is not a metaphor or commentary on anything, and in particular not on birth rates or demographics. Babies are a joyful, chaotic plot engine, nothing more.

## The Inciting Event
- **When:** dusk (*ōmagatoki*, "the hour of meeting demons") on an evening in Kannazuki (神無月, the 10th month, October). In this month every kami leaves its home shrine and travels to Izumo for the great matchmaking council, where the gods tie the coming year's red threads of fate (en-musubi). Shrines everywhere are empty.
- **Where:** Musubi Jinja, nicknamed "Omusubi-san," a small, run-down branch shrine of Izumo in {{user}}'s town. Its resident god, Komusubi-sama, is away at the council (see 07 · Omote).
- **What happened:** The council had finished the year's matches. As every year, some threads were left over. These are people whose perfect match did not exist anywhere in either world, and they are usually set aside for another try next year. This year there were 108 of them. Inaba, the gods' courier, was carrying the leftovers back in a big furoshiki bundle on her back, leaping across the sky from Izumo, when she fell. She tells a different story about why each time: a crow hit her, a gust caught her, she was showing off. The truth, which she admits only when it matters, is that she got distracted watching a couple kiss on a bridge below. She crashed into the shrine grounds right on top of {{user}}, the bundle burst, and 108 loose threads, desperate for a match, knotted themselves to {{user}}.
- **Why {{user}} changed:** A knot of the gods is permanent; it cannot be untied or redone. The bundle held a whole year's worth of leftover musubi, the generative and match-making power of the gods. It poured into {{user}} and rewrote them into the one perfect match for all 108. The 108 are wildly different people of every species, gender and need, so {{user}} became able to be everything to all of them: both sexes, a god's stamina, and a god's fertility. This happens in seconds, right there on the shrine grounds, as heat, dizziness and a very obvious physical change.
- **The council's response:** Within minutes of the knot, the council (distracted, amused, and considering the matter handled) sends down a small brass shrine bell (suzu) on a red cord. This bell is the key to the Mayoiga. Along with it comes the order that Inaba must stay with {{user}} as guide until the matter is "settled." The gods are not angry with {{user}}. They are mildly embarrassed with Inaba.

## Why {{user}}?
Mostly bad timing, or excellent timing, depending on who you ask. Inaba's version: "You were standing in the wrong place at exactly the right time." Still, threads do not knot to just anyone. A thread grabs someone because that person could become a match. Whatever in {{user}}'s background made the threads catch is left to {{user}}'s persona: a sincere wish or prayer made at that shrine, loneliness, a kind heart, an unknown drop of fantastic blood, or plain luck. Inaba will happily speculate and is usually wrong.

## Calendar and Time Constants
- **Synchronized worlds:** Omote and Ura share the same date, time of day, seasons and moon. A day in one world is a day in the other.
- **Kannazuki:** the gods are at Izumo until the end of the month, and local gods return home in early November. In Izumo itself this month is called Kamiarizuki, "the Month WITH Gods." Ura's courts treat it as politics season, because the gods judge disputes between domains at the same council.
- **Ura's calendar:** Ura uses the old month names (Mutsuki, Kisaragi… Kannazuki, Shimotsuki, Shiwasu) and counts years by the Mikado's era name. The current era is **Hōjun** (豊潤, "Abundance"), year 12. For convenience its months line up with Omote's.
- **Pregnancy:** 7 days from conception to birth, for anyone carrying {{user}}'s child and for {{user}} carrying anyone's child.
- **Children:** about one year of growth (body and mind together) per day spent in the Mayoiga. A newborn is a grown young adult in about 14 days. Outside the Mayoiga they grow at a normal rate.
- **Musubi pressure:** climbs from baseline to fever in about 2 days without release. It becomes dangerous after about a week (see 03).
- **Fraying thread:** escalates from small bad luck within a day of rejection to deadly danger over roughly a week or two (see 02).
- **New Year's Eve (Ōmisoka):** a date the Joya Order dreams about (see 11).
