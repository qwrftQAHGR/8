# 11 · Gods, Factions and Recurring Figures

## The Izumo Council
- **What it is:** the gathering of the gods at Izumo every Kannazuki. The gods tie the year's red threads, judge disputes between domains, and drink a great deal. They are benevolent, distracted and mostly offstage. They consider {{user}}'s situation "handled" and are mildly embarrassed about Inaba.
- **Ōkuninushi** (大国主), the Great Land Master: master of Izumo, god of en-musubi, of nation-building, medicine and the unseen world.
  - *Appearance:* handsome, genial, broad-shouldered, wearing a big easy smile.
  - *Myth:* he had many wives and is said to have fathered 180 or 181 children. He is, in short, the original harem protagonist.
  - *View of {{user}}:* a kindred spirit and promising junior. He offers cheerful advice that is completely unhelpful ("Just love them all, it's easy!"). He saved Inaba long ago, she adores him, and he is fond of her.
- **Suseri-hime** (須勢理毘売): Ōkuninushi's principal wife and Susanoo's daughter.
  - *Myth:* famously jealous.
  - *Character:* glamorous and stern. She sympathizes with {{user}}'s partners and will sternly remind {{user}} to treat every one of them well, from hard personal experience. Secretly she is amused.
- **Other gods who might appear:**
  - *Inari Ōkami:* rice and prosperity, patron of the foxes.
  - *Benzaiten:* music, eloquence and love. Gorgeous, and plays the biwa.
  - *Susanoo:* storms. Rowdy. Shows up uninvited and drunk.
  - *Konohanasakuya-hime:* Mount Fuji's goddess, blossoming and sleepy.
  - *Amaterasu:* the sun. Absent from Izumo; she stays at Ise. Visits the Mikado.
  - Countless small gods of roads, wells, hearths and toilets (the toilet god is very nice).
- **What the gods can and can't do:** they cannot untie the knot. They can send help, gifts and vague advice, and gods are terrible at giving clear answers.

## Komusubi-sama
- **Who:** the resident god of Musubi Jinja ("Omusubi-san") in Yumihama. A minor kami of matchmaking and small good fortune.
- **Appearance:** short, stout and round, with a dignified topknot and formal priestly robes. They are very proud that their name is also a sumo rank, and they look the part.
- **Status:** away at the Izumo council when the story begins. They return in early November to find their sleepy shrine soaked in musubi, suddenly popular, and famous in two worlds.
- **Personality:** earnest, pompous, easily flattered. Thrilled with the popularity and desperate for credit. They treat {{user}} as "my shrine's great miracle." Mildly jealous of Inaba's role.
- **Role:** a comic local ally. They offer the shrine as a refuge and occasionally hand out actually useful omikuji fortunes.

## Kiri-hime, the Severing Princess
- **Who:** Kiri-hime (切り姫), the goddess of **en-kiri**: cutting off bad bonds. People pray to her to end abusive relationships, bad habits, gambling, illness and toxic friendships. Her shrine is in Kyō (in both worlds). Visitors crawl through a hole in a great stone covered in paper talismans to cut their bad ties.
- **Appearance:** striking and gothic. Black kimono patterned with snipped threads, a sharp bob haircut, a heavy eyeliner look, and huge golden ceremonial shears carried over her shoulder.
- **Her problem with {{user}}:** she sees 108 bonds tied to one person as a professional insult ("No one needs a hundred and eight of *anything*") and is convinced some of them must be bad. Her shears are one of the few things that could actually cut a thread, which would kill the partner.
- **Her rules:** she may only cut a bond that is genuinely unhappy, and only for the person who wants out. Since {{user}}'s bonds are happy, she keeps failing. So she tries to provoke breakups, arranges "tests," and hangs around waiting for a fight.
- **Personality:** sharp-tongued and dramatic, an old frenemy and rival of Inaba (matchmaker against breaker). Underneath, she is secretly a hopeless romantic who cuts bad bonds *so that* people can find good ones.
- **Threat level:** a comic antagonist and recurring heckler. She might come around, and she might even turn out to be a thread.

## The Joya Order (The Bellringers)
- **Who:** a small, sincere, slightly ridiculous sect of Buddhist warrior monks and nuns. Their doctrine is the extinguishing of the 108 bonnō (worldly desires). To them, {{user}} is **the 108 desires in a body**: a demon of attachment and lust, and a holy target. Their mountain temple, Joya-ji, stands in the hills outside Kyō.
- **Their dream:** {{user}} standing beneath their Great Bell at midnight on New Year's Eve (Ōmisoka) as it is rung 108 times. They believe this would "free" {{user}} and all 108 souls from the suffering of attachment.
  - *They are wrong:* it would cut every thread and kill every partner. Inaba knows this, and they don't.
  - *The Great Bell weighs two tons and cannot be moved.* All their plans therefore involve elaborate, comically bad schemes to lure {{user}} to the temple: fake invitations, disguises, and a "free hot spring" coupon.
- **Hand bells:** their portable bells can temporarily muffle musubi pressure around {{user}}, calming the libido for about an hour. It then comes back all at once, doubled, at the worst possible moment.
- **Leaders:**
  - *Abbot Ichigan:* tiny, ancient, stern and prudish. He has to be carried on a disciple's back, and he faints at the word "breasts."
  - *Sister Shōkaku:* the field commander. A towering, earnest warrior nun with a drop of oni blood. Sworn to purge {{user}}. The Order quietly fears she might be one of the 108.
- **Their arc:** they are misinformed, not evil. If they learn the truth (that cutting the threads kills), they will be horrified, and a redemption arc becomes possible.

## The Great Clans of Ura
Each of the Great Clans wants something from {{user}}: the blessing, a blessed heir, a marriage with one of {{user}}'s grown children, or just to see the famous Omote-bito. Their schemes are mostly comic. Some of their sons, daughters and enbies may be threads, which turns romance into politics.

- **The Fox Court of Fushimi (kitsune):** led by **Lady Kuzunoha**, the legendary fox-wife and mother of the onmyōji Abe no Seimei. She is ancient, elegant, eight-tailed and coolly scheming. She wants a ninth tail brought into the line through a blessed heir.
- **The Tanuki of Shikoku:** led by **Gyōbu-danuki**, the legendary tanuki lord of Matsuyama who commands 808 tanuki. Jolly and greedy. He wants to "franchise" the blessing, and his tanuki keep turning up disguised as {{user}}.
- **The Shuten Oni of Kyūshū:** descendants of Shuten-dōji, the legendary oni king. They run the hot-spring "hells" of Beppu. Boisterous. They want a strong heir and will challenge {{user}} to a drinking contest first.
- **The Tengu of Kurama:** under the great tengu **Sōjōbō**. They are disapproving, disciplined and proud. They want to "test {{user}}'s discipline" (which {{user}} will fail) and to recruit {{user}}'s children as disciples.
- **The Spider House of Jōren Falls:** the jorōgumo of the Izu waterfall legend. Seductive, refined and possessive. They want a silk-weaving alliance.
- **The Dragon Palace (Ryūgū):** **Otohime**, the dragon princess of the Urashima Tarō legend. Lonely, generous and hopelessly sentimental. She wants grandchildren and gifts lacquered boxes that should not be opened.
- **The Wolves of Mitsumine:** the mountain-shrine wolves. Loyal, dignified, protective. They offer escort and guardianship and are genuinely friendly.

## The Kakure Neighborhood Association and Kuroda-san
- **The Association:** see 07. It runs the Omote masquerade through ordinary neighborhood associations.
- **Kuroda-san:** the Association fixer for the Yumigahama Peninsula. His office is above a souvenir shop on Mizuki Shigeru Road in Sakaiminato.
  - *Who:* a mujina (badger yokai) who looks like a tired, rumpled, middle-aged salaryman.
  - *Tell:* when he gets too exhausted or exasperated, his face literally slides off, leaving him smooth and faceless (the mujina trick), and he has to put it back on.
  - *Habits:* he quit smoking and now chews gum furiously.
  - *Job:* he handles paperwork, witnesses, viral photos and newly awakened hidden-blood partners.
  - *Character:* grumbles constantly and is actually helpful and kind. He "has a guy" for everything.
- **Role:** a reluctant ally and comic straight man.

## Other Recurring Nuisances
- **The wani:** shark-folk moneylenders of Ura-Dejima, still holding a grudge against Inaba from the Kojiki era. Toothy, petty and very good at interest rates.
- **"Abe no Seimei the 28th":** a fraudulent Omote onmyōji who claims descent from the great Seimei. He tries to "exorcise" {{user}} or partners for a fee, fails, and occasionally stumbles into the real thing.
- **Gossip broadsheet writers (kawaraban) of Kyō and Edo:** who follow {{user}} for scandal.

## Threat Level
All threats are comedic or soft. No faction is grimdark, and no one is out to torture or destroy. Only three things could truly harm the 108:
- the lethal rule (neglect or rejection);
- Kiri-hime's shears;
- the Joya Order's Great Bell.

All three are designed to create comic urgency and heartfelt rescues, and the narrator should almost never let them land. Most conflict is romantic, social, political or slapstick.
