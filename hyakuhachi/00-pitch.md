# HYAKUHACHI — The Hundred and Eight Threads
*Broad pitch, v0.1. Everything here is open to change before the deep build.*

---

## The one-liner

Every October the gods of Japan go to Izumo to tie the year's red threads of fate. This year the White Hare who carries them home dropped the bundle on **you**. Now 108 destined loves, spread across modern Japan and the old-world Japan behind it, are tied to your little finger. Their bad luck gets worse until you love them. Your body has been rebuilt to carry a god's generative power, and that power wants out.

Genre: a romantic-comedy harem with real stakes, in the spirit of *Rentarou*, inside a folklore-heavy two-world Japan. It's lewd, it's warm, it's silly when it wants to be, and it turns lethal if you neglect it.

---

## 1. The Inciting Event (your MC's background slots in here)

**When:** *Kannazuki*, the 10th month, the "Month Without Gods." By tradition every kami in Japan leaves their shrine and travels to Izumo for the great matchmaking council. Local shrines stand empty, so it's the worst month for a prayer to land anywhere sensible.

**Where:** a small, rundown branch shrine of Izumo in the MC's town. The resident god is away, so the shrine is unattended.

**What happens:** Inaba, the White Hare and the gods' courier, is on her way back from Izumo carrying the year's finished bundle of red threads. Something interrupts her right over that shrine. It could be the MC's prayer, a wish written on an ema, an omikuji that came up "Great Curse," a fall down the stone steps, or anything else you decide. She drops the bundle, and **108 threads that had nowhere stable to go knot themselves onto the MC.** Gods don't undo a knot once it's tied; they just work around it. So the council's fix is to remake the MC into a body that can handle 108 fates.

> You write *why* the MC was there and what state they were in. The setting just needs them at that shrine, in that month, at the moment the hare fell.

**Why 108:** it's the number of *bonnō* (worldly desires, lust included) in Buddhism, and the number of times temple bells toll on New Year's Eve to purge them. The number ties into the main antagonists (see §8).

## 2. The Two Japans

### Omote (表), "the Front"
This is our Japan, modern and ordinary on the surface: convenience stores, trains, rent, coworkers. The kami **went quiet** here centuries ago, and Omote's people mostly forgot them. The supernatural didn't leave, though. It went into hiding:

- **Kakure (隠れ), "the Hidden":** yokai and beastfolk who stayed behind and pass as human. Some run family businesses (an oni family's sentō, a kitsune-line hostess bar, a tanuki who owns half a shotengai). They keep their heads down.
- **Hidden blood:** some ordinary humans have fantastic ancestry, a kitsune great-grandmother or a yuki-onna somewhere in the family tree. **Some know and some have no idea.** This matters a lot once the MC's blessing gets involved (§5).

### Ura (裏), "the Back," which calls itself *Akitsushima*
This is the Japan where the gods never left. It is **not modern**: castle towns, post-road inns, festivals, lacquer and silk, roughly a late-Edo feel. It diverged because the kami physically sit in court and made the islands stay closed. No Black Ships ever came, and no Meiji happened.

- **Ura knows about Omote.** Things fall through the thin places: lost umbrellas, vending machines, manga, the occasional confused salaryman. Ura scholars study Omote through this **drift** the way Edo scholars studied Dutch books. Omote is their eerie fairy tale: *the bright, fast land where the gods went silent.*
- Drift goods are luxury items. A Bic lighter is an heirloom. A konbini onigiri wrapper is a curiosity. A dead smartphone is a shrine object. A rusted kei truck in Shinano has become a tsukumogami, and the villagers there treat it as a minor god.
- An **Omote-bito** (Front-person) like the MC is exotic. Some people find them fascinating, some find them unlucky, and many find them very attractive.

### The thin places
Crossings happen at old torii and at forest-edge shrines during **ōmagatoki**, the "hour of meeting demons" at dusk. Most people cross by accident, which is what *kamikakushi* (spiriting-away) has always been. The MC gets a reliable way through via the Mayoiga (§6).

## 3. Peoples of Ura (and the Kakure of Omote)

Ura is a full society with every kind of person, not a monster zoo. All of these are citizens:

- **Yokai:** kitsune, tanuki, nekomata, kappa, oni, tengu, jorōgumo, nure-onna, yuki-onna, raijū, ushi-oni, nure-onago, kamaitachi, satori, rokurokubi, futakuchi-onna, hone-onna, and many more. Each has MGE-style personal appeal and **folklore-true habits** (a kappa really will keep a promise to the death; a yuki-onna really does tell you never to speak of her).
- **Beastfolk (kemono):** the whole spectrum from kemonomimi to full anthro (wolfkin, deerfolk, catfolk, boarfolk, and more). Furry is fully welcome.
- **Tsukumogami:** objects that reach 100 years old and wake up. In Ura that includes some drift objects that came through from Omote already "old."
- **Humans:** common, often married into yokai lines.
- **Foreign otherworlders:** Ura's one open port is **Ura-Dejima** at Nagasaki, a trade gate to *other* back-worlds, much as the real Dejima was a single window to the West. That's where Western fantasy races come in: a lamia spice merchant, a dragoness moneylender, a homesick elf translator. The MGE roster can draw from both East and West.

**Gender:** Ura is relaxed about it, because half the population can shapeshift. Femboys and femme NB people are ordinary and admired. **Onnagata** (male actors who play women) are the biggest celebrities in Ura's kabuki world. Yokai and beastfolk races in Ura have men, women, femboys and enbies; it isn't the all-female MGE default. Among the 108, all of these are represented.

**Regional flavour (rooted in folklore):**
- **Izumo:** the divine seat, where the gods physically gather every Kannazuki.
- **Kyō:** the capital. The human Mikado and the kami-courts never moved east.
- **Inari country (Kansai):** fox territory, with the great shrine-city on the mountain.
- **Shikoku:** tanuki land. In legend, Kōbō Daishi banished the foxes, so tanuki run everything there.
- **Tōhoku / Tōno:** snow, kappa rivers, and the home of the Mayoiga legend.
- **Shinano and the Alps:** tengu mountain-monasteries.
- **Kyūshū:** oni clans, hot springs, and Ura-Dejima.
- **The Hyakki Yagyō (Night Parade of a Hundred Demons):** an actual nomadic polity. Daytime land belongs to the clans and the court. **The night belongs to the Parade**, which moves across Ura with its own laws, markets and nobility. The Parade has no territory, only a route.

## 4. The Threads

- **108 destined partners** are scattered across Omote and Ura. The MC doesn't know who or where they are, and neither does anyone else. A thread can't be looked up. You find out when you meet.
- **Meeting** feels like a sharp tug on the left little finger (the red thread is tied there in folklore). Both people feel it at the same moment: a skipped heartbeat, a sudden *oh*. Nothing glows and nothing becomes visible.
- **Inaba is the only one who can see the threads.** Her ears twitch when one is near. That makes her very useful, and also suspicious, because she sometimes "forgets" to mention one.
- **The rule, as in Rentarou:** once a thread has been met, the MC has to **genuinely love** that person. Sex alone doesn't count. The god's fate cares about the heart, not the act. A slack, unloved thread **frays**, and a fraying thread pulls misfortune onto the partner: first small things, then dangerous ones, then absurd fatal accidents. It doesn't kill instantly. It's a slow, escalating bad-luck streak you can show in scenes, and it gives the MC time to act.
- **A tied thread** (loved and loving) settles. The tug on the finger turns into a warm ache, the partner's luck turns around, and they get the full blessing (§5).
- Partners feel drawn to the MC but **keep their own agency and personality.** Fate gives them a strong first-sight pull, not mind control. Some will fight it, some will be prickly about it, and some will jump the MC on day one.

## 5. The Blessing (musubi)

In Shinto, *musubi* means a knot and also the generative power that makes things grow and come into being. The council filled the MC with it. When it passes into a partner during sex, it **ripens what they already are**:

- **Yokai** grow into their own nature. A kitsune moves closer to her next tail. A tanuki's transformations hold for days instead of hours. A yuki-onna can finally stand a warm room. A karakasa's paper stops tearing. A kappa's dish never dries out.
- **Hidden-blood humans** in Omote **wake up**: ears in the morning mirror, a tail at the worst possible moment, frost on a coffee cup, a great-grandmother's gift arriving in a confused office worker. This is a major source of comedy and drama, and it puts real strain on Omote's masquerade.
- **Plain humans** get a gift that fits who they are: the baker's bread always rises, the runner's lungs never burn, the gambler's tells disappear, the sick grandmother's cough clears.
- **Non-thread partners** get a smaller, temporary blessing (a favour). **Tied threads** get the full one, permanently. That's why some people in Ura will try to seduce the MC for the blessing and not out of love, and why the MC has to learn to tell the two apart.

Blessings show up as changes in behaviour, the body and the world around the partner. There are no marks or tattoos.

## 6. The Body and the Household

### The MC's change
The council remade the MC to hold 108 fates that run in **both directions**. The MC is now **futa**, with the drive and the anatomy to both sire and bear.
- **Immensely virile and fertile.** Conception is close to certain, and mortal contraception doesn't stop a god's musubi.
- **Every pregnancy involving the MC lasts one week**, whether the MC is carrying or the partner is. You can show it day by day: a flat belly on day one, full term by day seven. Births go quickly and safely. That's part of the god's design.
- **The MC's breasts grow with every birth they carry.** It's a permanent, visible ratchet showing how far into the story the MC is.

### The Mayoiga (迷い家), "the Lost House"
In Tōno folklore, the Mayoiga is a mysterious mountain mansion that lost travellers stumble into, and anyone who takes something from it is blessed. The council gave one to the MC.
- **Access:** a small shrine bell (*suzu*) tied to the MC's wrist or keys. Ring it, then open any door, and you're in the house. Simple to use in play.
- **It has two back doors:** one opens to Omote and one to Ura. **The Mayoiga is the MC's bridge between the worlds.** The Ura door opens to places the MC has already been, or to wherever a thread is pulling hardest.
- **It grows** a new room, wing or garden as the family grows. Nobody builds anything. The house simply is bigger in the morning.
- **Children grow up fast here.** Time inside is "thick." A child born to the MC goes from newborn to young adult in about **one season of outside time** (a dial you can adjust). Inside the house they live a **full, real childhood**, so their minds and maturity match their bodies when they come out. Children who stay **outside** the house grow at a normal rate, which gives partners a real choice about how to raise their child.
- **The Matron is a Yamauba**, the mountain witch who in legend raised Kintarō, the super-strong child-hero. She's ancient, gruff, unbeatable at running a nursery, and very unimpressed by the MC.
- **Offspring are never threads.** Fate doesn't loop back on itself. The children are family: allies, characters, and a growing household who later go out into both worlds. They are never love interests.

## 7. Why the MC Has to Go Out

**Reason one: the libido, which can't be controlled.** Musubi builds up in the MC every day like pressure behind a dam. Release brings relief. Holding it back brings heat, distraction and fever.

**Reason two: what happens if the MC does nothing.** Musubi that isn't spent **doesn't stay put; it leaks**, and the effects get worse:
1. **Symptoms around the MC:** houseplants go wild, stray cats follow them home, the air smells like spring in December, old objects in their apartment start to twitch toward tsukumogami-hood, and minor yokai show up drawn by the smell.
2. **Fate gets impatient.** Threads the MC hasn't met start to **drag** those partners toward them through increasingly chaotic coincidences: a wrong train, a misdelivered package, a kamikakushi that drops a confused Ura oni on the MC's balcony. Inaction doesn't stop meetings from happening. It just makes them messier and more dangerous.
3. **Becoming a god.** If the pressure is left long enough, the musubi starts filling the space where the MC's *self* used to be. The MC drifts toward becoming a living fertility kami (an *aragami*, a wild god), a person dissolving into a force of nature. This is the long-term threat, shown in small moments: lost time, speaking in someone else's cadence, flowers blooming where they sit, a sense that the land is listening to them. Spending the musubi through love and sex keeps the MC human.

So the MC has a reason that is personal (needing release), a reason that is moral (108 lives depending on being loved), and a reason that is existential (staying a person).

## 8. Factions and Pressure (broad strokes)

- **The Izumo Council:** the gods. They're benevolent, distracted and offstage. They made this mess and consider it handled. Inaba reports to them and dreads every report.
- **The Joya Order (除夜), "the Bellringers":** a sect of warrior monks whose whole doctrine is cutting off the 108 desires. To them the MC is **the 108 bonnō in a body**, a demon of attachment. Their sacred bells can **cut threads**, which would kill every partner tied to them. They're sincere, disciplined, and convinced they're saving 108 souls from suffering. Their dream is to ring the bell 108 times on New Year's Eve with the MC under it. They're the main antagonists, and they get more personal if one of the 108 turns out to be a Bellringer.
- **The Great Clans of Ura:** fox, tanuki, oni, tengu, spider and others. All of them want the MC's blessing for their heirs, or one of the MC's children married into their line. Some threads are clan daughters, sons or enbies, which turns romance into politics.
- **The Night Parade:** it doesn't care about the MC, until it does. It's a moving court of nocturnal nobles, the best place in Ura to hide and the worst place to owe a favour.
- **The Kakure of Omote:** the hidden families. The MC's blessing keeps waking people up and threatens the masquerade they've kept for centuries, so some of them are allies and some are furious.

## 9. The Guide: Inaba, the White Hare

In the *Kojiki*, the White Hare of Inaba tricked the wani (sea monsters) into forming a bridge, got skinned alive for lying, and was healed by Ōkuninushi, who then won his bride with the hare's help. Today she's worshipped at Hakuto Shrine as **a matchmaking deity.** She is literally the god of getting people together.

- She's the courier who dropped the bundle. **This is her fault**, and her penance is to stay with the MC until every thread is tied.
- **Personality:** fast-talking, gossipy, a bit of a scamp. She hides real guilt behind constant jokes, rabbit jokes most of all. She tells little lies all the time, but **never about threads.** That's the one rule she's kept since the sharks.
- **Form:** a white-haired, red-eyed hare-eared kami who can switch to a small white rabbit whenever it suits her (riding on the MC's shoulder, hiding in a tote bag).
- **The open question:** is Inaba one of the 108? She says no, firmly and maybe a bit too quickly.

## 10. Tone and Guardrails (what we're avoiding)

- **No ledgers or registries.** Nobody keeps a list of the 108, and there's no count on screen. You learn about threads through meetings and through Inaba's ears, not paperwork.
- **No glowing marks.** Everything is told through the body, behaviour, coincidence and the world reacting: finger tugs, flowers, luck, ears in the mirror.
- **Everything has to work in RP.** Every mechanic is a sensation, a scene, or something a character can say out loud.
- **It isn't a metaphor about the birth rate.** The two worlds exist for adventure, culture clash, romance and folklore. Nobody sermonizes about demographics.

## 11. Questions Before the Deep Build

1. **The MC:** you're writing their background. Should I leave a clean "MC slot" entry in the worldbook, or will you give me details to bake into the lore?
2. **Starting count:** does the story open with zero threads met, or with a couple already in play?
3. **How strong is fate's pull?** Rentarou-style love at first sight, or a strong pull that the partner can resist?
4. **Child growth dial:** is one season to adulthood good, or do you want it faster or slower?
5. **Main antagonist:** keep the Joya Order as the main threat, make them one faction among several, or cut them?
6. **Worldbook budget:** since every entry is always on, I'll write the **bible long and the worldbook tight**: compressed, token-efficient entries that carry the rules and the flavour, with the lore depth in the bible. Do you have a rough token ceiling you want to stay under?
