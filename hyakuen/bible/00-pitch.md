# HYAKUEN 百縁
### *A hundred bonds for a hundred yen*

**Setting pitch · v0.1 · 2026-10-16**
The broad strokes, before the deep build. All of it is open to change. Decisions I need from you are marked **(Q1)** to **(Q5)** and collected at the end.

---

## The pitch in one breath

It's Kannazuki, "the month without gods." Every kami in Japan has gone to Izumo to settle the coming year's *en*, the bonds between people. The MC climbs the steps of an empty neighborhood shrine, rings the bell, drops a hundred-yen coin in the box, and prays for a connection. The customary offering is five yen, because *go-en* also means "a bond." A bored white hare carries the prayer to Izumo. Three days into the sacred sake, someone there reads 百円 (*hyaku-en*, a hundred yen) as 百縁 (*hyaku-en*, a hundred bonds).

The gods laugh. Then, because gods never do anything halfway, they grant it.

A hundred red threads now run from the MC's little finger to a hundred people, human and otherwise, scattered across modern Japan and the hidden world behind it. Every one of them will love the MC. Every one of them will die if the MC meets them and doesn't love them back. No one could survive that many bonds as they were, so the gods also gave the MC what the god of matchmaking has: the *musubi*, the power that ties and the power that gives birth. The MC wakes up futa, ferociously virile and fertile. They own a house in a valley that isn't on any map. A smug stranger with hare ears is sitting at the foot of their bed, eating their rice crackers.

"So," says Inaba. "About the hundred yen."

## What it should feel like

- **A romantic comedy with a body count it's trying very hard to avoid.** It runs on Rentarou's engine: every meeting is a crisis and every crisis is a love story. When the stakes matter, they're played straight.
- **Earthy and sexy, not clinical.** The musubi is sap rising, rice ripening, spring showing up in October. Desire is warm and a little absurd, and nobody is ashamed of it.
- **Folklore you can stand in.** Every rule is pinned to real Japanese myth, so the setting feels old and inevitable instead of invented for the occasion.
- **Two worlds, one life.** The MC still has a train to catch on Monday. The hidden world is next door, not far away.
- **A household, not a harem roster.** Partners know each other, have opinions about each other, and raise children together. The family is the long game.

**Recurring images:** thread and knots, rice, blossoms out of season, hares and the moon, torii at twilight, five-yen coins on red string.

**Touchstones:** *The 100 Girlfriends Who Really, Really, Really, Really, Really Love You* (structure and comedy), *Spirited Away* (crossings), *Monster Musume* (living with monster girls), *Kamisama Kiss* (shrine romance), *Mushishi* (the quiet strangeness of country spirits).

## The night of the hundred yen

**The month.** Kannazuki is the tenth lunar month, "the month without gods." Izumo, the one place they're all present, calls it Kamiarizuki, "the month with gods." There, at the *Kamihakari* (the divine council), the gods decide who will be tied to whom in the coming year.

**The shrine.** It's small and local, with stone hares where the lion-dogs would usually stand. Like every other kami, its god is away at Izumo. A hare is minding the place as a favor. This is Inaba, the White Hare of Inaba from the *Kojiki*, the oldest matchmaker in Japanese myth, running late for the council as usual.

**The coin.** The MC offers a hundred yen. Five yen is *go-en*, "a bond." Ten yen is *tō-en*, "a distant bond," and people avoid it. A hundred is just showing off.

**The council.** Every loose thread of the year is on the table: everyone whose bond hasn't been tied yet. The hare sets the prayer down, somebody reads it aloud, and the pun lands.

**The gift.** A hundred bonds would kill an ordinary person, so several gods add something:
- **Kamimusubi**, the primordial goddess of generation, gives the MC the musubi. She once sent the healers who brought Ōkuninushi back from the dead.
- **Konohanasakuya-hime** adds the speed. She bore three sons after a single night.
- **Ōkuninushi** throws in a house. He is the god of matchmaking, and under the name Daikoku, the god of the kitchen and of plenty.
- The hare who started it all is told to see it through.

**The morning.** The MC wakes up changed, and Inaba is already there.

**Your slots** (you write these):
- Who the MC is and was: starting sex, age (an adult), work or university, home city.
- Why they were at the shrine, and what they actually prayed for.
- Anything in their blood they don't know about (see *Hidden blood*).

*Optional echo of the myth:* earlier that week, the MC was kind to an injured white rabbit. In the original story, Ōkuninushi was a nobody carrying his brothers' bags, and he was the only one kind to the flayed hare. Inaba always repays a debt. Inaba just doesn't do it to scale.

## The musubi

The whole setting hinges on a real Japanese double meaning. *Musubi* written **結び** means a tying, as in *en-musubi*, matchmaking. Written **産霊**, it's the Shinto word for the generative power that makes things be born and grow. It's also, wonderfully, the everyday word for a rice ball.

Japan ties wedding gifts with a knot that can't be undone, and birth gifts with a bow that's meant to be tied again and again. The MC carries both, and everything below follows from that.

### 1 · The thread

Each of the hundred threads runs from the MC's left little finger to one person. On that person's end, it's tied to their *tama-no-o* (玉の緒), "the jewel cord," the classical name for the cord that holds a soul in a body. Princess Shikishi's famous poem opens with it: *Cord of my life, if you're going to break, then break.* Every true bond in this world is tied that way, which is why heartbreak can kill in the old stories. The gods just tied a hundred of them to one person.

A thread stays slack until its two ends meet. After that:

| | When | What it feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tug** | Always, toward partners not yet met | An itch, then a pull, at the MC's little finger. It gets stronger as they get close. When the person is in Ura, it points at a place that isn't there. |
| **The Toll** | The first meeting | Both of them hear the shrine bell the MC rang that night. Nobody else does. |
| **The Settle** | When love is mutual and meant | Warmth under the breastbone, and the tug toward that person goes quiet for good. They're safe. |
| **The Fray** | Met, but not yet loved | Their luck turns: near-misses, fevers, falling flowerpots, cars that swerve too late. It escalates over a few weeks. The MC feels it as an ache in the finger. If nothing changes, the cord snaps. |

**The rules:**
- **The knot asks for love, not sex.** A confession that's meant, or a kiss given in love, settles it. What happens in bed is a different power (see *The Fever* and *The Blessing*). This keeps the two engines separate: love keeps them alive, and sex keeps the MC sane and makes things grow.
- **It can't be faked.** The thread knows.
- **It doesn't control anyone.** It makes the attraction real on both sides, but it doesn't make anyone behave. Denial is allowed. Denial is also *dangerous*: the tsundere who won't admit it is the one nearly hit by a truck. (Hello, Rentarou.)
- **Love has to hold at both ends.** A settled knot doesn't need constant attention. If the love is truly gone, though, or someone takes it, the knot loosens and the fray comes back (see *The Moon*).
- **The precedent.** The heavenly god Ninigi was offered two sisters as brides. He kept the beautiful one and sent the plain one, Iwanaga-hime, home. Because of that, mortal lives became as short as blossoms when they could have lasted like stone. It's the oldest rule there is: refuse a bride the gods offered you, and something dies.

**Knock-on effects:**
- **All of them were single.** The gods could only tie loose threads, so no marriage gets broken.
- **"A hundred" is *hyaku*, Japanese shorthand for "a great many,"** as in *hyakki yakō*, the night parade of a hundred demons. Nobody knows the exact number. Inaba claims to. Inaba also once "counted" a line of sharks to get across the sea.
- **Partners can faintly feel each other.** They're thread-kin, which makes a household possible: mostly fond, sometimes jealous. (Ōkuninushi's chief wife is the most jealous goddess in the *Kojiki*. Ask her how she managed.)
- **The MC can't choose who, only how.** No partner is negotiable. How each relationship goes is entirely up to play.
- **The thread never ties blood to blood.** The MC's children are family. They are never partners and never destined.
- **A few beings can see the threads:** Inaba, some gods, some very old foxes. To everyone else, the MC is just someone who checks their little finger a lot.
- **Keepsakes, not mechanics.** Partners tend to end up with a five-yen coin on a red cord. It starts as the MC's joke and becomes a tradition.

### 2 · The Fever (reason to go out, part one)

The musubi is generative power with exactly one opinion: things should be tied, and things should be born. In the MC, it builds like sap, regardless of what's on their calendar.

- **Simmer.** Flushed, distracted, thinking about partners at the worst possible moments.
- **Bloom.** The world around the MC starts to ripen. Buds open out of season. Fruit in the bowl turns soft and sweet overnight. Cats yowl. The couple at the next table start holding hands without knowing why. A cherry tree blossoming outside the MC's window in October gives it away, and every yokai who sees it knows exactly what it means.
- **Beacon.** The musubi's scent, like fresh-steamed rice and spring rain, carries. Hungry things come sniffing: opportunists, blessing thieves, things that live on other people's vitality. At the same time, the MC's judgment goes soft.

**What helps:** a night with a destined partner clears the Fever completely and buys about a day of calm. Anyone else, or the MC's own hand, only takes the edge off.

**Freely given, or not at all.** Musubi taken by force curdles. It brings no blessing and no child, only a curse (*tatari*) on whoever took it. The Fever pushes the MC *toward* partners, never past a no, and the same rule protects the MC from anyone who would try to take what they want.

**Knock-on effects:**
- Out-of-season blossoms show the MC's state at a glance. That's great for RP and mortifying for the MC.
- Partners feel the Fever through the thread. Some come running. Some tease.
- Long stretches away from partners are a real problem: exams, business trips, a week in Ura.
- A Bloom in the middle of ordinary life (a packed train, an office, a lecture hall) is a comedy set piece waiting to happen.

### 3 · The Pining (reason to go out, part two)

This is the consequence for inaction.

Since the night of the hundred yen, all hundred have felt it: dreams of a face they've never seen, a habit of turning toward doorways, a tune they can't place. It's sweet at first. It doesn't stay sweet.

The old word is ***koiwazurai*** (恋煩い), "love-sickness," and in Heian literature people waste away from it. Here it's literal. Partners the MC hasn't met lose their appetite, their sleep, their color, and their luck. Yokai fade: tails thin, disguises slip, powers falter. Over seasons, not days, it wastes them away.

So there's no safe option: **hide at home and they die slowly; meet them and fail to love them and they die fast.** The only way out is through.

**Knock-on effects:**
- **They come looking.** The thread pulls both ways, so many partners find the MC first: at work, at the konbini, at their door. *"You're the one from my dream."*
- **Some can't come.** They're bound to a shrine, a duty, a sickbed, a curse, the bottom of a lake. Those partners have to be found, which makes natural quests.
- **Inaba can tell who's worst off,** which turns Inaba into a quest-giver whether they like it or not.
- **Some resent the wait.** "I've been sick since October. *You took your time.*"
- **The whole cast shares one slow clock** that started that night. It creates urgency without a countdown.

### 4 · The body

- **Fully futa.** Whatever the MC started as, they now have both, and both work.
- **Virility.** Absurd stamina, appetite, and recovery. The MC never tires before their partners do.
- **Fertility.** Near-certain. Whoever's on the receiving end can conceive: the MC can carry anyone's child, and can give a child to anyone who's able to carry one. Ordinary contraception doesn't hold up against a god's gift. Latex tears and pills do nothing. The one thing that works is a severance charm from an *en-kiri* shrine, which means the faction most opposed to the MC sells the only contraception that works.
- **The scent.** Steamed rice and spring rain. In Ura the MC gets the nickname "Omusubi" (rice ball), said with affection or mockery depending on who's saying it.
- **Breasts grow with every birth the MC carries,** starting from wherever they started. Milk comes in with the first pregnancy.
- **The milk heals.** When Ōkuninushi's brothers killed him, two shell princesses brought him back with a salve the *Kojiki* calls "mother's milk." The MC's milk eases wounds, exhaustion, and fading. It can't mend a fraying thread, though. Only love does that.

**Knock-on effects:**
- The changes are real in the human world too: clothes, gym changing rooms, Japan's public-bath culture.
- In Ura, the MC's silhouette becomes recognizable on sight.
- The body keeps up with the growth (no pain, a strong back), and the growth slows over time instead of running away. You can change that (see *Dials*).

### 5 · The week

Anything conceived by or in the MC is born seven days later. This is Konohanasakuya-hime's touch. She's the goddess whose husband doubted that a pregnancy after a single night could be his.

- **The carrier knows** the same night: a warmth low in the belly.
- **Early in the week,** nothing shows. **By midweek,** the belly rounds fast. **The last days** are heavy, hungry, and spent nesting. **Day seven** is the birth.
- **Births are easy and safe.** She's the goddess of safe childbirth (*anzan*), and every birth in the MC's household is under her hand: no complications and no losses.
- **Twins and triplets happen.** She had three.
- **Yokai partners often slip into their true shape in labor, and many ask not to be watched.** When the sea princess Toyotama-hime gave birth, her husband peeked, saw a vast sea beast, and lost her forever. Looking after being asked not to wounds the bond.
- **Outside the Mayoiga,** a baby grows at a normal pace until someone brings it in.

**Knock-on effects:**
- The household grows fast.
- Ordinary observers barely register the week (see *The Kakushi*).
- A carrier can conceive again soon after a birth.
- While pregnant, the MC can still father children with others.

### 6 · The blessing (*goriyaku*)

Union with the MC passes musubi into the partner, and musubi does what it always does: it ripens things. Every partner is pushed toward the fullest version of what they already are.

- **Yokai** grow stronger and *show* it. A kitsune grows another tail, a tanuki's disguises become flawless, a tengu flies higher, a yuki-onna's frost lasts through August, an oni's horns lengthen, a snake woman sheds into a greater skin, a tsukumogami learns a new trick.
- **Humans with hidden blood** wake up: a fox's shadow, the sound of the sea in an inland city, horn buds, night sight. Sometimes this reveals an ancestry nobody knew about.
- **Ordinary humans** get a *goriyaku* that suits them, the way a shrine's blessings do. The failing shop starts to prosper, the student's mind clears, the athlete's knee heals, the shy one finds their nerve.
- **It deepens with love and with time,** and it's never counted.
- **Non-destined lovers get a taste:** a few days of luck or power that fades and leaves them wanting more.
- **Taken by force, it curdles** (see *The Fever*).

**Knock-on effects:**
- Partners change visibly over the story, and that drives plot on its own.
- As the MC's partners grow stronger, Ura's balance of power shifts. That means politics.
- Human partners in Omote have to hide new ears, new strength, new luck.
- Force curdles the blessing and Izumo protects the MC, so anyone who wants it has to *charm, bargain, or trick*. That's much better RP than a fight.

### 7 · The Mayoiga (the pocket dimension)

In Tōno folklore, the *mayoiga* (迷い家, "the lost house") is a grand house deep in the mountains that only lost people find. It has a black gate, red and white flowers, livestock in the yard, and a kettle on the boil, but nobody is home. Take something from it and you'll never want for anything again.

The gods gave the MC one.

- **It ripens anything unripe.** Ōkuninushi's old mallet hangs in the rafters: the *uchide no kozuchi*, the lucky mallet that grew Issun-bōshi to full size. Seedlings bear fruit overnight. The pantry is always full. **Children grow from newborn to adult in about three months,** a pace I'm calling Kaguya's Measure after the bamboo princess who did exactly that. After that they age normally. Adults aren't affected: the house ripens things, but it doesn't age them.
- **The red bowl.** Rice measured out with it never runs out. Carried into the human world, it means the MC never has to worry about money.
- **Obaba** is the house's *yamauba*, the mountain crone of legend who raised the boy hero Kintarō. She's enormous, wild-haired, and pipe-smoking, terrifying to intruders and perfect with children. The house's spirits help her: *zashiki-warashi* play with the kids, and *tsukumogami* (household objects come to life) do the chores.
- **Getting there:** get lost on purpose. Step through any doorway with your eyes closed, thinking of home. Anyone the MC has brought once can find their own way back.
- **Two gates.** The front gate lets you out where you came in. The back gate opens onto Ura, and it stays locked until the MC has crossed over once on their own.
- **It grows.** A new room appears whenever the family needs one. The garden is always in late spring.
- **Grown children** are free people: *mikogami*, "god-children." Each takes after their other parent's kind. All of them can see through the Kakushi, carry a little luck, and have a knack for bringing people together. Some stay to help with the nursery, the gates, or scouting for partners the MC hasn't met. Others go out into the two worlds.
- **A choice.** A partner can raise a child outside the house, at a normal pace. Some will.

**Knock-on effects:**
- The MC is building a clan. Within a few in-story years it will carry weight in both worlds.
- The mallet is the most stealable object in the setting.
- The house only lets in the people it chooses, so it's the one truly safe place.

## Two worlds

### Omote, the front
Present-day Japan, exactly as you know it: trains, konbini, salarymen, smartphones. The supernatural is real, but hidden behind the Kakushi. Old families remember. Yokai live among humans in human guise. Shrines are doors.

### Ura, the back
The *Kakuriyo* (幽世) is the hidden world Ōkuninushi has ruled since he handed the visible land to the heavenly gods. It's Japan's reflection: the same coasts and mountains, with everything else different. It isn't medieval fantasy. It's a **parallel modern Japan that never stopped believing**: lantern-lit cities that wake at dusk, steam trains pulled by burning-wheel yokai, onsen towns, night markets, kabuki houses. There's no phone signal, so gossip moves at the speed of crows. The moon looks bigger. Humans are rare there and get treated like interesting weather.

Places to start with:
- **Ura-Izumo**, the capital. Ōkuninushi's court, the hall where the gods meet every Kamiarizuki, and the shore where a sea serpent guides them in.
- **Ura-Edo**, the yokai metropolis that sits where Tokyo would be. Here, Edo never ended. Neon signs in old kana, alleys full of tanuki merchants, rooftops full of crows.
- **The Night Capital**, Kyoto's reflection, which is still Heian-kyō: old oni houses, the tengu of Kurama, and foxes running the markets under a thousand red gates.
- **Tōno-of-the-back**: rural and misty, with kappa in every river. The Mayoiga's valley opens off it.
- **Suehiro, "the Fan."** The foreign quarter at Ura-Nagasaki, built like old Dejima, where the otherworlds of other lands touch Japan's. *Suehiro* is a folding fan, and the word means "spreading wider toward the end." (See *Peoples*.)
- **Ryūgū-jō**, the Dragon Palace under the sea. Time slips there, and an evening inside can cost a week outside.
- **Ne-no-Kuni**, the Root Country beneath everything: old, dangerous, and Susanoo's.
- **The Moon**, above. White, silent, watching.

The far north and the far south have otherworlds of their own that don't answer to Izumo. They're neighbors, not provinces.

### Crossing
Walk under a torii at *ōmagatoki* (twilight, "the hour of meeting demons") with the other side in mind. Other ways through:
- sunshowers, which Japanese calls *kitsune no yomeiri*, "the fox's wedding"
- festival nights
- a few old wells, tunnels, and bridges
- the Mayoiga's back gate

Ordinary humans occasionally stumble through. That's what *kamikakushi*, "spirited away," always meant. Time runs the same on both sides, except in the places where it doesn't.

**How the MC discovers Ura:** the Tug points at a place that isn't there. They follow it at twilight.

### The Kakushi: why nobody notices
*Kakushi* is the gods' hiding. Ordinary eyes slide off hidden things. It isn't mind control, just inattention:
- A yokai's tail registers as a keychain.
- A week-long pregnancy reads as "she put on a little weight, then she didn't."
- A baby has gone to stay "with relatives."

The Kakushi thins at twilight, at festivals, under strong emotion, and around anyone with hidden blood. In practice, **nobody makes paperwork the plot.**

### Hidden blood
Japanese folklore has a whole genre for this: *irui kon'in-tan* (異類婚姻譚), tales of marriage with the non-human. Fox wives, crane wives, snake bridegrooms, mermaid brides, oni. They had children, and their children had children. A few families know: shrine lineages, old theater houses, fishing villages that won't eat a certain fish. Most don't. Hidden blood lets someone see a little way through the Kakushi and makes them likelier to fall through it. It also wakes under the blessing.

In a way, the whole setting is a modern *irui kon'in-tan*, told a hundred times at once.

## Peoples

- **Kami**: gods, from tiny local spirits to the great ones. Mostly in Ura, mostly busy, rarely seen.
- **Yokai**: everything else. Kitsune, tanuki, tengu, kappa, oni, nekomata, jorōgumo, yuki-onna, rokurokubi, tsukumogami, ningyo, wolves, crows, and a thousand oddities.
- **Shapes.** Most of them move along a spectrum:
  - a **human guise**, worn in Omote, where tells slip out under strong emotion (ears, tails, a flicker of horns)
  - a **half-shape**, with ears, tails, horns, or wings (kemonomimi territory)
  - a **true shape**: full anthro, full beast, or full monster

  In Ura, people relax into half-shape or true shape the way you'd loosen a tie. Fully furry anthros are an everyday sight.
- **Torai** (渡来, "the ones who came over"): monsters and fair folk from the otherworlds of other lands, who arrived along the western sea road over centuries and settled at the Fan. Lamias, harpies, centaurs, minotaurs, slimes, arachne, elves, dragons, succubi. They're generations deep now, with all the cultural friction and flirting you'd expect. Kitsune and succubi compete for the title of most dangerous seducer. The Western dragons and the ryūjin share a coastline and can't agree on whose sea it is.
- **Half-bloods** of every combination.
- **Gender.** In Ura, shapeshifters wear gender as lightly as a kimono, and the theater districts prize *onnagata*, performers of femininity, on and off the stage. The thread doesn't care about sex, but it has a type, and the type is femme. The MC's destined partners are women, femboys, and femme-leaning nonbinary people, of every kind.
- **Language.** Everybody speaks Japanese, so there's no translation homework.

## Inaba, the guide

The White Hare of Inaba, straight out of the *Kojiki*. This hare tricked a line of sharks into acting as a bridge, got flayed for bragging about it, and was healed by Ōkuninushi back when he was still a nobody. The hare repaid him by prophesying which princess he'd marry. Today Inaba is worshipped as a god of matchmaking: the first matchmaker in Japanese myth, and the reason the MC's life is now like this.

- **Looks:** slight, white-haired, red-eyed, with long hare ears that broadcast every mood they're trying to hide. A too-big haori patterned with waves, bare feet, and a faint smell of cattail pollen and salt.
- **Manner:** smug, quick, theatrical, and gluttonous (mochi, dango, sake, other people's senbei). Ancient, and acts like it about once a month. Guilty about all this, and far more invested in the MC's happiness than they'll admit.
- **What they do:**
  - see the threads (the MC can only feel them)
  - sense who's pining worst
  - know remedies, like fresh water and cattail pollen from the myth
  - know everyone in Ura worth knowing, and owe most of them money
- **The one rule:** Inaba lies about everything *except* threads.
- **Quirks:** won't go near the sea without a good reason, and won't go to an aquarium at all. Has cousins on the Moon, and doesn't visit them.
- **Gender:** femme-presenting and nonbinary. ("I'm a hare. Nobody asked hares.") Easy to swap for a femboy Inaba if you'd rather. **(Q1)**
- **Thread status is your call (Q1):**
  - **A · Outside fate.** Inaba isn't on the thread. In a story where every love is destined, Inaba is the one the MC could choose freely. Inaba is also the matchmaker who arranged a hundred loves for someone else.
  - **B · Secretly on it.** One of the hundred threads runs to Inaba. They had already met the MC at the shrine before the knots were tied, so the bell never rang for them, and that's how they hide it. As a god, they can hold out against the fray for a long time. They get quietly worse while sending the MC off to everyone else. The MC catches them murmuring Princess Shikishi's poem when they think the MC is asleep: *cord of my life, if you're going to break, then break; if I live on, I won't be able to hide this anymore.*

## Powers and people

- **Ōkuninushi**: lord of Ura and god of matchmaking, also worshipped as Daikoku, god of the kitchen and of plenty. He's famous for his many wives and, by one count, 181 children. He was killed twice and brought back twice. The MC delights him ("Only a hundred?"). He extends **Izumo's protection**, which makes open violence against the MC a crime in Ura. That's why threats arrive as gifts, schemes, and seductions instead.
- **Suseri-bime**: his chief wife and Susanoo's daughter. She's the most jealous goddess in the *Kojiki* and the only person in either world who has actually run a household like the MC's. On any given day she's either the MC's best advisor or their harshest critic.
- **Kamimusubi**: the primordial goddess and source of the musubi. She appears only in dreams: vast, maternal, impossible to read.
- **Konohanasakuya-hime**: goddess of blossoms, Mt. Fuji, and safe childbirth, and the warm patron of every birth in the household. She is Iwanaga-hime's sister (see *Partner seeds*).
- **Obaba**: the Mayoiga's yamauba.

## Pressure

### The Moon
The Moon Capital is home to Kaguya-hime's people: ageless, serene, and passionless. To them, desire, birth, and death are *kegare*, earthly impurity, and the musubi is an outbreak of it. They don't kill, because killing is unclean. They make people **forget**. Their weapon is the *hagoromo*, the feather robe that took away Kaguya's earthly feelings when she went home. Put one thread of it on a partner's sleeve and their love goes quiet, the knot loosens, and the fray begins. The MC has to make them remember. Envoys come down on moonlight and are strongest at the full moon.

### The coveting
This isn't a faction so much as the weather. Anyone who wants power wants the blessing:
- kitsune houses chasing tails
- tengu lords
- merchant tanuki
- a succubus from the Fan who takes the MC as a professional insult
- human families who'd love to have their bloodlines woken

Izumo's protection and the curdling rule mean they all come bearing gifts.

The Killing Stone at Nasu, the rock that held the nine-tailed fox Tamamo-no-Mae, split in two in March 2022. That really happened. In this setting, something walked out of it: the most famous seductress in Japanese legend, short a few tails and very interested in the MC.

### The severers
Shrines that cut bad ties (*en-kiri*) are as old as shrines that tie good ones. Their kami look at a hundred bonds forced by the gods and see something that ought to be cut. They aren't villains. They practice the other half of the same craft, they sell the only contraception that works on the MC, and their goddess is on the thread.

## Guardrails

Your avoid-list, turned into design rules, plus a couple of mine.

- **No ledgers.** No book of names, no partner count, no bureaucracy of fate, no stats. The hundred is deliberately fuzzy, and nobody lists the partners or the children.
- **Nothing glows.** No marks, crests, sigils, tattoos, or womb prints. Every cue is a sound, a touch, a smell, a dream, or a run of luck. Blessings show up as natural changes: a tail, a voice, a harvest.
- **Built for RP.** Every rule has a sensory cue and a simple trigger. Time is told in phases, not counters. A handful of characters are on stage at once, and the rest are offstage, alive and busy.
- **Not a parable about Japan's birth rate.** Nobody in-world frames any of this as demographics, the nation, or "saving" anything. The gods did it because of a pun, and the stakes are personal and mythic.
- **Adults only.** Every partner is an adult, and small-bodied races are written as adults. Children are family and never sexualized, and the thread never ties blood to blood.
- **Freely given.** Force curdles the musubi. That's part of how the world works, not a rule bolted on afterward.
